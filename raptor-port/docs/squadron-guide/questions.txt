TALON: questions for F-16 schedulers and training appointment holders

Read the guide first. Short answers are fine. Reply with the question number.
Where a question names S or C codes, they are the row codes in the guide's tables.

Q1. Which numbers in section 5 are different for you, and what is your value?
Context: These are the times the checks measure with: crew rest, brief, step, dekit, debrief, tight turn, days in a row. Reply by code, for example "S6: 45 min". If a number changes by the kind of sortie or the time of day, say so.

Q2. Which checks in section 6 do not apply to you, or work differently?
Context: Reply by code, for example "C22: does not apply" or "C12: for us this is red, not amber". A check that fires when it should not is as bad as one that is missing.

Q3. Which rules or directives do you look up by hand when you plan, that the app should flag for you?
Context: This is the main one. One line a rule: who it applies to, the limit, and whether breaking it should be red (a rule broken) or amber (worth a look). The aim is that you no longer open the document: the app flags it.

Q4. Which of your jets are single-seat and which are two-seat, and who may sit in the back seat of a two-seater?
Context: Every line today has a front seat and a back seat, and several checks are about the back-seater. Single-seat lines are planned but not built yet, and their rules are not written.

Q5. What decides who may fly with whom, or lead whom, in your squadron?
Context: Today the app uses the F-15 squadron's categories (OCU, CAT A to D) and instructor qualifications to flag a pairing that is not allowed or needs approval.

Q6. Which qualifications or currencies should stop or flag a person for a sortie or a duty?
Context: Today the warnings read four: SC DAY, SC NIGHT, day refuelling and night refuelling. The SANS tick and the Scheduler tick decide who files SANS availability and who may sign a day. Each squadron can keep its own list of qualifications.

Q7. How do your SC MAIN, SC SPARE, AVALON and BB differ from section 7: their hours, who may hold them, and what else a person may do while on one?
Context: Today an SC MAIN is checked in full. SC SPARE, AVALON and BB are checked more lightly than a flying line unless they fly. Say also if you hold any other standby or duty post the app should know.

Q8. How does a flying day run for you, in order, and which of those times do you print on the programme?
Context: Today a wave's typed times run in-time, rally, brief, take-off, landing, and the app flags any typed out of that order. Step, dekit and debrief are not typed: the app works them out from take-off and landing (S6 to S8). It measures crew rest and the working day from all of these.

Q9. For training: how do you track a student's or an upgrade's progress today, and what should a scheduler see about it while planning?
Context: Today the Tracker holds each student's syllabus chart and marks, and the schedule flags crew pairings by category. Tell us what a training appointment holder checks by hand before a sortie is planned.

Q10. What does your printed programme show that this one does not?
Context: Anything: a column, a mark, a note at the foot, a colour that means something.

Q11. Can you share one real week of your programme, in any format?
Context: Seeing a real week answers most of the above faster than any list of questions.
