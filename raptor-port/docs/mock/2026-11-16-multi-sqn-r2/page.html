A · Detachments

A1 · How a man is put on a detachment

Example: 142 sends Anvil and five others to Exercise North, 13 to 31 Jul.

Your question was whether this goes through Inputs, or through a scheduler or admin. Today the app already records this as the overseas duty input: the man files it himself, or a scheduler files one for several men. It already takes him out of reach at home and shows on the Leave War.

Recommended: keep that one input and add one choice to it, Detachment (mark 1a). Its Title fills in with the detachment's name by itself (1b). A man with that input is on the detachment's crew for those dates. The detachment's own page lists who and when, and its admins can add a man there, which files the same input for him. It takes effect at once, as every input does, and both squadrons' schedulers see it.

Other choices: (B) the admins keep a dated list on the detachment instead, and men never file it; (C) through the input, but it waits for an admin's yes before he moves.

A2 · How he shows at home while away

Recommended (A): the overseas duty row he gets today, named by the detachment, "Exercise North", with "OD" small under it. The app draws this itself already when an input has a title of its own (mark 2, nothing drawn in). Nothing new to learn, and the Leave War already counts him away.

Other choice (B): a new kind, "DET", as round 1 drew it. One more kind beside LL, OIL and OD.

On a phone

A3 · Closing a detachment

You said an admin could close it once it is over, or archive it to see the old programme. Recommended: one button, Close detachment (mark 3c in C1's picture below). It always keeps the detachment's schedules, read only, under "Past detachments" (3d), because a published schedule is never erased. Its people come back to their squadron from that day. Other choice: two steps, Close, then Archive.

B · Two squadrons flying at home

B1 · The mark on the other squadron's line

Your real example: 142's two lines fly in 149's wave and show on 149's schedule, today typed twice by hand with "142 LINE" in the remarks.

Redrawn, 17 Nov. You found the first drawing ugly: the "142" took the callsign's big letters and, on a phone, fell onto a line of its own. All three new looks use the app's own small badge, the one MAIN and SPARE wear on standby lines. It is grey here, because its teal means MAIN. The badge sits after the mission. On a phone it goes under the mission, one size smaller, as the Flies chip does. Here Monday's RU stands in for 142's formation in 149's wave. The line keeps every box and seat, and either scheduler may plan it.

Recommended: look 2 (5d). The badge says whose line it is, and the whole formation sits one shade lighter, both jets and its area line, so you see the block is 142's at a glance without reading. Other choices: look 1 (5c), the badge alone, the quietest; look 3 (5e), the badge and a thin grey line down the formation's left edge.

Two of your example's points need nothing: the app numbers no lines, so there is no line number to leave out; and the count at the top keeps the squadrons apart, as you ruled (picture D2 below).

B2 · Which of the other squadron's lines show on mine

On your example, 142's schedule showed its own lines and also the one 149 line its men fly against, marked "149 LINE", but not all of 149's lines. Recommended: a schedule shows its own lines wherever they are planned, plus any line of a shared wave that a scheduler ticks to show there. A shown line wears the same mark as in B1 ("149"), and is read only there. Other choices: show the whole shared wave; or only my own lines.

B3 · Who publishes and signs a wave both squadrons fly in

Recommended: the squadron whose wave it is. On your example, 149's four sign 149's day, 142's lines in its waves included. 142's schedule shows those lines as issued by 149, and 142's four sign 142's own day. Where 142 changes its line in 149's wave, it reads pending on 149's day until 149 publishes it. Other choice: each squadron publishes its own lines, wherever they fly.

B4 · A pick onto a day the home squadron has already published

Example: 142's Monday is published. 149 then picks Comet for 08:00–12:00.

Recommended: his "Attach · 149" row arrives on 142's Monday as a pending change, as a late input does today: dotted under its name, the day reading "1 pending", and the four sign-offs down until 142 publishes it. The picture is the app's own drawing of an Attach filed after publishing.

On a phone

B5 · Which checks read across both squadrons at home

You have ruled that a man's clashes are checked across both squadrons at the same place. Recommended: the checks follow the man: crew rest, two things at once, days in a row, leave and medical read everything he is on, on both schedules, and both schedulers see the warning. Areas and sims are shared at home, so a clash there is checked across both too. Jets stay each squadron's own count, unless your squadrons share jets: tell me if they do.

B6 · Whose Logic settings judge a borrowed man's line

Each squadron sets its own Logic page (brief time, crew rest and so on). Recommended: a line is judged by the settings of the schedule it sits in. Comet flying in 149's wave is judged by 149's settings for that flight; his own day at 142 by 142's. Other choice: always his own squadron's settings.

C · Admin and signing up

C1 · The Admin page for squadrons and detachments

Recommended, as drawn: a new area, Squadrons (3a). It lists each squadron with its place (3b): only a master admin adds a squadron. Under it, a squadron's open detachments with their place, dates and people, "Close detachment" (3c) and "+ New detachment"; then past detachments, kept read only (3d). The rows are cloned from the Users list; the spacing at their left is tighter than it would be when built.
