Two squadrons: round 7

Your round 6 answers drawn on the real app, plus what two new reviews found. To answer, type the number and your choice, e.g. "Q8 yes, Q9 B". Items R12–R35 I will design as written unless you say no to one.

Blue outline on a picture: what you already decided. New pink outline: new this round, from the reviews.

The example used all through this page

149 and 142 are the two squadrons. 149 sends six people (Hawk, Spur, Talc, Vane, Crisp and Moss) to Exercise North, 13–31 Jul. Exercise North has its own schedule, numbered 249, and is 7 hours ahead of home. Ace is one of 149's admins. All names, dates and figures are made up.

Words used on this page

  • DET: the new input a man files to go on a detachment (your idea).
  • Published day: a day's schedule that has gone out. Amendment: a new version of it published later (AL1, AL2…).
  • Marked as changed (the app's word on screen is "pending"): a published day with changes not yet published. It shows that way until someone publishes it again.
  • Badge: the small grey "149" or "142" beside something that belongs to the other squadron.

First: four things I recommended in round 6 (and you agreed) turn out wrong in one case

  1. 1"Every date goes by home's clock." If the exercise is ahead of home and Ace closes it late in the evening, it is already the next morning there. Closing it would wipe the day they are flying at that moment. Fixed by Q8.
  2. 2"Closing the detachment clears everything on the remaining days." Some rows come from a person's input, such as Hawk's medical downchit. Removing that row cancels the downchit itself, everywhere. Back home he could then be put on a flight with no medical warning. Fixed by R13.
  3. 3"Another unit's flight on those days: just tell that unit." Closing the detachment would delete the flight. The other unit is still at the exercise flying it, so its crew lose that day's flying and OIL. Fixed by R12.
  4. 4"Their DET ends on the day you close." If you close after the planned end date, that makes their DET longer, and the card gives the wrong "back from" date. Fixed by R14.

Questions for you (one decision each)

  1. Q8Closing late at night while the exercise is hours ahead. Recommended: each place knows its hours from home. Closing a detachment never wipes a day that has already started there, and crew rest between two places counts the time difference.
  2. Q9One man works one day for both squadrons, and the two published days use different hours for a full day of OIL. Recommended A: whichever day was published first decides, for that date.
  3. Q10A transfer that would leave 142 with no admin. Recommended: the transfer sheet refuses it straight away: "make another admin first".
  4. Q11Two flights booking the same airspace or sim at the same time. The app doesn't check this today, not even within one squadron. Recommended A: make it a separate job to build.
  5. Q12Two squadrons' courses sharing one syllabus chart: deleting an event wipes the other squadron's students' marks. Recommended: only someone in charge of every course on that chart may delete.
  6. Q13Who signs off a detachment's day when nobody there has the Scheduler tick. Recommended: anyone qualified from that squadron, with the people at the detachment listed first.
  7. Q14A DET button on the Leave War? Recommended: yes. The buttons read CSE · OD · DET while a detachment is open on that day.
  8. Q15Should the Leave War's counting rows count people away on DET? Recommended: a switch on each row: on for people rows, off for team rows.
  9. Q16Insights for someone on DET. Recommended: he shows as "249", never as "not flying", plus one figure for his total weekly hours across both schedules.
  10. Q17Posting a man out while he is on DET. Recommended: allowed as today, but the sheet first lists what it will end.
  11. Q18A rule that blocks a transfer when he is on DET to a 142 detachment. Recommended: drop the rule.
  12. Q19A DET column in the Leave War's days-taken table? Recommended: yes.
  13. Q20May a man shorten his own DET when he is already planned on a day there? Recommended: no. He may file it and make it longer; a scheduler shortens it.
  14. Q21A man who joins the detachment after its day is published. Recommended: the detachment's published day stays as it is. His home days are marked as changed.
  15. Q22A SANS man on DET. Recommended: as today, he marks the days he is available, the detachment's days included. The DET window reminds him.
  16. Q23Transferring a SANS man who isn't on the Leave War. Recommended: the same Post out sheet, which can also be opened from Admin → Users for someone not on the Leave War.

New from the reviews: I design these as written unless you say no

Closing a detachment

Ace presses "Close detachment" for Exercise North on Thu 23 Jul, before its planned end on 31 Jul.

The "Close detachment" card

One card asks Ace to confirm and lists what will happen. Each line appears only when it applies. In one press, closing the detachment:

  • ends the six people's DET, so they are back on 149's schedule;
  • clears the remaining days that were already published, and publishes an amendment on each;
  • drops the remaining days that were never published.

The two pink lines are the ones the reviews changed: the first line (their DET end date, R14) and the line about 242's flight (R12).

If Ace presses it late at night, when it is already the next day at Exercise North, the card says that day is kept. This only applies if you agree to Q8.

New R12 · Another unit's flight on a day that closing clears

Example. On Sat 25 Jul, Exercise North's published schedule holds a flight belonging to another unit, 242: their RU, crewed by Kite and Lark, flying in 249's wave. Closing the detachment clears every flight on the remaining days, so it would delete 242's flight, even though 242 is still there and flying. Kite and Lark would lose Saturday's flying and its OIL.

Recommended: closing doesn't delete it. It moves 242's flight into a wave of 242's own, at the same times. 242's day is marked as changed, for 242's own scheduler to publish. The card has a line saying so (the pink line about 242 in the picture above).

Other choice: the "Close detachment" button can't be pressed while another unit's flight is still on a later day. The card names each flight, so someone can move them first.

New R13 · Closing never removes a person's input

Example. Hawk's medical downchit covers Sat 25 Jul and shows as a row on Exercise North's day. If closing removed that row, the app would cancel the downchit itself, on every schedule. Back at home from Fri 24, he could then be put on a flight that Saturday with no medical warning.

Recommended: closing clears only flights, waves, duty desks and ground-programme rows someone put there by hand. A row that comes from a person's input (a downchit, leave and so on) stays and follows him home.

New R14 · Closing only ever shortens a DET

Example. Exercise North was planned for 13–31 Jul. The bell reminds Ace on the last day, but he only closes it on Mon 3 Aug. If closing set everyone's DET to end on the closing day, all six would be on DET until 3 Aug, and the card would say "back from Tue 4 Aug". That's wrong: they were home from 1 Aug.

Recommended: each DET ends on whichever comes first: its planned end or the closing day. The card's first line appears only for people whose DET it shortens, showing the old date ("was 31 Jul").

Also: if an admin moves the detachment's end date earlier (or its start date later), the app asks first, just as it asks "Extend…?" when you move the dates out (round 6). It refuses while a published detachment day would fall outside the new dates.

New R15 · No Unpublish after closing

Example. Closing the detachment has cleared Sat 25 Jul and published an amendment. An admin then presses Unpublish on that amendment. Today, Unpublish brings back the version before it: the cleared flights and their OIL come back, though the detachment is closed.

Recommended: on an amendment that closing published, the Unpublish button can't be pressed, and it says why. To correct the day, publish a new amendment. (You already agreed that Undo can't take back closing a detachment.)

The picture also shows one line where the four sign-off names would be, and Hawk's downchit row still there (R13).

The sign-off boxes stay empty, ready for a later correcting amendment. I drew the Unpublish button plainly dimmed. The app's usual look for a button that can't be pressed is a dim blue, and here that looked more lit up, not less.

Going on a detachment: the DET input

What you decided: a man goes on a detachment by filing a DET input that names it. At home he shows as away. On the Leave War he still counts as available manpower. OD stays for staff trips.

The DET window (round 3's picture redrawn)

You can pick any open detachment of either squadron, only within its dates. It doesn't ask the OIL question.

New R16 · Whole days only, and fewer options than OD. If DET simply copied OD's window, it would start at 06:00–18:00, so the six would look free at home after 18:00. It could also be sent to the Ground Programme, cancelled, marked "information only", or changed into an OD. Each of those would leave a man in two places at once, or nowhere. Recommended: no times (whole days only) and none of those options. Once saved, a DET can't be changed into another kind of input. It never counts toward crew rest and never asks the OIL question.

New R17 · Before saving, list his duties at home on those dates. Hawk is on 149's published Sat 18 Jul SDO desk. Filing a DET from 13 Jul would leave Saturday's published schedule still showing him on the desk, and still giving him its OIL, until someone publishes it again. Recommended: before Save, the window lists those duties, each set to "Take him off", which can be switched to "Keep". Other choice: no list. Rely on the red warning that shows afterwards (decided in round 6).

At home: one row

On 149's schedule, the six show as one row under Unavailable, titled "Exercise North" with "DET" small under it. They can't be planned at home.

New R18 · One DET for each man

Example. "+ Put people on it" files a DET for all six together. If that is one record, then when Spur goes home sick on 20 Jul and his end date is changed, all six DETs end on 19 Jul.

Recommended: it files a separate DET for each man, from the same window, grouped together on the People list. Changing one changes only that man. A man who already has his own DET on those dates is skipped and named. This changes your earlier answer that it "files one DET input".

Other choice: one record holding a separate end date for each man. That would change how every input for several people works.

On the Leave War (you decided: a mark of its own, counted as available)

New R19 · DET's own words. If DET copied OD here too, a man on DET who is still on a home duty would read "Overseas + planned", and the legend would say "out of reach, cannot be planned for anything". Recommended: DET has its own legend line, colour and filter, and reads "On detachment + planned".

New R20 · The day panel shows "On DET · 6", so whoever approves leave sees that six of the people counted as Available are actually away. The buttons are Q14. Whether the counting rows count them is Q15.

New R21 · Leave for someone on DET. Three of the six ask for leave on Wed 22 Jul. Available shows green, because people on DET count as available, so the leave is approved, and 249 can no longer fly its programme. Recommended: on a DET day, his leave window and the approver's line say "On DET · 249: 6 there, 5 with this." Other choice: later, a required number of people set for the detachment itself.

New R22 · The bell for a DET

Example. Saber files a DET for himself; no scheduler picked him. He leaves home's crew list and joins 249's manpower, and neither scheduler is told. Today the bell only tells the people on the input. A changed end date just says "dates changed".

Recommended: filing, changing or deleting a DET tells the schedulers of both the detachment and his own squadron, one line each, saying he is joining, moving or back home, with the dates. Whether he may shorten it himself is Q20.

New R23 · Moving a DET to another detachment changes the published days

Example. Hawk's DET is moved from 249 to another detachment, over a published Tuesday. Today the app only notices that kind of move for Attach inputs. His home day keeps showing the old detachment's name with its sign-offs, and 249's published day is never told he left.

Recommended: the app notices which detachment a DET names. Every published day, at home or at a detachment, that now looks different is marked as changed. A man added is Q21. Also: Undo, Redo and loading an old saved plan will first list what they would break, as other actions already do.

Planning each other's people

149 picks Saber (a 142 man) for ACADEMICS on Tue 14 Jul. At home, on 142's schedule, he shows a row "ACADEMICS · 149" with "ATTACH" small under it (you decided).

149's row on 142's published day

The row appears once 149 publishes, with 149's grey badge. It never marks 142's day as changed. A change 149 hasn't published yet shows faintly beside it.

New R24 · The clash warning also looks at 149's published day. 142 takes Saber off ACADEMICS on 149's published Tuesday. That only changes 149's working copy; 149's published day still has him. If the clash warning only looked at the working copy, 142 could put Saber on its own 14:30 flight with no warning. And if 149 never publishes again, two published schedules have Saber at the same time, and OIL counts both. Recommended: the warning names 149's published flight until 149 publishes again, and the line under "Take him off" says so.

New R25 · 142's own team row still shows changes. The "never marked as changed" rule covers only 149's row. If 142's own published ALL AVAIL group changes, 142's day is still marked as changed, as it is today.

New R26 · A night flight past midnight: one row on each day, for its part of the flight. Taking him off removes him from the whole flight.

Saber is told, and can see the flight

New R27 · He also sees the wave's reporting notes. He sees 149's flight but can't change it. Without the report time and brief room he can't turn up. Recommended: his view includes the wave's reporting notes. It opens from the bell and from his row.

Two squadrons changing one flight

You decided there is no lock. Both squadrons may change a shared flight; an "editing" mark shows to both; the later save stands; the person whose change was replaced is told; and Publish warns first. The pictures: 142 changed its RU flight in 149's wave, and 149's schedulers are told.

The app has no "someone is editing" mark today, and no card before publishing ("Publish day" publishes at once). So both are drawn new, from the app's own parts: the mark in the app's "changed" look, the card in the look of its usual question card.

New R28 · One bell line a day for each squadron. Otherwise a week of shared flying would ring 142's scheduler for every pick, publish and change. Recommended: one line a day for each squadron, updated as things change.

New R29 · The printed day names its own squadron

Example. Today, 149's (or 249's) published day, printed, reads "142 Flying Programme" in its title and heading.

Recommended: the print's title, heading and file name show the schedule's own number and name. Exported rows show which squadron or detachment each flight belongs to. 142's print shows 149's published rows with 149's badge.

Access and transfer

Access to the other squadron: "View" only (you decided)

On Admin → Users there is one choice: "149 · View". The row's name, "Other squadrons", is my wording.

The transfer sheet (you decided: fixed rules, lines starting "As of today:")

The sheet has no choices; its lines start "As of today:" (your wording). He joins 149 as a member. Both squadrons' admins are told when the transfer is set and again on the day. On the day, the bell reads "Ranger · Fri 3 Apr · Posted out · now with 149".

New R30 · His appointment ticks end too. You decided a transfer ends his admin rights and his Scheduler tick in 142. But his MANAGEMENT and SXO ticks would come with him and put him in 149's Management group. Recommended: on the day, his Scheduler, Management and SXO ticks are cleared, the change history records it, and 149's admins are told.

New R31 · Deleting, archiving or posting out a man the other squadron has planned

Example. 149 has Viper (a 142 man) on its published flights 1–5 Dec. 142's admin deletes him. His seats on 149's days go empty, 149's days are marked as changed with nothing saying why, and only admins are told.

Recommended: Post out (every option), Archive and Delete first list the other squadron's flights and published days after the date, before saving. They tell that squadron's schedulers when it is set and again when it happens.

SANS people across the two squadrons (you decided)

Borrowing: a SANS man marks his available days once, in his own squadron, as today. Those days show on both squadrons' SANS panels; on the other squadron's, with his squadron's grey badge. Either squadron may pick him. A pick shows on his home schedule as an automatic row and tells his squadron's schedulers. Marking days available never earns OIL.

Transfer: a SANS man moves by "Transfer to Sqn" like anyone else. He arrives still SANS, with his available days. The "SANS" choice is not offered for him, since he already is SANS. How the admin reaches the sheet is Q23.

Places and holidays

Round 4's place card now says "From tomorrow"

You decided a new place counts from the start of the next day. So round 4's card, "Once saved, 142 and 149 can plan each other's people", was untrue until midnight; it now reads "From tomorrow". The same picture shows a place's hours from home (Q8), drawn as recommended.

New R32 · The shared public-holiday list

Example. 149's admin adds Fri 7 Aug to the public-holiday list both squadrons share. 142's published Fri 7 Aug is now out of date. Today, the admin who adds it is also asked the OIL question for everyone, 142's men included.

Recommended: before saving, the app names each squadron's published days that go out of date and tells both squadrons' admins. The admin who adds the holiday answers the OIL question only for his own squadron; the other squadron's schedulers answer for theirs. This changes the earlier rule that whoever adds a holiday answers for everyone.

Questions

New Q8 · Closing late at night while the exercise is hours ahead

Example. Exercise North is 7 hours ahead. Ace presses "Close detachment" at 23:00 Thu 23 Jul at home, which is already 06:00 Fri 24 there. Going by home's date (your round 6 answer), Fri 24 counts as "after today". So closing wipes the day 249 is flying right then, publishes an amendment and takes back its OIL.

The same gap breaks crew rest. Hawk's travel day ends at midnight Sunday there, which is 07:00 Monday at home. 149 plans him for Monday 14:00 at home. That is 7 hours of real rest, but the app counts 14 and doesn't warn.

Recommended: each place has its hours from home, set on Admin → Squadrons (0 unless someone sets it). Closing keeps any detachment day that has already started there, and the card says so. Crew rest between two places adds the time difference. Postings and the late mark still go by home's date. This changes your round 6 answer that every date goes by home's date.

Other choice: everything stays on home's date. The "Close detachment" button can't be pressed while it would wipe a day already started there, and says when it can be. Crew rest across two places isn't added up, and his first flight at home after a DET says so.

New Q9 · One day worked for both squadrons: which "full day" counts for OIL

Example. Saber (142) works Sat 28 Nov for both squadrons. You decided his hours that day are added together into one OIL credit, judged by 142's setting for how many hours make a full day. A published day keeps the settings it went out with. 149's published day kept 6 hours. Then 142 changes the setting to 8 hours and publishes its afternoon, which keeps 8 hours. Saber worked 7 hours: a full day by 6 hours, only half a day by 8 hours.

Recommended A: whichever published day counts him first fixes the value for that date. A later change needs his home day published again, which shows the old and new OIL. Nothing changes underneath a published day. Report and debrief times still follow each flight's own schedule.

Other choice B: the later of the two values decides. A line on the publish card says so.

New Q10 · A transfer that would leave 142 with no admin

Example. Ranger's transfer to 149 is set for Fri 3 Apr. On that date he would be 142's last admin. Two of your round 6 answers disagree here. One makes him 149's man from 3 Apr. The other makes the transfer wait until 142 has another admin. He would end up counted as 149's but still 142's admin, with no clear OIL settings and no way to cancel the transfer after the date.

Recommended: the sheet refuses it straight away, and so does any later step that would cause it, such as removing another 142 admin: "Ranger is 142's last admin. Make another admin of 142 first." So a transfer never has to wait for this.

Other choice: the whole transfer waits. He stays fully 142's, "Transfer to 149 waiting" shows, and it can be cancelled until it goes through.

New Q11 · Two flights booking the same airspace or sim

Example. 142's RU and 149's VL both book NORTH, 09:00–10:00. Nothing warns. Today the app checks no airspace or sim double booking, not even within one squadron, and airspace is typed as free text. In round 2 you agreed that people, airspace and sims would be checked across both squadrons, a promise the app can't keep yet.

Recommended A: make "airspace and sim clashes" a job of its own: one list of airspaces (with which ones overlap) and sims for each place, picked from the list instead of typed.

Other choice B: across squadrons, check people only, and drop the airspace and sim promise.

New Q12 · Two squadrons sharing one syllabus chart

Example. A 142 course and a 149 course use the same syllabus chart (you allowed sharing). A 142 member deletes an event's ball and saves. Every student's marks on that event are gone, 149's students included. Giving each course its own squadron doesn't stop this.

Recommended: only someone with rights over every course using that chart may delete a ball or its marks. Anyone else is refused, and the other squadron's courses are named.

Other choice: anyone may, with a warning before Save naming 149's courses.

New Q13 · Who signs off a detachment's day

Example. None of the six at Exercise North has the Scheduler tick. Three of the four sign-off boxes only accept someone with that tick, so 249 can never publish, and its weekends never earn OIL.

Recommended: the detachment's sign-off boxes offer anyone qualified from its own squadron, wherever they are, with the people at the detachment listed first. Never the other squadron's people.

Other choice: only people on its DET that day. "+ New detachment" warns while none of them has the tick.

New Q14 · A DET button on the Leave War

Example. A member files his detachment with the Leave War's OD button, as he always has. He is counted out at home, and 249 can't plan him. Nothing says why.

Recommended: the buttons read CSE · OD · DET whenever his squadron has a detachment open on that day (drawn in the Leave War pictures above).

Other choice: no DET button. An OD filed during an open detachment says once: "Going on 249? File DET."

New Q15 · Should counting rows count people on DET?

Example. The six count as 149's manpower, so they also fill 149's standby-team row. The row shows green, so the last home crew's leave is approved.

Recommended: each counting row gets a switch, "Count people on DET": on for people rows, off for team rows. It is a new setting; today a row's settings only know seats, CATs and quals.

Other choice: rows count as today, and the idea that a row can leave DET out is dropped.

New Q16 · Insights for someone on DET

Example. In the week of 13 Jul, Hawk flies 149's Monday, then nine sorties at 249. 149's Insights shows him as a low flyer, and the five who left on Sunday as "not flying". No screen shows his whole week.

Recommended: each schedule's figures stay its own, with a note beside them saying what they cover. A man on DET shows as "249", never "not flying". Separately, one figure for his total weekly hours across all schedules.

Other choice: each schedule's own hours only, labelled plainly, and no total figure.

New Q17 · Posting a man out while he is on DET

Example. Talc is at 249 until 31 Jul and is posted out on 20 Jul. Today his DET ends the day before, his seats at 249 go empty from that date, and 249's published days are marked as changed.

Recommended: it works as today, but the sheet first lists what it will end: "On DET · 249 until 31 Jul: his DET ends 19 Jul; 249's published days with him are marked as changed" (the same idea as R31). A posting is a fact; refusing it wouldn't stop him leaving.

Other choice: every posting option is refused during a DET, and waits as a held posting does today.

New Q18 · A rule that blocks a transfer during a DET

Example. A round 6 answer blocks a transfer to 149 while he has a DET to a 142 detachment across the date. But you also allowed a DET to name either squadron's detachment. So after the date he would simply be a 149 man on a 142 detachment, which is allowed anyway.

Recommended: drop that block, so a transfer has one fewer reason to wait (see also Q10).

Other choice: keep it.

New Q19 · A DET column in days taken

Example. The Leave War's days-taken table has an OD column. A man's 19 days at 249 would show nowhere in it.

Recommended: a DET column beside OD.

Other choice: no column, since DET is not time away from the squadron's manpower.

New Q20 · May a man shorten his own DET?

Example. Spur filed his own DET to 31 Jul. 249 has planned him on its published Sat 25 Jul. He changes his end date to 20 Jul.

Recommended: he may file his own DET and make it longer, but not shorten or delete it over a day he is planned on there. The window says so, and a scheduler does it instead.

Other choice: he may change it freely. The detachment's day is marked as changed and its schedulers are told (R22).

New Q21 · A man who joins the detachment after its day is published

Example. Gale files a DET to 249 from Mon 20 Jul. 249's published Mon 20 doesn't have him on it.

Recommended: 249's day doesn't change. Only removing someone who is on it marks it as changed. At home his days are always marked as changed, since he becomes away (R23).

Other choice: 249's day is marked as changed too, since its manpower changed.

New Q22 · A SANS man on DET

Example. Crisp is a SANS man, so he can only be planned on days he has marked as available. On DET he can't be put on any flight at 249 until he marks his available days there, one by one.

Recommended: as today, he marks his available days for the detachment's dates too. The DET window reminds him.

Other choices: a DET makes him available there automatically; or SANS people are not offered in the DET window at all.

New Q23 · Transferring a SANS man who isn't on the Leave War (your question)

Example. Tally, a SANS man of 142, moves to 149 from Fri 3 Apr. Post out lives on the Leave War, and SANS people only show there if an admin has added the SANS group ("+ SANS"), which you usually don't.

Recommended: the same Post out sheet, with the same rules and checks, can also be opened from his row on Admin → Users. That button shows only for someone who isn't on the Leave War, so each man still has exactly one way in.

Other choice: keep one way in. The admin adds the SANS group to the Leave War to find him.

Checked today: once the SANS group is added, Post out opens on a SANS man's row as for anyone. But it still offers him the "SANS" choice, though he already is SANS. Your answer in round 7 takes that away; I have filed the fix.
