Your seven colours, measured on this day

“Difference” is how far apart two colours look: under about 10 people mix them up at a glance, 20 and above is clearly different. I mark under 12 against a schedule colour and under 15 between two amendments.

Does it read clearly?

Mostly yes, and better than I expected. Your “latest only” pick is what saves it: on the whole day only AL6’s changes carry a solid line and AL7’s a dotted one, so what is newest can be found at a glance even with about ninety marks on the screen. The older five are colour only and stay quiet.

Where it is busy: the Common Programme, where almost every row is a different colour, and the flying waves, where the amendment colours sit among the cyan chips, the amber in-time lines and the red warning pucks. It reads as “a lot has changed here”, which is true. Which older amendment a colour means cannot be read off the row any more; that is the job of the “Changed in” line under the signed line.

The outlines hold up. A red box with a dotted line inside reads as “emptied, waiting” in every place I staged, and the grey draft outlines are plain on Tuesday. On a flying line the take-off and landing are stacked with no room between, so there the outline steps out by only 1px; it does not touch the landing time under it.

No line touches anything: on this day the tightest gap under a line is 2.3px (a flight remark over its stores chips), the same as on the simpler day.

Points for you: four of your colours sit near a schedule colour

These are your picks and I have not changed them. Each is a point with a fix you can take or leave.

1. AL2 orange and Personal Inputs (4 apart: the same colour). A request changed in AL2 is orange words under an orange heading and stripe. Once AL2 is not the newest it has no line, so on that section colour is all it has. It is also 26 from the missing-time red, enough for most eyes and close for a red-green reader (13); there the shapes differ. Fix: clay for AL2 (12 from Personal Inputs), in the swatch list.

2. AL5 green and Duties (9 apart). A duty time changed in AL5 is green beside the green block labels (see 22:00 on the second OPS-O desk). The labels are capitals and the times are numbers, which helps, but it is the pair you named first. AL5 green and AL3 mint are also only 17 from each other. Fix: sage for AL5 (15 from Duties), or keep green and move it to AL7, which few days reach.

3. AL3 mint and Available crew (7 apart). Low risk in practice: that section holds pucks, not words, so a mint word never sits in it. Against Duties’ green it is 14. Fix: none needed; ice if you want more distance.

4. AL6 azure and the flying waves’ cyan (13 apart). A changed area time is azure beside the cyan AREA label and the cyan stores chips (see 1955-2105 in the night wave). While AL6 is the newest its line carries it; once it is older, it is blue on blue. Fix: sky is no better here; lilac or clay are the only free tints far from cyan, and lilac is your AL4. Leaving it is reasonable, since a day rarely reaches AL6.

AL7 pink inside the red outline is 21 apart for ordinary sight: two colours, and one is a closed box while the other is a dotted line. No fix needed.

Could one of your swatches be a different colour from the one I drew? Your picture was read as lemon, orange, mint, lilac, green, azure and today’s pink. Two could be a neighbour in the list: the pink may be the paler proposed pink, and the orange may be today’s darker orange. If either looks off in the try-out, tap that swatch.

Recommendation
Keep your look, underline and puck picks as they are. Of the colours, change only AL2 (orange to clay) and AL5 (green to sage), the two that land on a section people read words in. If you would rather keep all seven, the day still works: the newest change is always clear, and the two look-alikes only matter once those amendments are no longer the newest.
