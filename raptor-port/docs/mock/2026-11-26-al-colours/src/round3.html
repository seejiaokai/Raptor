With only the latest underlined, what does a changed puck wear?

You asked what happens to the number chips under your pick. A puck cannot go “colour only” as words do: its own colour says pilot, WSO or ground crew. So its corner mark has to do the job. Try the three under “A changed puck wears” in the try-out.

When it happens
Whenever an amendment swaps a person, on a day that has more than one amendment. A swap is the most common amendment there is.
Example
Monday’s sims: one crew seat was changed in AL1, another in AL3 (the newest that is out), and a third is waiting for AL4.
Choices and their costs

A. Every amendment keeps its number: “1”, “3”, and a hollow “4” while waiting. Cost: pucks do not follow your pick; an old change is marked as loudly as the newest, and every changed puck carries a tiny number that covers the corner of its CAT box.

B. The newest amendment out and anything waiting keep the number; older ones keep a small plain tab in their colour. Cost: two sizes of mark on one day; an older puck’s amendment is told by its tab’s colour and the day’s “Changed in” line, not by a number.

C. Older amendments’ pucks lose the mark entirely. Cost: a puck changed in AL1 looks exactly like one that never changed. Changed words at least keep their colour; a puck would keep nothing, so the change people most need to see would be the only one that disappears.

Recommendation
B. It is your pick carried onto pucks: the number does for a puck what the line does for words, marking only what is new, while the plain tab does what colour does for older words, saying “this changed, earlier”. It keeps your rule that a changed puck wears a tag, filled once out and hollow while waiting, and never a ring.
What it changes of your earlier decisions
A changed puck is still marked by its tag alone. Today the tag says “AL4”; here it says “4” on the newest and nothing on older ones. This replaces my earlier suggestion (a plain tab on every changed puck), which gave pucks no way to show which change is newest.

Underline only the latest change (answered)

You picked “Latest out + waiting”: the newest amendment that is out keeps its solid line, anything waiting keeps its dotted line, and older amendments keep their colour only. The try-out now starts that way and every picture of my recommendation on this page is drawn that way. The other two choices are still in the try-out’s “Underline” setting to compare. “Newest” is worked out day by day, since each day has its own AL numbering.

What your pick gives
The newest change stands out and the older ones go quiet. Edit Schedule and View Schedule always line the same published amendment, so a scheduler and his members see the same thing. (With “Latest only”, the published AL3 would lose its line on Edit Schedule the moment a scheduler started AL4, while View Schedule still lined it.)
Can the older ones still be told from unchanged words?

For ordinary sight: yes, at both sizes. The weakest are a changed time in sky (13 from the grey that times are written in) and in lilac (15); everything else is 18 or more. Quieter than a lined change, which is the point.

Each cell: how far the amendment’s colour is from that kind of unchanged word, for ordinary sight, for a red-green colour-blind reader, and in black and white (difference in lightness). Under about 10 they look alike.

A point for you: colour-blind readers

A red-green colour-blind reader loses some older changes completely. A name changed in AL3 (ice) looks the same to him as an unchanged white name (1 apart), and a time changed in AL2 (pink) the same as an unchanged grey time (4 apart). When every amendment was lined, the line still told him something had changed; now, for older amendments, nothing on the row does. The day’s “Changed in” line and the changes window still list them.

A fix that keeps your pick: a switch of the person’s own, “Underline every amendment”, for anyone who needs it. It is the try-out’s “Every AL” choice, kept as a personal setting; everyone else sees your pick. No colour change fixes this: to these readers every pale tint sits on white or on grey.

In black and white
Nearly all of it goes: lemon and ice print as white words; pink, clay, sky and lilac as the grey of the times. A printed or exported schedule should keep today’s small “ALn” words, as question 4 already says.
What it changes of your earlier decisions
You decided that changed times, areas and remarks keep their marks. Older amendments keep a mark (the colour) but a weaker one.
