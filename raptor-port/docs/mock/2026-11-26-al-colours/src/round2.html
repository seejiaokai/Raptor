The line, and what sits under it

You liked the line but it touched things: on Edit Schedule the line under “1B: BFM-7” ran into the TPOD and 2 TKS chips, and 15:15’s sat on its row’s edge. I staged an amendment in every tight place I could find (list under the table) and measured, under every line, how far it is from the nearest thing below it.

What I chose
The line now sits directly under the letters’ feet, 1px thick, and nothing is given extra room. On my first page it hung 3px lower, outside the room the words own, which is why it reached the chips. I tried it 2px and 1px lower first: both still came within 2px of the chips, of the next row’s edge, or of the row a block’s label sits on. A waiting change has the same line, dotted.
Why this one
The other two ways out both cost more. Giving a changed item a little room underneath would push its row, and every row under it, down by a pixel or two the moment something is amended, and you have said rows and pucks must not shift. Drawing the line as a picture inside each word’s box breaks on names that wrap to two lines. A plain tight underline moves nothing and wraps with the words.
A jet whose stores changed
That mark sits on the whole remark cell, and a line there would have to run under the chips, on the row’s edge. So for stores the chips themselves take the amendment’s colour (their edge dotted while waiting) and the remark’s words, which did not change, stay as they are.
On the Scheduler Board
A box cannot underline its own words, so the line is drawn inside the box under them, 4px up from the box’s foot and a little in from its sides. It never lies on the box’s own edge.

With the missing-time rule

The rule from the other try-out: a row with a person on it needs both its times before the day can be signed or published. Here it is on the duty desks, drawn with my recommended amendment look and the proposed colours. In every picture the outline is the rule’s; the coloured line or tab is the amendment’s.

When it happens
A scheduler empties a time on a day that is already published, usually to retype it. Rare, and brief: it lasts until he types the new time.
Do the two marks read as one?
Choices and their costs

A. As drawn. The outline keeps its red and sits on the box’s edge; the amendment’s dotted line sits inside it with 3px between them. Cost: when the next amendment is AL4, a pale clay-brown line sits inside a red box. They differ in shape (a closed box against a dotted line) more than in colour.

B. Move AL4 away from the reds. Cost: every free colour that is far from red is already AL1, AL2 or AL3, so AL4 would have to trade places with one of them, and then that one sits inside the red box instead.

C. Hide the amendment line while the box is empty. Cost: an emptied time on a published day would stop looking like a change that is waiting, which is the very thing the scheduler must not miss.

Recommendation
A. Keep the outline red and outermost, the amendment line inside it. On View Schedule no outline at all, as you are leaning: a reader cannot fix a missing time, and the published face never shows an empty one that is still waiting.
What it changes of your earlier decisions
Nothing about the rule. On the Scheduler Board today the waiting mark is a dotted edge round the whole box with the red line just inside it; here the red line is the edge and the waiting mark is a dotted line inside the box, so the two no longer share an edge.

Look-alikes: an amendment colour next to a schedule colour

You were right, and I should have caught it on the first page. I had checked my colours against the app’s warning, button and puck colours, but not against the sections’ own colours. Three of my first four sat on one: periwinkle on the Common Programme’s stripe and heading, mint on Available crew’s, orange on Personal Inputs’. I have changed the sequence.

What is free on the schedule

The schedule already spends red, orange, amber, green, mint, cyan, blue-violet and purple on its sections, pucks and warnings. Measured against all of them, only two strong colours are left with nothing near them: lemon yellow and pink. Everything else is some section’s colour, or close to it. So the new sequence gives AL1 and AL2 those two, and from AL3 on uses pale tints (ice, clay, sky, lilac), which are much lighter than any section’s stripe or heading, so they cannot be taken for one.
