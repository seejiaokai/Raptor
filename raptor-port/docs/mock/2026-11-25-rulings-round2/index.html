Question A · signing a day New

A sign-off that answers only some of the day's OIL questions: are the rest asked again at the next sign-off box?

When it happens

A scheduler signs a weekend or holiday that has two or more duty inputs with no OIL answer yet. When he picks the first sign-off name, the day's OIL list opens. He answers one row, leaves the others and presses Save. That is roughly weekly.

The example

Fri 17 Jul, made a public holiday. Three inputs have no OIL answer: Ranger's Training "Range brief", Hunter's Duty "Gate duty" and Nomad's OD. Saber picks Ace in the CUR CK box. The list opens with "3 TO ANSWER". Saber picks Yes · HO on Ranger's Range brief only, leaves the other two and presses Save.

What the app does today

Save keeps the one answer and signs Ace in. The two rows left unpicked are treated as if Saber had pressed Later.

The other three boxes then ask nothing. SKED CK, PLANNED BY and APPROVED BY take their names straight in, with no list.

The day publishes, and the note names the two inputs left.

Why this is a question

Your words so far are about Later: "Later asks no more that day." They cover a scheduler who presses Later. They do not cover a scheduler who presses Save with a row still unpicked. The builder treated the two the same and did not ask. And nothing on the list tells the scheduler that Save will leave the unpicked rows as Later does. He learns it from the note after the day has gone out.

The choice

  • Keep it as built, and say so on the list. The rows left unpicked are not asked again at the other boxes. When a row is left unpicked, the list's foot says what Save will do with it, in the same few words as Later's line.
  • Ask again at each box. Every sign-off box opens the list again until each row has an answer or Later is pressed.
  • Keep it as built, with no new line. Nothing changes in the app.

Recommended

Keep it as built, and say so on the list. This is a recommendation, not something your words settle. Asking again at the next box would mean an answer given there changes what the day earns after the first name has signed, and that would clear the name just signed. The publish note already names every input left, so nothing goes out unseen.

The cost: one new line on the OIL list. They are new words, so the exact line is drawn for you before anything is built. As today, nobody on the unpicked inputs earns OIL.

Question B · the published schedule New

A changed late cut-off: should it move the LATE marks on published days at once?

When it happens

An admin changes the late cut-off behind the gear on the Inputs or SANS page while some days of the week are already published. That is roughly a few times a year.

The example

Ranger's LL for Tue 14 Jul is filed on Thu 25 Jun. The cut-off is 14 days before the week starts, so it is on time. Tuesday is signed and published. Later Saber raises the cut-off to 21 days.

What the app does today

Saber types 21 and presses Save. The window's own example now reads "inputs are due by the end of Mon 22 Jun", so an input filed on 25 Jun is late.

With no other step, Ranger's LL on the published Tuesday reads LATE.

Nothing reads pending and nobody signs again. Tuesday keeps its four names.

The View-only Sched page was not looked at in this walk.

Why this is a question

Two of your rulings pull in different directions here. For the Logic page's settings you ruled that a changed setting reaches a published day only from its next amendment. Separately, you ruled that a LATE mark a scheduler drops leaves a published day at once, because it changes nobody's day (the reason you were given, and accepted). Neither says what a changed cut-off does. The first is about the settings the day's checks read, and whether the late cut-off is one of those was never asked. The second is about a scheduler dropping one mark by hand.

The Logic page already says "A changed late cut-off moves the LATE marks at once, on published days too", but that was never put to you as a question.

The choice

  • At once, as built. The LATE marks on published days follow the new cut-off straight away. Nothing reads pending and no names clear.
  • From the next amendment. A published day keeps the LATE marks it went out with. A changed cut-off makes the day read pending, and its marks move only when it is published again.

Recommended

At once, as built. This is a recommendation, not something your words settle. LATE is a mark, not a check: it closes no seat and raises no warning. It also follows the reason you accepted for a mark a scheduler drops, that it changes nobody's day, so it should cost no new signatures.

The cost: a published day can show a LATE mark it did not go out with, or lose one, with no amendment and nobody signing for it. The other choice costs more work: every published day holding an input whose mark would change reads pending, and its four names clear until it is signed and published again.

Question D · the end-time rule New

The AMT sim block's own BRIEF and DEBRIEF rows: must they have an end time too?

When it happens

A scheduler puts an AMT sim block on a day. The block has three rows of its own: BRIEF, BOX and DEBRIEF. On the demo week the BRIEF and DEBRIEF rows are typed with a start and no end. It would come up on every day that carries a block typed that way. How often your real weeks do is yours to say.

The example

Sat 18 Jul carries the demo's own AMT block: BRIEF 11:00 with no end, BOX 11:30 to 12:30, DEBRIEF 12:30 with no end.

The rule read word for word. First your phone, then a desktop. BRIEF and DEBRIEF each have a start and an empty END, so both are outlined red (pink tags 3 and 4). BOX has both times and is left alone.

Why this is a question

Your words were "every item must have a start and end time", and the rule as recorded lists "a sim". Read word for word, these two rows are items with no end. So a day carrying this block could not be signed or published until someone types two more times.

But the app treats a blank end on these two rows as normal. The Logic page's sim debrief check speaks of "an AMT's DEBRIEF row (AMT debrief, where that row has no end)", and the app already gives both rows their length from the Logic page's settings. Whether you meant them when you said "every item" was never asked.

The choice

  • Leave these two rows out. An AMT block's own BRIEF and DEBRIEF rows get no red outline and are not counted. The BOX row, and every other sim row, follow the rule.
  • Count them, as the rule reads. They are outlined red, and the day cannot be signed or published until both ends are typed.

Recommended

Leave these two rows out. This is a recommendation, not something your words settle. They are the sim's own brief and debrief, their length already comes from the Logic page, and counting them would block a routine day for two times nobody types today.

The cost: one exception to "every item". These two rows can still go out with no end typed. The other choice costs two more times typed on every such day before it can be signed.
