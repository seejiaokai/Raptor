2 · The signing look

You asked: round 3's picture 10b read as published while 142 had not signed. Each schedule must show plainly its own day's state.

Answer: one rule. A schedule's day head (DRAFT, or the ORIG seal once its four have signed) is the only "published" mark on it, and it is always that squadron's own day. A wave that belongs to the other squadron wears no status chip at all: only its grey badge, and under its name one quiet grey line in words, whose wave it is and whether it is out. Each picture is one screen as it is, top bar to the wave; between the day head and the wave the app shows the issues bar and the Common Programme, hidden here to keep the picture short.

Why this reads right

  • Only one place says "published", and it is always this schedule's own squadron. 142's day reads DRAFT until 142's four sign it, whatever 149 has done.
  • The shared wave speaks in words, not a chip. Round 3 gave the wave its own ORIG or DRAFT chip, which looked like the day's state. The grey line uses the look of the date beside "Monday", so it reads as a note, never a status. It reads "149's wave · published by 149" once 149 is out, and "149's wave · 149 publishes it · not published yet" before.
  • 142's own line is never lost. In moment 2, 142 has published but its RU sits in 149's wave: the line says "RU goes out when 149 publishes." A member reading View-only Sched sees the same line.
  • On 149's schedule nothing new is needed: 142's RU wears only its badge, and 149's day head says it all.

(a) If 149 has no wave that day, can 142 add a line to 149's schedule?

No (my reading of your rulings on shared waves, not yet your ruling; it is in the list at the top). "+ Wave" on 142's board says 149 has no wave that day. 142 plans in a wave of its own, and 149 may join it.

(b) Is round 3's picture 9 142's board right after "Join a 149 wave"?

Yes. It is 142's Scheduler Board: 142's blank line inside 149's wave. Redrawn with item 2's grey line in place of the chips, and this time with the board's own bar at the top. That bar names no squadron today, which is why the picture could not tell you whose board it was. Mark 4f offers a small grey "142" beside the board's day title, the same badge recipe; nothing in the bar moves (measured). It goes with round 3's Q2.

(c) Can both sides edit? Does a 142 scheduler have rights on 149's schedule?

Only on what concerns both. A line that concerns both squadrons may be changed by either scheduler, from either schedule. Everything else only its own squadron's scheduler changes.

Recommended: yes. The app already keeps one input for several people, and the Inputs page already picks several people. 7 · Crew rest across two schedules

You ruled: each squadron's admin keeps its own crew rest, and the later line's schedule judges.

The worked case. Saber, a 142 man, is lent to 149 and flies on 149's VL in its night wave on Monday: he lands at 21:10, so with the debrief his day ends at 23:10. On Tuesday 142 plans him on its 2nd wave, in at 10:00. 142's crew rest is 12 hours, so he is clear only at 11:10: a breach. 149's (made up for this case) is 10 hours, which he would meet. The Tuesday line is the later one and it is 142's, so 142's 12 hours judge.

  • 142's schedule: the warning on Tuesday (7a), and the dotted "breaks tomorrow's crew rest" ring on Saber in 149's VL (7b). VL shows on 142's schedule, with 149's badge, because it holds a 142 man (my reading).
  • 149's schedule: the same dotted ring on its own VL (7c), so 149's scheduler sees it too.
  • Who may fix it: 142 moves its own Tuesday line, or either scheduler changes 149's VL, since it holds a 142 man (my reading).

How it was drawn. The warning and the rings are the app's own, raised by its real rules. To make the case, Saber was put on Tuesday's line and that wave's RU in-time changed from 11:40 to 10:00 in the demo data. In the warning only "Monday" is re-lettered "Monday, 149's night wave,"; every other word is the app's. Saber's "Attach · 149" row, which 142's Unavailable list would also carry, is not drawn.
