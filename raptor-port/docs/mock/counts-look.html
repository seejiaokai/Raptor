Seen in the app

Question 1 An input for several people is titled “Ace +2” in its window. Should the title name everyone?

This happens whenever someone opens an input that has several people in it, such as a Training for three, from the Inputs page or the schedule. It happens often; my guess is several times a day. The same “Ace +2” also names the input in the OIL question’s heading and in a few other questions about it.

Today
“Ace +2 · 15 Jul”: the first name from A to Z, then how many more. With ten people: “Basher +9 · 16 Jul”.
Named
“Ace, Hex, Ranger · 15 Jul” fits on one row. With ten people, the title is cut off with “…” after about six names, because the title stays on one row.
Also on screen
Just under the title, the window says “3 picked”, and each of the three is lit in the list of people.
Your earlier word
On 10 Oct you ruled that an input’s card, and the desktop Inputs list, name everyone, never “+1”. The window’s title was not part of that question.
I recommend
Keep “Ace +2” in the title. The people are lit just below it. A title of every name would be cut off on a phone, and it would need a new design to wrap.

This happens when someone deletes an input that has several people in it, or takes himself out of one, or changes its dates. One rule makes all of these words, so one answer settles them all. How often it happens has not been measured; my guess is a few times a week.

Today

The same count is in “It stays for the other 2 people”, “Date changes apply to all 3”, “3 people’s Training is already on the programme”, and the Inputs calendar’s own delete question. “Input added for 3 people” is said only when the new input is not already in view.
Named

Also on screen

I recommend
Name them. A delete for several people takes the input away from each of them, and the screen does not show who they are at that moment. Even ten names fit in the question’s box.

Named

Question 4 The publish note says “4 inputs have no OIL answer”. Should it name all four?

This happens when a scheduler publishes a weekend day or a holiday while four or more of its inputs still have no OIL answer (he pressed Later when the sign-off asked). Up to three are named today. Four or more at once is rare; that is a guess.

Today
“Saturday 18 Jul published and approved. 4 inputs have no OIL answer, so nobody on them earns OIL.”: two rows.
Named
“Saturday 18 Jul published and approved. Ranger’s Training, Hex’s Training, Ace’s Training and Tally’s Training have no OIL answer, so nobody on them earns OIL.”: three rows. It names them the way the note already names three, as the next picture shows.
Three, today
“Published AL1 · 1 item on Sat only · approved by Bolt · 1 day with changes still held · Saturday 18 Jul: Tally’s Training, Ace’s Training and Hex’s Training have no OIL answer, so nobody on them earns OIL”: an amendment published later the same day, three inputs still unanswered, four rows.
I recommend
Name them all. This note is the last word before nobody on those inputs earns OIL, and a scheduler needs to know which inputs to fix. It is rare, so the longer note is seldom seen.

Kept as they are unless you say otherwise

Each of these numbers is the fact itself, or tapping it opens the list it counts. One line each, with why it stays.

  • The day’s “3 pending” button: it opens the changes it counts.
  • The bell’s red number, and its “↓ 3 more” at the foot: one counts unread lines; the other says there is more to scroll to, as you chose on 8 Nov.
  • The bell’s holiday line, “3 inputs need an OIL answer”: tapping it opens the list that names them, and the count goes down as each is answered.
  • The warning list’s heading, “⚠ 2 issues · 3 warnings”: the list it heads names every one.
  • The changes window: “5 changes”, and a shared input shown as “Input · Meeting · 4 people” with the names under it: the list below names them, as you chose on 7 Oct.
  • The list of changes waiting to go out: “Range safety brief · 4 people”, with the names under it: the names are listed right there.
  • The Inputs calendar’s “+2 more” and a shared bar’s “4 · Meeting”: tapping opens the day with every name; you chose the bar’s count on 10 Oct.
  • A Leave War day’s “+2” corner, and the bidding chip’s “Open 1 Apr – 30 Jun +1”: each opens the full list.
  • “Published AL1 · 1 item on Sat only”: the number of items is the size of the amendment, and they are on the schedule.
  • “3 differences from AL1 are pending” after switching to a saved plan: it is the same count as the day’s pending button, which opens them.
  • “2 inputs need an OIL answer.” after a load or a plan switch: the note opens the list that names them.
  • “OIL answered for 3 inputs”: he has just answered them himself in that list.
  • The input window’s “3 picked”: each one is lit right beside it.
  • The Grades window’s “3 people” beside each grade: it is how many hold the grade; deleting one is question 7.
  • Leave War notes such as “3 days filed. 2 days were not yours to change”, “2 of 5 days deleted”, and the picked block’s “3 days”: the days are the ones he just picked.
  • The Approvals page’s “3 pending · 1 new”, and “+2 pending in JAN – DEC 27 ›”: tapping opens them.
  • OIL and leave figures: balances, “2 OIL awards (Sat 18 Jul, Sun 19 Jul)”, “half a day”, “3 days”: the number is the fact, and the awards are already named.
  • Admin and Logic: “Cleared 12 items”, “3 rules back to squadron standard”, “2 new bug reports”, “3 reports, newest first”: each counts what was just done, or a list shown below.
  • The Tracker: “3 events deleted — Undo restores them”, and its fail totals: the events were just picked; the fails are listed.
  • The Insights tiles: sorties, formations, warnings: the numbers are the point.
