1 · The guideHow the app speaks

The app speaks as a capable colleague on the scheduling desk would. It tells you what happened, what it means for you and what to do, in a sentence a person would say.

Calm, exact, brief. It is not a form (“Operation completed”), not a telegram (“Published, no times, no OIL”) and not a mate (“All done!”).

Where it comes fromYour own words, the best outside guides on writing for screens (GOV.UK, Google, Apple, Microsoft, Atlassian, the US plain-language guide), the one measured study of tone, and the tool you sent. Nothing was installed. The good parts are written into our own guide in our own words.

What happened, why, what it costs, where to put it right, every name and every number. Only filler is cut. This is your ruling of 13 October.

3A dash is not how the app joins two thoughts

Your ruling. In order: the linking word (so, because, but). A comma where the second part only renames the first. A colon where the first part is only a name. A full stop for a new thought or an instruction. A dash only where nothing else reads as well, and never two in a note.

TodayThe 07:30 brief was after the new take-off — clearedGuideThe 07:30 brief was after the new take-off, so it was cleared

4The linking word is a fact, and it is never guessed

“So” and “because” say why. The writer reads the link from what the app does, and says how it is known. Where it cannot be told, the note becomes two sentences.

GuessedYou left OIL Earn, so the next undo will change the dayCheckedLeft OIL Earn without undoing. The next undo would change the day itself.

5What happened first

Then what it means for you. Then what to do. One done thing may be two words (“Saved”, “Monday published”). Anything after it is a full sentence.

6The squadron’s words stay

OIL, AL, CX, SANS, FO, HO, PH, crew rest, MAIN, SPARE and the names the screens already use are never swapped or explained away. Everything else takes the short everyday word.

Notrequires · prior to · is displayedButneeds · before · shows

7Say what will work, with the reason

A refusal says why and what to do instead. “Not” is written out where it is the whole point of a warning.

GuideMonday is still a draft. Publish the day first, then its changes.

8You, never we

The app is nobody. No “we”, no “please”, no “sorry”, no “oops”, no blame. When another person did it, the note names them.

9One steady voice

A success and a refusal sound the same. A cost is said plainly. No capitals to stress a word, no exclamation mark, no “Heads up”. The app’s own names in capitals (MAIN, SPARE, ALL AVAIL) keep them.

10A pair speaks as a pair

The on-note and the off-note of one switch are the same sentence turned round. Refusals of one kind start the same way.

GuideLine is SPARE now: … · Line is MAIN now: …

11Nothing added, and read by someone else

A rewording claims nothing today’s wording does not. Each one is then read cold by a fresh reader who has never seen the old wording. If their answers differ, it goes back.

12None of the habits that mark machine writing

From the tool you sent: the dash everywhere, the opener that announces instead of saying, “not X, but Y” where nobody thought X, a closing line that only repeats, lists of three for the sound, the stock words (crucial, key, robust, seamless, ensure).

How it was checked. The guide was read three times before reaching you, each time by a fresh Opus reader and by Fable, neither seeing the other’s report. They found real faults and all are fixed. The heaviest: an early draft let the writer guess a linking word, and a guess can turn a note round (check 4 shows the case). The third read was done with the tool you sent beside it, and it also checked every reworded note against the app’s code. That is how it found that one of your thirteen (N8) is a note the app can no longer show.

One reading of mine for you to confirmWhen the aims pull apart I have put them in this order: every fact first, then a sentence a person would say, then as short as those two allow. So a human sentence is sometimes a word or two longer than the clipped one you approved. Where a wording ends longer than what the app shows today, it is marked Longer below. If you would rather have short win over human, say so and I redo the list.
