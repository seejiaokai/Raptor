Question 1 · the OIL card's last words

End the card's line with "and the OIL follows", or "and the OIL changes to match"?

When it happens

An admin, or the man himself, taps a weekend or holiday on the Leave War that shows OIL the app worked out. The card says where the OIL came from: the published schedule (F2) or an accepted duty input (F7).

The example

Anvil is on a Range safety duty, 09:00 to 12:00 on Sat 18 Jul. Saturday is published, and his OIL box shows HO*. The admin taps it. Under "Where it came from", the line approved in round 3 reads "Earned off a duty input that was accepted. Change that input and publish the day again, and the OIL follows." A reader given only these words read "the OIL follows" two ways: the OIL changes to match, or the OIL is given afterwards, as if the line were steps for getting OIL. "Changes to match" can only mean the first.

The choice

  • 1 · "…and the OIL changes to match." On both lines: Earned off the published schedule. Change the schedule and publish the day again, and the OIL changes to match. and Earned off a duty input that was accepted. Change that input and publish the day again, and the OIL changes to match.
  • 2 · Keep round 3's "…and the OIL follows."

Recommended

1. Two words longer, and it can only be read one way. A second reader given these words read them as intended.

That reader also asked whether "Change that input" means only editing it, or rejecting or deleting it too. Round 3's words have the same phrase, and the card's job is to point at the input, so I would leave it; it is checked in the running app before anything is built.

Question 2 · the Logic page, "An input closes its hours"

Plainer words for the line under "An input closes its hours"?

When it happens

A scheduler opens the Logic page to see why a man shows as busy, or not, while an input of his is on the day. Rare, but this is where the app explains the rule.

The example

Round 3's words (your "F as written"): "On the working copy an input closes the man for the hours it covers, from the moment it is filed. SANS availability and an upchit close nothing, nor does an input a scheduler takes off the programme, cancels (CX) or marks information only." A reader given those words alone, and a second given a first rewording, found:

  • "the man" could be the man who filed it, not the man it is for;
  • "from the moment it is filed" could mean his busy time starts at that minute;
  • "closes" is used only in the check's own name and one other row, so a reader has to guess what it does to the man;
  • "nor does an input a scheduler … cancels" reads as "it never counted", where in fact it stops counting once the scheduler does it;
  • the scheduler's controls are Reject and the ⓘ marked "Info only", not "takes off the programme" and "information only".

The choice

  • 1 · The plainer words: On the working copy the man an input is for counts as taken for the hours it covers, as soon as it is filed. SANS availability and an upchit take nobody's hours. Once a scheduler rejects an input, cancels it (CX) or marks it Info only, it stops taking the man's hours.
  • 2 · The same, with "busy" for "taken", as the third reader suggested: On the working copy the man an input is for counts as busy for the hours it covers, as soon as it is filed. SANS availability and an upchit make nobody busy. Once a scheduler rejects an input, cancels it (CX) or marks it Info only, it stops making him busy.
  • 3 · Keep round 3's words.

Recommended

2. A third reader, given only choice 1's words, understood them as meant: the man is busy for those hours from the moment it is filed; never for SANS availability or an upchit; freed by Reject, CX or Info only. But "counts as taken" made that reader think first of "picked for something", and of leave taken. "Busy" is the reader's own word for it, and the plainest. The reader also asked whether "On the working copy" means only there, whether "as soon as it is filed" means no Accept is needed, and whether CX cancels the input or a programme item. Each is checked in the running app before anything is built.

Choice 2's own words have not been read cold yet; they are before anything is built. Choice 1 is nine words longer than round 3's, choice 2 eight. The check keeps its name, "An input closes its hours". I stopped after three rounds of rewording, which is my reading of your limit on review rounds.

Question 3 · hiding an "Overlaps standby" warning

A hidden "Overlaps standby" warning: give it F5's new words too?

When it happens

A man on an SC standby line that is not flying is also down for a meeting in his standby hours. The warning list shows an amber "Overlaps standby" line. The scheduler knows about it and presses the ✕ to hide it.

The example

Rebel is on SC AM, 07:00 to 13:00 on Mon 13 Jul, and has a Range brief meeting 09:00 to 10:00. In round 3 you approved F5's new note for a hidden conflict between two schedule items: it stays hidden while the same man is on the same two items, even if their times change. Round 3 also told you (point E10) that every other warning comes back when its words change, so they keep today's words. That was wrong for this one: the app's code keeps an "Overlaps standby" warning hidden the same way: by the man and his two items, so moving the meeting to 10:00 would leave it hidden. (Read in the code, not yet tried on screen; it is tried before anything is built.) So today's words, "it comes back if the situation changes" and "It returns if the warning changes", are not true for it.

The choice

  • 1 · Yes: F5's note, and the matching hover words above, on every warning the app keeps hidden by its man and two items, an "Overlaps standby" line included. Every other warning keeps today's words.
  • 2 · No: keep today's words on "Overlaps standby". They would stay untrue for it.

Recommended

1. The app treats both the same, so they should say the same. The words would follow how the app keeps the warning hidden, not its heading.

Settled without asking, for your information

No tap reaches them today (a tap opens the input's window, which already shows your F3 line), so there is nothing to picture. My reading, not your ruling: they will follow your F3 line, "Only Ace, Ranger (who filed it) or an admin can change this input" where the man who filed it may, "Only Ace or an admin can change this input" where he may not.
