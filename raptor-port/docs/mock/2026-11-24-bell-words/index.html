The bell after an Undo: the new words

Nothing in the app has changed. These are drawings for you to approve. When an admin presses Undo on a change, anyone who had already read about that change in the bell needs to be told. This page shows the words the bell would use. Each "Today" picture is the real bell. Each "New" picture is that same bell with only its words changed. "The first line" below means the line a person read before the Undo. If nobody had read it, it still simply goes from the bell, and the person who pressed Undo gets no line. Tap any picture to enlarge it.

Round 2: the words are clearer. What changed is highlighted.

What to look at now: eight questions

  1. 1. Should every bell line drop the crossed-out words and "taken back", and say what is true now instead?I recommend: Yes, every line. The new line says what is true now.
  2. 2. In the change history, should a line about an Undo say "undone" where it says "taken back" today?I recommend: Yes. Change that one word and keep everything else. "Undone" is your word for an Undo, and the change history stays a record of what was pressed.
  3. 3. When a change to an input or a public holiday is undone, should a person who had read about it get a new line?I recommend: Yes, as part of this job. Without it the person still believes the first line.
  4. 4. One SANS line still says "taken back". Should it get the new words too?I recommend: Yes. Otherwise it would be the only crossed-out line left in the bell.
  5. 5. When Saber presses Undo and then Redo, should the line for the Redo say "again"?I recommend: Yes. Without "again" the line looks like the first one repeated by mistake.
  6. 6. When a Post out date is moved earlier, should an admin get two lines: one for the move and one for the posting?I recommend: Yes, both. The first says what Saber changed, the second says the posting has happened.
  7. 7. What should a tap on the new line "…is no longer a public holiday" open?I recommend: Nothing. The tap only marks the line as read. It is the same as a tap on a line about a deleted person.
  8. 8. When Saber changes two things about an input in one go and then undoes it, what should the line say?I recommend: The first change in the title, the second in the sentence under it. A line about an input today names only the first two changes, so this keeps that rule.

Three cases still have no words

  • Bidding dates changed so that some days were dropped and others added, such as 1 Jan – 31 Mar changed to 1 Feb – 31 May. An Undo of that closes some dates and opens others.
  • A Post out changed so that no outcome is picked ("off the manpower").
  • A Post out whose date and outcome are both changed in one save.

Question 1 Should every bell line drop the crossed-out words and "taken back", and say what is true now instead?

Ranger reads in the bell that the LL on Mon 20 – Tue 21 Jul is approved. Saber then presses Undo.

Today
Ranger's bell shows "Your leave · Mon 20 – Tue 21 Jul", then "LL approved" crossed out, then "taken back · Saber".
New
It would show "Your LL on Mon 20 – Tue 21 Jul is no longer approved. · Saber", and under it "It's pending again."
I recommend
Yes, every line. The new line says what is true now, so Ranger does not have to work it out from a crossed-out one. You chose the crossed-out look on 5 Nov. This would replace it on every line, not only the SANS ones. No new line says "taken back". Where a word for it is needed, the word is "undone".

Question 2 In the change history, should a line about an Undo say "undone" where it says "taken back" today?

Saber presses Undo on the approval of Ranger's LL, then opens the change history.

Today
It shows the Undo as "Undo — a decision on a bid". The words "taken back" appear in two other lines: when an approved leave is put back to a bid ("Ranger · LL approval taken back — back to a bid · 20 Jul–21 Jul") and for Undo post out ("Leave War · Ranger · posting out taken back · 20 Jul").
New
The line "Undo — a decision on a bid" stays. The Undo post out line would read "Leave War · Ranger · posting out undone · 20 Jul". Putting an approved leave back to a bid is not an Undo, so that line keeps its words.
I recommend
Yes. Change that one word and keep everything else. The change history is a record of what was pressed, not a message to one person, so its lines keep their short form. Only the word for an Undo changes.

Question 3 When a change to an input or a public holiday is undone, should a person who had read about it get a new line?

Question 5 When Saber presses Undo and then Redo, should the line for the Redo say "again"?

Saber moves Ranger's LL to Thu 23 – Fri 24 Jul. Ranger reads that line. Saber presses Undo, then Redo.

Question 6 When a Post out date is moved earlier, should an admin get two lines: one for the move and one for the posting?

Ranger was posted out as SANS on Mon 20 Jul and Hex, an admin, read that line. Saber moves the Post out date to Fri 17 Jul.

Today

New
It would show "Ranger's Post out date moved to Fri 17 Jul. · Saber", then the fresh "Posted out · now SANS" line.
I recommend
Yes, both. The first says what Saber changed, the second says the posting has happened.

Question 7 What should a tap on the new line "…is no longer a public holiday" open?

Hex, an admin, reads "Public holiday · Mon 10 Aug / 3 inputs need an OIL answer". Saber then undoes the holiday.

Today
That line just vanishes. A tap on the original line opened the OIL list for the holiday's dates: every input on those dates still waiting for an OIL answer, each with Yes and No.
New
It would show "Mon 10 Aug is no longer a public holiday. · Saber". The OIL list the original line opened is now empty, except for inputs on a weekend inside a longer holiday, which still need an answer.
I recommend
Nothing. The tap only marks the line as read. A tap opens nothing, the same as a tap on a line about a deleted person. This would be the only new line that does not open what its original line opened.

The first change in the title, the second in the sentence under it. A line about an input today names only the first two changes, so this keeps that rule.

On an iPhone, a date in a long line may wrap onto a second line.
