The OIL bar, under the top bar

You ruled on 13 October that “Not swapped — OIL not answered” moves from the foot of the screen to the band under the top bar, where the “Not saved” warning sits, and that you see it drawn before it is built. This is that drawing. Nothing of it is built yet. Tap any picture to see it full size.

How to read the pictures. Built is the app as it stands, at your iPhone’s size (390 wide) or a desktop’s (1440 wide). Drawn is the same real screen with the bar drawn in its new place; nothing behind the drawn bar works yet. The pictures were taken in a Windows browser, so the letters are slightly narrower than on your iPhone.

1 · On a phoneScheduler Board

A

One line under the board’s own bar

What changesToday the bar floats at the foot, half the screen wide, over the names in Available crew. Drawn, it is one line across the screen directly under the board’s buttons: the words on the left, “Answer OIL” and “OK” on the right. It covers nothing. The board moves down by that one line while the bar is up.

What is the sameThe words, the two buttons and what each does. It still stays until you tap one of them.

The lookThe band is the “Not saved” warning’s own: the same height and the same amber. “Answer OIL” keeps its cyan, as the one button to press.

Wider lettersYour iPhone’s letters are wider than these. If the words and both buttons do not fit one line, the words keep one unbroken line and the buttons go under them at the right. No word is cut. The third picture shows that case in letters a seventh wider. To be seen on your own phone before it is called done.

On Edit ScheduleThe same line sits along the bottom of the app’s top bar, exactly where “Not saved” sits. The week moves down by one line.

When a save has also failedBoth lines show, “Not saved” on top and the OIL line under it. “Not saved” goes first because it is about losing work.

3 · On a desktopThe same line, at the right

On a desktop the words and buttons sit at the right end of the line, as “Not saved” and its Retry do. Today’s desktop bar reads well at the foot; it moves so the bar lives in one place on every screen, as you ruled.

By keyboard, built with it
  • When the line appears, the keyboard goes to “Answer OIL”. Today it is over a hundred Tab presses away.
  • Escape closes the line, as OK does.
  • A passing note raised while the line is up goes back to its usual place at the foot. It no longer has to sit above the bar.
