SANS warning lines — questions from the bug check

Round 1 · 27 Nov 26 · from the walk of the new amber line ("no availability filed") and the "offered … only" line. Every picture is the real app. Tap a picture to enlarge it.

What to look at now

  1. The pop-up after you plant him: which words? — recommend: the crew list's short words
  2. A sim's line doesn't say OFT or AMT, and invents an end time — recommend: name the sim, mark an assumed end
  3. The order of Fly, OFT, AMT differs between the Inputs page and the lines — recommend: Fly · OFT · AMT everywhere
  4. One man on two flights gets two lines — recommend: keep, one line a flight
  5. On leave and planned with nothing filed: two lines — recommend: keep both
  6. The amber "A" mark's hover words don't mention "nothing filed" — recommend: the new words below

To answer: "all as recommended", or a number and your choice.

1. The pop-up after you plant him: which words?

When it happens: a scheduler drops or taps a SANS man who has filed nothing into a seat. A short pop-up appears at the bottom for a few seconds. Several times a week.

Today the pop-up repeats the new warning line. But the crew list, and the hint while you hover over the seat, use shorter words. And if his warning line had been hidden earlier, the pop-up already falls back to the short words. So the same fact reads two ways.

  • A (recommended): always the crew list's words. Marlin — SANS — no availability filed for today. For a man whose SANS posting starts on a later date (Ranger, from Fri 17 Jul): Ranger — SANS from Fri 17 Jul: no availability filed. The same words before and after the drop; the warning list keeps the longer line naming the flight.
  • B: always the warning line's words, as today, the hidden case included. Ranger's pop-up then reads Ranger — SANS: no availability filed for today. Planned on VL BFM (T/O 13:00) and no longer names his SANS start date.

2. A sim's line doesn't say OFT or AMT, and invents an end time

When it happens: a SANS man is planned on a simulator (OFT or AMT) and either filed nothing or filed something else. The line sits in the day's warning list.

The line names the sim only by its row's name, Sim EP-9, so it doesn't say whether that is the OFT or the AMT. The old line did (not offering AMT). And when only a start time is typed, the line prints an end the row doesn't show. The app takes the sim as running 90 minutes, so 09:00 becomes 09:00–10:30.

  • Recommended words (reviewed by a fresh Fable): timed — … Planned on OFT EP-9 10:00–11:00 / … Planned on AMT BOX 13:00–14:00; a start but no end — … Planned on OFT EP-9 09:00–10:30 (end assumed), like the flying lines' "landing assumed"; no times — … Planned on OFT EP-9 (no times yet); a row with no name — … Planned on this OFT row (no times yet). The older "available 08:00–12:00 only" line ends the same way, so it changes too.
  • Leave as it is.

3. The order of Fly, OFT, AMT differs between the Inputs page and the lines

When it happens: a SANS man (or a scheduler) files availability on the Inputs page's SANS tab; later a line names what he offered.

The SANS tab's tick boxes read Fly · AMT · OFT. His SANS card's letters, the crew list's letters and the new lines read F, O, A — "offered OFT and AMT only". Two places, two orders.

  • A (recommended): the tick boxes become Fly · OFT · AMT, so every place reads one order.
  • B: the lines and letters follow the tick boxes (F, A, O — "offered AMT and OFT only").
  • C: leave both.

4. One man on two flights gets two lines

When it happens: a SANS man with nothing filed is planned on two flights the same day.

He gets one line for each flight, and the day's count counts both. Hiding one line with its ✕ hides only that flight's line.

  • A (recommended): keep one line a flight — each names its flight, and each can be hidden on its own.
  • B: one line a man a day, naming both flights.

5. On leave and planned, with nothing filed: two lines

When it happens: a SANS man is on leave that day, is planned on a flight anyway, and has filed nothing. Rare.

The list shows the leave line and the new SANS line.

  • A (recommended): keep both — two separate facts, each hidden on its own.
  • B: only the leave line while he is on leave.

6. The amber "A" mark's hover words

When it happens: on a desktop, a scheduler rests the pointer on the amber "A" on a man's puck.

Today it reads Advisory — on shift and also down for a ground event or programme item, or planned outside SANS availability. It doesn't mention a man with nothing filed, who now gets this mark too.

  • Recommended (reviewed by a fresh Fable): Advisory — on shift and also down for a ground event or programme item; or SANS availability not filed, or planned outside it.
  • Leave as it is.
