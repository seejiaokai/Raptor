To answer: "all as recommended", or a number and your choice.

1. A morning or afternoon filer: the warning list still says clock times

When it happens: a SANS man files a morning (AM) or an afternoon (PM) only, and a scheduler plans him outside it. Several times a week.

You ruled that his reason in the crew list says SANS — available AM only. It now does. But the day's warning list still says the clock times the morning stands for: Zenith — SANS: available 00:00–12:00 only. Planned on OFT EP-9 14:00–15:00. The same fact reads two ways on one screen.

  • A (recommended): the warning list says it as filed too — SANS: available AM only. Planned on OFT EP-9 14:00–15:00, SANS: available PM only. Planned on VL BFM (T/O 09:00). Hours he typed stay hours. These words were checked by a fresh Fable.
  • B: as A, and say where the morning ends — SANS: available AM only (to 12:00). Planned on …, for a flight close to midday.
  • C: leave the clock times.

2. Hours typed as 00:00–12:00 are saved as AM

When it happens: a SANS man (or a scheduler for him) files availability, picks Custom and types 00:00 to 12:00 — exactly the morning. Rare.

The form has always turned hours that are exactly a morning (or exactly an afternoon) into AM (or PM): opened again, it shows AM picked. So his reason reads SANS — available AM only, not available 00:00–12:00 only. You said hours he types stay hours; these hours are the morning itself.

  • A (recommended): keep it as AM — it is the same stretch of the day, and the form already shows it as AM.
  • B: keep typed hours as hours — the form remembers he typed them, and his reason says available 00:00–12:00 only.

3. A man on an AMT row renamed BRIEF disappears from the row but is still warned about

When it happens: a scheduler plans men on an AMT row, then renames that row BRIEF or DEBRIEF (the AMT block's brief or debrief row). Rare.

A BRIEF or DEBRIEF row shows no people, so Kraken vanishes from the row. But he is still planned there: the warning list says Kraken — SANS: no availability filed for today. Planned on AMT BRIEF 13:00–14:00, and his name in the crew list keeps the amber "A". Renamed back, he shows again. This was the same before your answers (it read "Sim BRIEF").

  • A (recommended): keep him showing on the row — a planned man is never hidden; the scheduler sees him and moves him. Drawn for you first.
  • B: renaming a row with men on it to BRIEF or DEBRIEF is refused, saying to move them first. Drawn for you first.
  • C: leave it.

4. A sim row with an end time but no start says "(no times yet)"

When it happens: a scheduler types a sim row's end before its start. Rare, and usually only for a moment.

Without a start the app cannot check his hours, so the line reads Planned on this OFT row (no times yet) while the row shows an end. It never makes up a start.

  • A (recommended): leave it — it is right that nothing can be checked yet, and typing the start fixes it.
  • B: say what is missing — e.g. (no start yet); the exact words checked by a fresh Fable first.

5. Other warning lines still call a sim "Sim"

When it happens: any warning about a sim that is not a SANS line — a man in two seats at once, a missed AMT debrief, a man already on a row. Daily.

The SANS lines now say OFT SIM and this OFT row. Right above them, the clash line about the same two rows says Conflict: Sim SIM 10:00–11:00 & Sim 10:00–11:00. Others read the same way: Misses AMT debrief …: Sim BOX … and, in the crew list, already on Sim EP-9 10:00–11:00 · not added twice.

  • A (recommended): name them OFT or AMT too, in every line, the same way — OFT EP-9, this OFT row. A job of its own; the exact words checked by a fresh Fable first.
  • B: leave the other lines.
