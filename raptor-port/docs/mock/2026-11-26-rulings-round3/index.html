Question H · rows with nobody on them Corrected

A row with a start, no end and nobody on it: must it have an end before the day is signed?

When it happens

Whenever a scheduler types an event for the squadron with no one put on it: a dinner, a commander's engagement, a beer call. How often your real weeks have them is yours to say.

The example

Mon 13 Jul's Common Programme: DINNER WITH CMD 18:30, CMD ENGAGEMENT @ CREW ROOM (ALL) 21:30 and, on Friday, MASS DEBRIEF + BEER CALL 17:00 each have a start and no end. A row with a person, ALL or ALL AVAIL on it needs its end whatever you answer here, because your 25 Nov rule covers it either way. This question is only about rows with nobody on them.

The choice

  • 1 · Yes, every row, as you ruled on 25 Nov. A dinner gets a planned end, as a flight gets a planned landing. Nothing changes in your ruling.
  • 2 · Only rows with someone on them (a person, ALL or ALL AVAIL). A row with nobody on it may go out with a start only. This narrows your 25 Nov ruling.
  • 3 · Only on a weekend or holiday. This narrows your 25 Nov ruling too, and gives two rules to remember.

Recommended

1, every row, as you ruled. It is your rule as you said it, one rule with no list to remember, and a typed end is one the scheduler chose rather than one the app made up. In my chat answer this morning I recommended 2 without saying it changed your ruling. That was wrong. 2 is a fair choice, since a row with nobody on it touches nobody's crew rest, conflicts or OIL, but it is your call to change your rule, not mine.

The cost: an end typed on every such row before the day can be signed. A row saved in a template keeps its end, so it is typed once.

One fact corrected: round 2 said an input filed with a start and no end could not be checked. It cannot happen: the app asks for both times, or All day.

Question I · no start time Corrected

A row with someone on it and no start time: is it flagged and refused the same way?

You asked: "What if no start time is indicated. Will it flag the same". Your rule was written down as "a start and no end" only, but your words were wider: "every item must have a start and end time".

"Someone on it" means a person, ALL or ALL AVAIL, or a callsign typed in its people box. A desk showing only the role it came with (SDO, SXO, OPS O) does not count.

When it happens

Most days, while a scheduler is planning. He seats a crew on a line before the take-off is known, puts names on the Standard duty block (SDO, SXO, OPS O), or places a BB standby shift. How often such a row is still blank when the day is signed is yours to say.

What the app does today Corrected

On a working day, nothing marks these rows. On a weekend or holiday the warning list already says the row has no usable times, so nobody on it earns OIL, and the publish note says so too. A man with a whole-day absence (leave, a course, overseas duty, an all-day meeting) is flagged on such a row. A landing typed alone still ends the man's day for the next day's crew rest. Nothing is refused: the day can be signed and published as it is.

The choice

  • Yes: any row with someone on it and no start. Its empty time boxes are outlined, and the sign-off boxes and Publish day are refused, exactly as for a missing end. The cost: every named desk, standby shift and seated line needs its times before the day can be signed.   • Only flights and sims. Desks, standby shifts, programme and ground rows with no times stay free. The cost: two rules to remember, and a man on a desk with no times counts for nothing in crew rest and work hours.
  • No: only a start with no end is flagged. The cost: a flight with four crew and no take-off can be published. On a weekend or holiday the warning list says nobody on it earns OIL, but nothing stops it.

Recommended

Yes. It is what your first words said. With no start, the app can check nothing that needs a time: crew rest from it, conflicts, OIL and hours.

On 29 Sep you asked for a future job: a "?" shown where no timing or end time is entered. The outline is that mark. Recommended: the outline replaces that job, which is then closed.

Question J · when the outline is red Corrected

When does the outline turn red, on a draft and on a published day?

You asked: "Will it be irritating to the user planning the schedule that it keeps seeing red?"

What you ruled on 25 Nov

The outline is red from the moment the start is typed, all through drafting. In this app red already means something is wrong, such as a conflict or a crew-rest breach. A half-planned day is not wrong; it is unfinished.

When it happens

Every day a scheduler drafts ahead, and whenever he changes a published day for its next amendment. A day planned a week early would show its unfinished boxes for days.

The example, drawn two ways

The draft Thursday of question I: VL with no take-off, the SXO desk with no times, the sim with no START.

The choice

  • 1 · Red, as you ruled. On a draft and on a published day alike. The cost: a day drafted early shows red boxes for days, beside the red that means a real problem. (A softer form: red only once he moves off the row, so a line he is still typing does not flash red. Say "J 1 softer" for it.)
  • 2 · Grey while drafting, red once a press is refused. The boxes wear a thin grey outline. When a sign-off box, Publish day or Publish AL is pressed and refused, they turn red and stay red until their times are typed. The same on a draft and on a published day being amended. The cost: this changes your 25 Nov ruling on when the red shows. Grey is easier to overlook, so it needs question G's count line. A slightly larger build.
  • 3 · Red only on days close to flying, such as today and tomorrow. The cost: a third rule to remember, and a box that changes colour overnight with nothing pressed.

Recommended

2, the same on draft and published days. It changes what you ruled, so it is yours to refuse. A real week has many unfinished boxes; red for days beside the red of a conflict would make the real warnings stand out less. Grey says "still to type", and red arrives when it is true: the press was refused. The box is marked from the start and the count is always in the sign-off panel, so nothing is learned late.

Corrected: in the chat I recommended red at once on a published day, for a reason that holds for a draft just as well. One rule for both is simpler.

Reply "J 2", "J 1", "J 1 softer" or "J 3".

Question D · the AMT block's own rows Corrected

Whenever a scheduler puts an AMT sim block on a day. The block has three rows of its own: BRIEF, BOX and DEBRIEF. "+ AMT" makes all three blank; the scheduler types their times. The crew goes on the BOX row; the BRIEF and DEBRIEF rows usually have nobody on them.

  • Leave them out always, even with a person on them.

Recommended

Leave them out while nobody is on them. The app knows their ends for everything they are used for then. With a person on one, its end matters for his conflicts and OIL, so it is typed.

The cost: one exception to "every item", for the AMT block's own two rows with nobody on them.
