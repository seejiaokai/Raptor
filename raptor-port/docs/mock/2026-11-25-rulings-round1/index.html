Question 1 · OIL

A weekend flight published with a take-off and no landing: should it earn OIL?

When it happens

A scheduler publishes a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday, and one flight has its take-off typed but its landing box is blank or "TBD". It is rare, a few times a year. But each time it decides how much OIL a man gets.

The example

Sat 18 Jul. Hunter is in VL's front seat. Take-off is 10:00. Nobody types a landing. The wave's in-time is 09:45. The day is signed and published.

What the app does today

It assumes a landing time. It adds the Logic page's flight length (1 hour) to the take-off, so 11:00, then the 2-hour debrief, so 13:00. Hunter is credited half a day, AM — worked 09:45–13:00. No landing was ever typed.

One more effect. If an admin later changes the flight length on the Logic page, that published Saturday reads "1 pending" and its four sign-off names clear, because its OIL would now work out differently.

Why this is a question

You were asked about the assumed landing for warnings, and then for Insights and the long day. You said yes to both. OIL was never asked. The builder carried the assumed landing across to OIL without checking.

Your earlier rulings point the other way. A seat with no times gets no OIL, because the credit would be invented and not earned. A ground row with a start and no end is counted over a assumed hour but earns nobody OIL. The Logic page itself shows the split: a flight earns to the assumed landing, a sim with no end earns nothing.

The choice

  • No OIL until the landing is typed. The day says which line has no landing. The scheduler types it and publishes again, and the OIL is then worked out from real times.
  • Keep it as built. The flight earns up to the assumed landing. Nothing changes in the app.

Recommended

No OIL until the landing is typed. It matches what you ruled for every other row with a missing time, so OIL is never worked out from an assumed time. The fix for the scheduler is one box and a publish. The assumed landing stays for warnings, Insights and the long day, as you answered.

Question 2 · OIL

A man who is only on standby: should ALL AVAIL count him?

When it happens

A weekend or holiday has a standby crew (SC SPARE, AVALON or BB), and a brief or event for ALL AVAIL falls inside the standby hours. That is a few times a month. On a weekday the same thing happens, but there it only changes the count, not anyone's OIL.

The example

Sat 18 Jul. Hex is on SC SPARE, 07:00 to 13:00. An OPS BRIEF for ALL AVAIL runs 09:00 to 10:00, and its OIL question was answered yes, half a day.

What the app does today

Two screens disagree about Hex. The crew list shows him free for the brief. But ALL AVAIL treats his standby as "busy" and leaves him out, so he earns no OIL from a brief he would attend.

Left: the crew list says free. Picked for the OPS BRIEF, Hex is in full colour under "WSOS · 17 FREE". Right: ALL AVAIL leaves him out. The same brief's window reads "Who's available 44" with "WSOS · 16". Hex is not among them.

Why this is a question

You have said three times that a standby man is free: he "should not be faded as they are available to be planned", "they can be doing other things during standby", and "for planning they can be planned for other thing". Each time it was about the crew list or the warnings. Nobody asked you about the ALL AVAIL count, and it was left on the old rule.

The choice

  • Count him in. A man who is only standing by is in the ALL AVAIL count and its window, and earns with the others.
  • Keep him out. As today. Standby counts as "busy" for ALL AVAIL only.

Recommended

Count him in. ALL AVAIL means "who has nothing else on at that time", and by your own words standing by is not something else. The scheduler can still take any one man out by a tap in OIL Earn.

The cost: a weekend already published with a standby crew and an ALL AVAIL item will read pending until it is published again.

Question 3 · the published schedule

A SANS man with nothing filed, put in a jet or sim: should the warning list say so?

When it happens

About monthly. A scheduler copies last week's waves forward and a SANS man comes across in a seat. Or he drags a SANS man onto a seat without picking the seat first. Or the man deletes his own filing after he was planned.

The example

Tue 14 Jul. Jester is SANS and has filed nothing for that day. The scheduler drags him into VL's front seat, take-off 14:40.

What the app does today

The crew list strikes Jester's name. That is the only sign. The warning list says nothing about him, and the day publishes.

Left: the crew list. Under "SANS AVAIL · 1 FREE", Jester is struck and dimmed. Right: the day goes out. Tuesday shows ORIG with its four names, and the note at the foot reads "Tuesday published and approved".

Compare a man who filed the wrong thing. If Jester had filed "OFT only", the list would speak up.

Why this is a question

The silence was a builder's choice in August, never put to you. Since then you ruled two things close to it. The man himself gets a reminder on his bell. And the crew list needs no extra words, because the strike and "0 free" already say it and a scheduler should not be told to file. Neither covers the warning list, which is the part that stays on the day and goes out with it.

The choice

  • One amber line on the warning list. A plain fact for the scheduler, such as "Jester has filed no SANS availability for Tue 14 Jul". It never tells him to file.
  • Keep it silent. As today. The struck name in the crew list is the only sign.

Recommended

One amber line, the same weight as "not offering Fly today". It would cover the same seats your reminder to the man covers: a flying seat or a sim. The exact words are drawn for you before anything is built.

The cost: a published day that has such a man on it will read pending until it is published again. The demo weeks will show the new line in a few places.

Question 4 · crew rest

A duty desk with a start and no end: should crew rest leave it out until the end is typed?

When it happens

A scheduler types a duty's START and forgets the END, or does not know it yet. That is a few times a month. It matters when the same man flies or reports early the next day.

The example

Fri 17 Jul. Ridge is on the SXO desk from 20:00. No end is typed. On Saturday he is on VL with a 07:00 in-time.

What the app does today

It assumes the duty ended one hour after it started, at 21:00, and works crew rest out from that.

That assumed end can be wrong both ways. It can show a breach that is smaller or larger than the real one. Worse, if the duty really ran to 02:00 and the man reports at 10:00, the app reads him clear from 09:00 when he is not.

Why this is a question

You have answered this same case three times: for a standby shift, for a desk made from an SC shift template, and for an AVALON or BB desk whose wave flies. Each time you chose "left out of crew rest until its END is typed". The builder applied that to those three only. An ordinary duty desk was never shown to you, so it still uses the assumed one hour.

The choice

  • Leave it out until the end is typed. One rule for every duty desk. In the example, Ridge's Friday desk raises no crew-rest warning on Saturday until someone types its END. The empty END box is the only sign.
  • Keep the assumed one hour. As today, for ordinary desks only.

Recommended

Leave it out until the end is typed, the same as your standby desks. One rule is easier to trust than two, and a crew-rest figure built on an hour nobody typed can read "clear" when it is not. The Logic page's crew-rest line would then say "a duty with no end time". Conflicts are not affected: a desk with no end still blocks its assumed hour for those, as your earlier answers kept it.
