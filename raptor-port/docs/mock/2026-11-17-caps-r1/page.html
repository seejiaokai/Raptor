What your squadron uses today, in words

From the example you sent (described only; its address and the names on it are not kept here). It is a web page titled "142 e-CAPS". At its top a link to an "Ops Limit" page and the line that it can be submitted from Saturday. Each emergency procedure is a heading in capitals (fire during start, abort, bleed air caution and others), and under it pairs of empty boxes, item and action, typed from memory, sometimes with fixed words between them such as "(if app)". At the foot: Check Answers, then a callsign box and Submit, which only work once the answers are right, and a link to check who has submitted.

The Ops Limit page is tables of numbers typed from memory: engine temperatures, RPM and oil pressure on the ground and in flight, landing gear and flap speeds, canopy, tyres, start times, fuel warnings, crosswind and negative g. It has the same Check Answers and Submit.

The "check submission" page lists two tables, CAPS and LIMITS, each a name and the date done, with a count of each. The CAPS dates all fall in this week; the LIMITS dates run back about three weeks. That is why Q3 asks whether Ops limits clears every week.

Q1 · What does the form hold?

Your squadron's two tests, kept in the app, each in a window laid out as your current page lays it out: CAPS and Ops limits side by side (1a), each procedure's heading (1b), and each step a pair of boxes typed from memory (1c). Check answers (1d) edges each wrong box red and right ones green, and says how many are left to fix (1g), without showing the right answer. Submit (1e) stays grey until every box is right, then records him as done this week.

An admin keeps the headings, the boxes and their right answers, so a changed procedure is changed once, for everyone.

Example. Ranger opens CAPS on Monday, fills in the boxes and presses Check answers. "Fuel master – on" edges red and the foot reads "2 answers to fix". He fixes both, Submit lights, he presses it, and the scheduler's list shows him "✓ Mon 13".

Recommended: as drawn. Other choice: only a "Done" tick each person presses, with no test in the app. Simpler to build, but the answers would stay on your current page.

Platform flag (D1551): see below. Checking the answers on the person's own phone means the right answers reach his phone, where someone who knows how could read them.

Q2 · Who must fill it in?

Every pilot and WSO on the squadron's Quals page, students included. Ground crew and guests are not asked. The scheduler's button opens the list you asked for: the week's figures (3a), then those flying this week first (3b), the ones not done at the top in amber, "Not done" (3c), then everyone else under "Not flying this week" (3d). Each row shows the date he did each test, and the check's switch (Q5).

Example (this demo week): 38 people fly. Four have not done CAPS (made up for the picture), so the first tile reads "34 of 38" and those four lead the list.

Recommended: as drawn. Other choice: only those flying this week must do it. Then the "Not flying" group would go.

Q3 · Does Ops limits clear every week too?

You ruled that the form clears every week. Your current page suggests CAPS does, but Ops limits may run longer: its dates go back about three weeks. So each test gets its own period, set on the Logic page in a new card (6a CAPS, 6b Ops limits), beside the switch for the schedule check (6c). A period is changed with Logic's own Edit, as every setting is.

Example. Ops limits set to every month: Basher did it on 7 Jul, so he shows "✓ 7 Jul" all of July and "Not done" from 1 Aug.

Recommended: CAPS every week (your ruling), Ops limits every month, both changeable. Please tell me how often Ops limits is done today.

On a phone

Q4 · Does a flyer who has not done it raise anything on the schedule?

Yes, in amber, the app's colour for "have a look". His puck in a flying seat wears the amber advisory ring (4a), the day's warning list gains a line, "CAPS not done", that reads on its own (4b), and the day's count rises by one (4c). Amber never stops a publish. The check is listed on the Logic page with the other checks, in a group of its own (6d), and is switched on or off there (6c).

Example (demo Thursday). Blade flies VL at 12:45 and has not submitted CAPS. The list reads "Blade — CAPS not done: this week's test not submitted, flies VL (T/O 12:45)". Once he submits, the line and the ring go by themselves.

Recommended: as drawn. Other choice: nothing on the schedule; the list behind the CAPS button is the only place it shows.

On a phone

Q5 · What does "toggle off the check" switch off?

Two readings of your words. One man for this week: the switch on his row (5a) turns off; his row reads "Not checked" (5b); he raises nothing on the schedule; next week it is on again by itself. The whole week: one switch at the top of the window (5c) turns the check off for everyone that week, for a stand-down or a holiday week.

Example. Relay did CAPS on paper while away. The scheduler switches off Relay's row; Relay's puck carries no amber ring this week.

Recommended: one man for this week. The whole week's switch can be added later if you want both.

On a phone

Q6 · Where does a person open it?

Beside Insights, because both are about the week. On a desktop a "CAPS" button in the top bar (2a); on a phone "CAPS and Ops limits" in the schedule's ⋯ menu (2b). The same button opens a member's own tests, and for a scheduler the list of who has done them. The bell tells each person when the week's test opens, and again before he flies if it is still not done; a tap opens the test.

Recommended: as drawn. Other choice: a page of its own among the page names (Edit Schedule … Admin). Easier to find, but one more name on a bar that is already full on a laptop.

Every place in the app this touches

  • The top bar and the ⋯ menus (Edit Schedule, View-only Sched, the Scheduler Board's own bar): the new button or item (Q6).
  • Warnings: a new amber check, its line in the day's list, the ring on the puck, the day's count, and the Legend's list of flags (Q4).
  • The Logic page: a settings card and a check of its own (Q3, Q4).
  • Quals: the list of who must do it comes from its pilots and WSOs (Q2).
  • Insights: its neighbour on the bar; its window's look is reused.
  • The bell: new lines, "this week's CAPS is open" and "not done, you fly tomorrow".
  • Publishing: nothing; an amber line never stops a publish. Whether it shows on a published day's face, live or frozen, is a question for the build round.
  • Undo: a switch turned off is a change Undo takes back. A submitted test is not undone.
  • The Leave War and Inputs: R4 reads leave and overseas duty to excuse a man by itself.
  • Admin: somewhere to keep the tests' headings, boxes and answers.
  • Two squadrons (next in your order): each squadron keeps its own tests; a man borrowed by the other squadron is checked by his own.
  • The database step: two new tables, the tests with their answers and the submissions, each submission carrying its week, so "cleared every week" deletes nothing.
  • Print and export: nothing.

Platform flag (D1551)

Checking answers safely may need the IT side.

On the Power Apps app on Dataverse, a check in the app itself means the right answers travel to each person's phone, where someone who knows how could read them. A check on the IT side's server, out of sight, needs something beyond the app, and the IT side allows no plug-ins (D1749). I am not sure whether one of their allowed tools could do it; it will be found out by asking the IT side in the database round. If not, the choice is the in-app check, which is what your current page does too.

Everything else here works on the platform: clearing every week needs no deletion, since each submission carries its week; the bell lines are inside the app.
