Design look, 13 October

Three things you asked for on one page: ways to redo the Leave War’s clash note on a phone, the shorter passing notes before and after, and a look over what was built on the night of 13 October against the design guide, with everything a keyboard cannot yet do. Nothing in the app has been changed. Tap any picture to see it full size.

How to read the pictures. Built is the app exactly as it stands, at your iPhone’s size (390 wide) or a desktop’s (1440 wide). Drawn is the same real screen with a proposal drawn onto it; nothing behind it works yet. The pictures were taken in a Windows browser, so the letters are slightly narrower than on your iPhone.

1 · Leave War, on a phoneThe “clashes with the schedule” note

Today

What you sent a picture of

What is wrongThe amber note sits inside the second row of buttons. On a phone that row never wraps, so the note gets whatever width the buttons leave: 100 of 390. Two clashes then stand 294 tall, the stage button is cut to “O..”, and the grid starts nearly halfway down the screen.

The wordsEach clash is one sentence of about 17 words: “Fable: weekend/PH work earns FO but 18 Jul 26 holds LL — decide the bid on the sheet”.

On a desktopThe same note is one slim line under the buttons and reads well. It stays as it is, as you ruled for the desktop’s top rows. The last picture shows it with the shorter words only, for you to take or leave.

The same in every option: only an admin sees it, as now. Each clash is said as who · the day · what clashes, then what to do at the right: “Fable · Sat 18 Jul · earns FO, holds LL — Decide the bid ›”. A tap on a clash takes you to that day (or to the Inputs page where the leave was filed there). With no clashes, nothing shows and the top is your two lines.

A

One slim line under the two rows, that opens

What you seeA full-width amber line under your two rows: “⚠ 2 clashes with the schedule · Show ▾”. A tap opens the list right there and pushes the grid down; “Hide ▴” closes it.

CostOne line (31 tall) while there are clashes. Open, each clash is one line, two where a long name and a long instruction meet. Past five clashes the list would scroll inside itself.

RecommendedIt can’t be missed, it takes the full width, the two rows above keep every word, and one tap reads everything. It is also the fewest new parts: one line and its list.

B

A count on the second row, that opens a list

What you seeAn amber “⚠ 2” button beside “Under 0 days”. A tap drops a list over the grid, exactly as the under-manned days do today.

CostNo height at all. But the second row has no room: the stage’s name gives way and reads “OPEN FOR BID…”. And a small amber “⚠ 2” is easier to miss than a line.

Good forKeeping every row of the grid in sight. It works the same way as the button next to it.

C

One line that names the first clash

What you seeThe same slim line as A, but it says the first clash outright and counts the rest: “⚠ Fable · Sat 18 Jul · earns FO, holds LL · +1 more ›”. A tap opens the rest.

CostThe same one line as A. With one clash it needs no tap at all. With a long callsign the line runs out of room and the clash would be cut or wrap to two lines.

Good forThe usual case of a single clash.

2 · The passing notesShorter, and wider on a phone

W

Half of “wordy” is the box, not the words

Found while measuringOn a phone every passing note is only half the screen wide (195 of 390). It is centred in a way that leaves it half the width to grow into. That is why your two notes ran five lines deep.

MeasuredThe unpublish note: five lines as built. The same words in a full-width box: three lines. The shorter words in the full-width box: two.

RecommendedDo both: the note takes the phone’s width less a small margin, and the words are shortened. The desktop’s note is already wide and stays as it is.

The same causeThe bar that stays after an unanswered OIL question is half-width for the same reason. It is drawn full-width in section 3.

The list. The app can show 276 passing notes; 99 run to nine words or more. These are the ones worth changing, each with its shorter wording. The rule followed: what happened, then the one thing you must know; the day named once; no warning dropped. Notes that already do that in few words are listed at the end as “leave”. The Leave War’s and the Tracker’s own notes are not in this list; they belong to the whole-app words job you set aside for later.

Two separate reads were made and then put together: mine, pressing every control and key in the built app, and a fresh reader given only the pictures, the design guide and the app’s own record. A third, automatic scan of the seven screen files for common design faults found nothing. On the usual ten usability measures the four surfaces together scored 27 of 40: the calendar is clean; the OIL question and its bar pull the figure down.

How heavy each finding is. 1 it misleads, hides the way back, or cannot be used. 2 it is inconsistent or cluttered. 3 polish. Each says what a person meets and the exact change proposed. “Put to you” marks a finding against something you approved from a picture or ruled: it is a question, not a fault.

OIL

The OIL question before a swap

The idea is right and could only belong to this app: nothing moves until the question is answered, and the two buttons say what each does. Its frame is what lets it down.

L12The two answers sit in a box inside a box

What you meet: inside the window an outlined box; inside that a darker strip with a line under it that stops halfway across; inside that the two answers, pushed in from the left. It reads as broken. Why: the answers’ row is wearing a style that belongs to the week strip in the top bar. Change: both go. The answers sit flat under the question, starting at its left edge. You asked the same of the medical clash window on 28 Aug: “remove the box around the buttons. It looks unclean”. Guide §3.

L22The ✕ follows the title instead of sitting in the corner

What you meet: “OIL — Tally, Range brief ✕”. The cross moves with the length of the name and the title, and is nowhere near the top-right corner where the window behind it has its own. Change: the ✕ at the far right of the top bar. The app’s other small question windows share the fault; one change puts them all right. Guide §0, the same thing in the same place.

L32About 70 words where about 40 do the job Put to you

What you meet: a two-line heading in capitals, the question, 35 words of small print and a hint by the buttons. The one thing that matters, that a half day can become a full day, is buried at the end. Change: the wording in the list under these pictures. “Deserve” is kept; it is your word for this question.

L42At a save, “Add” becomes “Save”, and “Cancel” does not say what it cancels Put to you

What you meet: you press “Add” on a new input. The question comes up offering “Cancel / Save”, with the form’s own “Cancel / Add” still in sight behind it. This “Cancel” only takes you back to the form, but it reads as if it might throw the input away. The swap’s version already does it properly (“Don’t swap / Swap”). Change: “Back / Add” for a new input, “Back / Save” for a change. Guide §1, one name for one thing.

L52A picked answer is as loud as the main button Put to you

What you meet: once “Yes — credit HO” is picked there are two solid cyan buttons in one small window, and the eye no longer knows which one to press. Everywhere else a picked choice is dark with a cyan outline (“No end”, “Month”). Change: the picked answer takes that outline look; “Swap” stays the one solid button. The day’s one list of OIL questions would take the same look, so a picked Yes or No reads the same in both. Guide §3, one main button a view.

Cal

The date calendar

It reads as made for this app, and nothing on it moves under the finger: the title has a fixed room, there are always six rows, and the month-and-year lists are exactly as tall as the dates they replace. The run of dates in Admin’s form teaches itself with no hint on screen. What is left:

L82A date that cannot be picked can still look alive

What you meet: in “Until”, dates before the start are faint, except two kinds. Today (the 13th) stays bright cyan on its disc, the liveliest thing in its row; and the Thursdays (the 1st and 8th) stay bold. A tap on any of them does nothing, with no word why. Change: a date that cannot be picked is faint and plain, whatever else it is. Today keeps its disc, so it still marks today.

L92On a phone the card does not say which date it is for Put to you

What you meet: the centred card covers the box that opened it and looks the same for every box. On the Tracker’s panel it covers three of five date boxes; in the marking pop-up it covers both “Done on” and “Failed on”. A finger that landed on the wrong box picks a date into the wrong place with nothing to warn you. On a desktop this cannot happen: the panel hangs under its own box. Change: on a phone only, one line of small capitals at the top of the card with the box’s own name (“FROM”, “UNTIL”, “DONE ON”). It is the same on every month, so nothing moves. Guide §3, nothing sits over what it is about.

L102Four keys are missing

Home and End, a year at a time, and the keyboard’s way into and out of the month-and-year lists: K1 to K4 in section 4.

L113The sentence under a run of dates is cyan, though it is not a button

“Fri 9 Oct 2026 to Wed 14 Oct 2026” in Admin reads as a link. Change: the ordinary white bold. Guide §4, cyan means press me or picked.

L123The faint dates are very faint Put to you

You do not pick them, but you read them to find your place in the week; outdoors on a phone the row reads as nearly empty. Change: one small step stronger, still plainly fainter than a weekend date.

L133In the month-and-year lists the keyboard’s place is shown by turning one word cyan

With the keyboard on the months, “October” is cyan and “2026” white. It reads as “October is chosen, 2026 is not”. Change: both stay white; the list that has the keyboard wears the app’s cyan ring. The title’s own ring is also drawn round the words only, not the empty room beside them.

Trk

The Tracker’s date boxes

The ✕ inside a filled box, where the arrow was, is the right answer: no extra box, no extra column, and it says what it does.

L142A future day looks pickable, is picked, and is then refused Put to you

What you meet: in “Done on”, “Failed on” and the two Last Flown boxes, tomorrow looks exactly like yesterday. You tap it, the calendar closes, the box goes back and a line says why (your ruling of 28 Sep). The calendar already has a look for “may not be picked”, but only for days that are too early. Change: on those four boxes the days after today are faint and take no tap. The refusal line stays as the safety net. Better never made than put right.

L152A date reads as a fact before anything is marked Put to you

What you meet: an event not yet done shows “Done on (when marked): Tue 13 Oct 2026”, and with Fails at 0 it shows “Failed on: Tue 13 Oct 2026” in full white. Both read as records. The second has no hint at all. Change: until a mark or a failure actually carries the day, the date in the box is drawn in the dim colour an empty box already uses; it turns white the moment a mark carries it. No word changes.

L163On a desktop the side panel’s boxes change width when filled Put to you

What you meet: a box grows when it gets a date and shrinks when the ✕ clears it, so the ✕ and the arrow are never where the last one was; the column of four fields has three widths. Change: one width for that column’s boxes, wide enough for a full date. No box gets smaller.

Considered and left
  • Six rows always, Monday first, no tick, Reset or Clear, the Today icon, the centred card, the small panel on a desktop. Ruled.
  • The small “›” beside the month as the only sign the title opens the lists, and no ✕ on the calendar card. Both are the iPhone’s own.
  • Two presses to answer the question (pick, then “Swap”). It is the app’s pattern for every forced choice, and the several-days question needs the second button.
  • “Yes — credit HO”, “No OIL”, the bar’s bare “OK”, its staying until tapped. Ruled.
  • After the flash, nothing marks the two pucks that were not swapped, and the bar names nobody. You ruled the flash and the bar; “Answer OIL” brings the question back, and it names them. Say if you want the bar to name them too.
  • Buttons under a finger’s size (the Tracker’s ✕, the board’s small buttons). Not a finding by measurement alone; no missed tap was shown.
  • The many greys, weights and corner sizes. The sleek-interface job’s, as pictures to you, not this look’s.

Next

The keys calendar step two must carry, written before it is built

Step two puts the one calendar into an input’s own window, the holiday form, the Leave War’s range pickers and “Jump to a date”. There the calendar sits inside its form instead of floating over it. Its keys:

  • One Tab stop for the dates. Tab moves on to the form’s next box; nothing is trapped, because nothing floats.
  • Arrows a day or a week · Page Up / Down a month · Shift + Page a year · Home / End the week’s ends.
  • Enter or Space picks. For a run of dates: the first Enter is the start, the second the end, a third starts over, as a tap does.
  • Escape with a run half-made drops the half; otherwise it closes the window or sheet the calendar is in. In “Jump to a date” Enter picks and jumps.
  • The month title: Enter opens the lists with the keyboard in the month list; Enter in a list returns to the dates of that month.
  • Today is the Today button, one Tab from the title. No extra key.
  • In a Leave War sheet, Tab stays inside the sheet, as the sheet already keeps it.

So step one and step two matchK1 to K4 above are the same keys. Building them once, in the calendar itself, gives them to every place it is used.
