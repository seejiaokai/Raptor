Design look, 13 October

Three things you asked for on one page: ways to redo the Leave War’s clash note on a phone, the shorter passing notes before and after, and a look over what was built on the night of 13 October against the design guide, with everything a keyboard cannot yet do. Nothing in the app has been changed. Tap any picture to see it full size.

How to read the pictures. Built is the app exactly as it stands, at your iPhone’s size (390 wide) or a desktop’s (1440 wide). Drawn is the same real screen with a proposal drawn onto it; nothing behind it works yet. The pictures were taken in a Windows browser, so the letters are slightly narrower than on your iPhone.

Changed since you first saw this pageYou chose the clash note’s option A and asked for it neater: it is redrawn at the top of section 1 with everything lined up. You said some shorter wordings had lost their key words: the list in section 2 and the OIL question’s words are redone so every key word stays. The ring round a picked date is the keyboard’s only; its caption now says so.

OIL

The OIL question before a swap

The idea is right and could only belong to this app: nothing moves until the question is answered, and the two buttons say what each does. Its frame is what lets it down.

L12The two answers sit in a box inside a box

What you meet: inside the window an outlined box; inside that a darker strip with a line under it that stops halfway across; inside that the two answers, pushed in from the left. It reads as broken. Why: the answers’ row is wearing a style that belongs to the week strip in the top bar. Change: both go. The answers sit flat under the question, starting at its left edge. You asked the same of the medical clash window on 28 Aug: “remove the box around the buttons. It looks unclean”. Guide §3.

L22The ✕ follows the title instead of sitting in the corner

What you meet: “OIL — Tally, Range brief ✕”. The cross moves with the length of the name and the title, and is nowhere near the top-right corner where the window behind it has its own. Change: the ✕ at the far right of the top bar. The app’s other small question windows share the fault; one change puts them all right. Guide §0, the same thing in the same place.

L32The reason is shouted in capitals over two lines Put to you

What you meet: “THIS INPUT FALLS ON A NON-WORKING DAY — 18 JUL (WEEKEND / PH)” as a heading in capitals, then the question. Change: the same words as one ordinary sentence leading into the question. The first redraft cut the wording to about 40 words; by your word of 13 Oct that was too summarised, so the words now stay and only the look changes (the list under these pictures).

L42At a save, “Add” becomes “Save”, and “Cancel” does not say what it cancels Put to you

What you meet: you press “Add” on a new input. The question comes up offering “Cancel / Save”, with the form’s own “Cancel / Add” still in sight behind it. This “Cancel” only takes you back to the form, but it reads as if it might throw the input away. The swap’s version already does it properly (“Don’t swap / Swap”). Change: “Back / Add” for a new input, “Back / Save” for a change. Guide §1, one name for one thing.

L52A picked answer is as loud as the main button Put to you

Everywhere else a picked choice is dark with a cyan outline (“No end”, “Month”). Change: the picked answer takes that outline look; “Swap” stays the one solid button. The day’s one list of OIL questions would take the same look, so a picked Yes or No reads the same in both. Guide §3, one main button a view.

L82A date that cannot be picked can still look alive

What you meet: in “Until”, dates before the start are faint, except two kinds. Today (the 13th) stays bright cyan on its disc, the liveliest thing in its row; and the Thursdays (the 1st and 8th) stay bold. A tap on any of them does nothing, with no word why. L92On a phone the card does not say which date it is for Put to you

Next

The keys calendar step two must carry, written before it is built

Step two puts the one calendar into an input’s own window, the holiday form, the Leave War’s range pickers and “Jump to a date”. There the calendar sits inside its form instead of floating over it. Its keys:

  • One Tab stop for the dates. Tab moves on to the form’s next box; nothing is trapped, because nothing floats.
  • Arrows a day or a week · Page Up / Down a month · Shift + Page a year · Home / End the week’s ends.
  • Enter or Space picks. For a run of dates: the first Enter is the start, the second the end, a third starts over, as a tap does.
  • Escape with a run half-made drops the half; otherwise it closes the window or sheet the calendar is in. In “Jump to a date” Enter picks and jumps.
  • The month title: Enter opens the lists with the keyboard in the month list; Enter in a list returns to the dates of that month.
  • Today is the Today button, one Tab from the title. No extra key.
  • In a Leave War sheet, Tab stays inside the sheet, as the sheet already keeps it.

So step one and step two matchK1 to K4 above are the same keys. Building them once, in the calendar itself, gives them to every place it is used.
