1 · Is the design good? My honest answer

Mostly yes. What you have ruled (D2088 to D2111) builds two squadrons out of things the app already has: the overseas duty input, the Attach row, the ORIG and DRAFT tags, the just-added ring and the question card that hangs from a button. That keeps it easy to learn, keeps it looking like one app, and leaves fewer new places for a bug. The weak spots are in the gaps between the rulings: moving from one schedule to another, where the other squadron's people show, and two buttons that act out of sight once two squadrons share a wave.

Is it easy to use?

Yes, for anyone who already uses the app: every step uses a screen he knows, and nothing has to be set up twice. Where it fails today is feedback. "+ Line" and "✕ Wave" can change things he cannot see, and a scheduler has no way yet to open the other squadron's schedule. Q2 to Q5 fix those.

What works

  • Every line has one owner, and that squadron signs it (D2106), so a change never has two amendments to go into.
  • Where a squadron is decides what it shares (D2092): at the same place, two squadrons can plan each other's people and their clashes are checked; at different places, never. Nobody has to tick a sharing setting.
  • A detachment uses the overseas duty input (D2101, D2102). A man away shows at home the way he always has, titled by the detachment.
  • Look 2 (D2104) marks the other squadron's line clearly: a grey badge and a lighter block, no new colour.
  • A pick onto a published day waits as a pending change (D2107), as every other late change does.

What is weak, and the fix for each

  • The place change has no start date and no owner shown. Fix: a master admin picks the place from a list, with a "From" date (Q1).
  • There is no way to move between schedules. Round 1 suggested a menu, but it was never put to you. Fix: the TALON mark as a menu (Q2).
  • The other squadron's people have no place to be picked from. D2091 and D2092 let a scheduler plan them, but no list shows them. Fix: their own group in each crew list (Q3).
  • "✕ Wave" throws away the other squadron's lines with no question. Fix: ask, only then (Q4).
  • "+ Line" adds lines out of sight. This is true today, before any squadron change. Fix: "+ Line" at the wave's foot (Q5).
  • Two rulings disagree about the other squadron's line in your wave (D2089, D2105). Fix: Q6.
  • Crew rest across two schedules has no rule yet when the two squadrons' settings differ. Fix: Q7.
  • D2105's "tick to show the other squadron's line" has no control drawn yet. Where it sits depends on Q2 and Q3, so it waits for them.

Against the design guide

I checked it myself, in one pass. The others it asks for were not run tonight, to save time. It passes on: built from parts you know; one mark with one look (no new colour, no glow); questions asked at the button you pressed (lesson 77); buttons keeping their size (D487). It fails on: showing what happened, where "+ Line" and "✕ Wave" act out of view (your ruling D850; fixed by Q5 and Q4).

2 · Walkthrough: putting 149 in 142's home

Q1 · Does moving 149's place take a start date?

Who does it: a master admin, since only a master admin looks after squadrons (D2093). He opens Admin → Squadrons (D2110) and taps 149's row (1a). Place (1b) is picked from the places list (1e), never typed, so two squadrons can only match exactly. From (1c) is the day the move starts. One line (1d) says what Save will do: "From 1 Dec, 142 and 149 can plan each other's people, and clashes between the two are checked. Both squadrons' schedulers are told." My recommendation is that both squadrons' admins and schedulers get a bell line when it is saved.

Example. On 20 Nov the master admin sets 149 to Home base from 1 Dec. Until 30 Nov nothing changes on either schedule. From 1 Dec, 142's crew lists show 149's free people, and a 142 man double-booked by 149 is flagged on both schedules.

Recommended: a "From" date, as drawn. Other choice: the move takes effect the moment it is saved. That is simpler, but a move planned in advance could not be set up early.

On a phone

Q3 · Where do the other squadron's free people show?

Two crew lists need them: Edit Schedule's Available crew, and the Scheduler Board's own list, where a 142 scheduler joining 149's wave picks his crew. In both, 149's people come last, in their own group, so 142's own people stay first. Each one shows when he is free, so nobody drags a man into a wave he cannot fly.

  • Edit Schedule (2a): grouped by time as the list already groups 142's: "149 · 2nd wave · night 16:20–end · 1 can fly" (Jolt, busy with 149 in the morning), and "149 · available all day · 2" (Mako, Brick).
  • The Scheduler Board (2b): under each column, "149 · 1 free". A man free for only part of the day says when, in the list's own small line: Jolt "free from 11:30".

Dragging one onto a seat is a pick (D2091): his home schedule then shows "Attach · 142" for him (D2098).

Recommended: as drawn. Other choice: 149's people mixed into 142's groups, each with a small badge. That makes one list, but a busier one.

On a phone

Q2 · How does a scheduler move between schedules?

Today "142 TALON" is a title; it was named so when the app became TALON (D1606). Proposed, the whole TALON mark becomes a button (3a), its small number reading "149 ▾", and it opens "Schedules" (3b). A person sees only the schedules he may see, so a member with one schedule sees no ▾, as today.

Which pages follow the choice: the schedule pages (Edit Schedule, View-only Sched, the Scheduler Board) and Logic, since each schedule has its own settings (D2109). Inputs, Quals, the Leave War and the Tracker stay on his own squadron's people, because the people stay his squadron's wherever they fly.

Example (your own, D2088: 149 opens detachment 249). A 149 scheduler at home taps "149 ▾", picks "249 · Exercise North", and Edit Schedule now shows the detachment's week. He opens Inputs: it still lists 149's people, the detachment's men among them. He picks "149" to go back.

Recommended: as drawn.

The fields are: Name "Exercise North"; Short name "249" (4a), which is what the top bar and the Schedules menu show, so a long name never breaks the phone's top bar; Place, from the list (4b); From; and To (4d). Q6 · May 149's scheduler change 142's line inside 149's own wave?

Two of your words point different ways. D2089 (16 Nov, your lean): a 142 formation in 149's wave "may be planned by either squadron's scheduler". D2105 (17 Nov, the later one, ruled): "a shown line of the other squadron is read only there". D2105 was asked about lines a scheduler ticks to show for reference, so I cannot tell whether it was meant to cover a line inside a wave 149 owns and signs (D2106).

Example. 142's RU is in 149's 1st wave. 149's scheduler sees RU's take-off clash with VL's area time and wants RU 10 minutes later. Under D2089 he moves it, and 142 is told. Under D2105 he cannot, and has to ask 142's scheduler to change it.

Recommended: in a wave 149 owns, 149 may change 142's line, and 142's schedulers get a bell line. 149 signs that wave anyway (D2106). Read only stays for a line shown only for reference from a wave you do not own (D2105). Other choice: the other squadron's line is read only everywhere; the owning squadron asks.

Step 4 · Who signs (as ruled, D2106; round 2's pictures)

149 owns the wave, so 149's four sign it, 142's line included. 142 never signs it. If 149 publishes first, 142's view of the wave reads "149 · ORIG" (round 2's marks 10a, 10b). If 142 publishes its own day first, the wave still reads "149 · DRAFT" until 149 publishes (marks 11a, 11b).

Q4 · Should "✕ Wave" ask first when the wave holds the other squadron's lines?

Today "✕ Wave" removes the wave at once, every line in it. On 149's board that would throw away 142's RU without 142 knowing. Proposed, only when the wave holds the other squadron's lines, the question card hangs from the button (13): "Remove 1st wave? It holds 142's RU. 142's schedulers are told. Undo puts it back." The answers are Remove wave, or Keep (the safe one). A wave with only 149's own lines is removed as today.

Recommended: as drawn. Other choice: 149 may not remove such a wave until 142 takes its lines out. That is safer, but it blocks 149 on a busy day.

On a phone

Q7 · When two squadrons' crew-rest settings differ, which one judges?

D2109 says a line is judged by the Logic settings of the schedule it sits in. Crew rest is measured between two lines, and those can sit in two schedules.

Example (made-up settings). Say 149's crew rest is 12 hours and 142's is 10. Saber, a 142 man, flies 149's night wave and his day ends at 23:10 on 149's schedule. Next morning 142 plans him with a 10:00 report. By 142's 10 hours he is clear from 09:10, so there is no flag. By 149's 12 hours he is not clear until 11:10, so it is a breach.

Recommended: the settings of the later line's schedule judge the gap. Here that is 142's, the squadron planning the next day, which follows D2109. Other choice: the stricter of the two always judges. That is safer, but 142 would be held to a rule it never set.

5 · Q5 · Adding several lines quickly

First, your "click twice" question. Round 2's other choice was a card on 149's "+ Line" asking "Add a line for 149 · 142", which would have meant two presses for every line. You turned it down (D2106). 149's "+ Line" stays one press, and 142 adds its lines by joining through "+ Wave" (step 1 above). So no line now takes two presses.

Your stay-open idea is about adding several lines quickly, and your worry is right. It is true today, before any squadron change. "+ Line" sits in the wave's head and each line is added at the wave's foot. The screen does not move, and the passing note says "Line added" each time with no count. I pressed it three times on the real app. On a desktop and on a phone alike, none of the three new lines was wholly on screen. A scheduler who presses again because nothing seemed to happen ends up with lines he never saw.

A card that stays open fixes nothing about where the lines land: they still land out of view, so the card has to say where they went. Below, today beside three ways. Each keeps buttons at their size, closes on a click outside, uses the plain blue ring, and asks at the button (lesson 77).

Recommended: A. The same "+ Line" button, the same size, moves from the wave's head to its foot, under the last line. The board keeps it under your finger, so each press adds a line just above it, in view, in the just-added blue ring. You see every line as it is made, and the note is no longer needed. Its cost: to add a line to a long wave you scroll to its foot first.

The same everywhere (D754): the board's other add buttons ("+ Row", "+ Item", "+ Note", "+ Block") can add out of sight in the same way. My recommendation is that the same rule applies to each one whose list can run below the screen, the new thing always appearing in view. If you take A, I will draw those in the next round before anything is built.

6 · Does it affect other parts of the app?

Yes, in these places. With one squadron, each of them works exactly as it does now. Where a point is my own reading, not your ruling, it says so.

  • The day's count. A schedule that shows the other squadron's lines counts them apart, "2 (142) 6 (149)", never one figure (D2096). Round 2 drew it.
  • ALL AVAIL. Your lean (D2097): two squadrons' ALL AVAIL events at the same time raise no clash, and the people decide. Recommended in round 2: each squadron's count holds only its own people, so a man never earns OIL twice for one afternoon.
  • OIL. My reading: a man flying a 149 line earns OIL on his own OIL, and it arrives when 149 publishes, since the latest published version decides OIL.
  • The Leave War. A man on a detachment is already counted away, through his overseas duty input. Still open: whether the "Attach · 149" row (D2098) counts him away on the Leave War for those hours. My recommendation is no, since he is at work at home; it goes to you in a later round.
  • The Tracker. A student flying a sortie on 149's schedule: my reading is that the sortie counts on his own Tracker, as it would on 142's. That is not yet asked; it comes in the Tracker's round.
  • Insights. A man's own hours follow him across both schedules. Each squadron's totals count only its own lines.
  • Inputs. The overseas duty input gains the Detachment choice (D2101).
  • Warnings. At the same place, people, areas and sims are checked across both squadrons, and jets are each squadron's own (D2092, D2108). Crew rest across two schedules is Q7.
  • Publishing and amendments. Each squadron signs its own waves (D2106). A pick onto a published day waits as a pending change (D2107).
  • Undo. Undo already refuses to undo a change someone else has changed since (D148). That now includes a change by the other squadron.
  • The bell. It needs new lines: a place change (Q1), a pick of your man (D2091), a detachment opened, due to end or closed (R1, R3), a shared wave removed (Q4), and your line changed by the other squadron (Q6).
  • Admin and sign-up. A new Squadrons page (D2110). "Request access" asks which squadron (D2111).
  • Print and export. The lighter block does not show on paper, so a printed 149 day must still show the "142" badge.
  • When the shared database comes. Everything saved must say which squadron it belongs to. The mark that shows who is editing a day must also cover a wave both squadrons fly in, so two schedulers never change it at once.
