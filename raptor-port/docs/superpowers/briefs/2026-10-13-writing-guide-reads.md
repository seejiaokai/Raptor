## Where the two agree (do these)

1. **§1a test 9 lets the writer be the cold reader** — the arrangement that failed on 13 Oct. *Opus:* "Each new wording
   is given ALONE to a fresh reader — a helper started for it, never the chat that wrote it — who writes down: what
   happened, why, what does it cost, where do I put it right, and can any part be read two ways? The writer sets those
   answers beside the original's; one that differs, or a second reading, is a lost fact and the wording goes back."
   *Fable adds:* the reader "then restates every claim of the note in their own words, each named thing and number
   included; the restatement is compared with the original claim by claim" — because the four questions never ask about
   a second clause ("anything added later"), so the reversal of 13 Oct could pass them. And the example should say
   "could be read as the opposite", as the record does.
2. **§1a test 10 overrides D825 and §0** ("left as it is — and made easier to READ instead" re-words unasked). *Fable:*
   "— and, where it is still hard to read, the same words split into two sentences or a headline with its detail under
   it, no word lost, is offered to him as a change of its own." *Opus:* the same, "offered to him as a picture (D541),
   never done unasked".
3. **The short form in `.claude/rules/ui-design.md` omits measuring the box** (the cheapest fix, the step most likely
   skipped) and lets the writer read cold. *Fable's wording:* "**Shortening is not summarising (D825, D832):** the box
   is measured before the words; only padding, a thing said twice, an announcement, an intensifier or a hedge may be
   cut; the note stays a sentence, each clause with its verb; each change shows the exact words cut and is read cold
   by someone who has not seen the original; a note that cannot lose a word is left whole — the guide's §1a."
   (*Opus's* is the same with "cut only a word the note says the same facts without" in place of the list — see the
   disagreement below.)
4. **§8's new line is a slogan** — note 580 asked for a count checked by a script. *Opus:* insert "the report states
   both counts — the record's gaps and the page's —". *Fable:* append "; the record's gap count and the page's are
   compared by a script, never by eye."

## Where they differ — the next draft must settle it

**Test 2, "only four kinds of word may be cut".**
- **Opus (a BLOCKER in its report): the closed list forbids cuts he approved the same day (D829).** Applied literally
  to the approved list: N19 "so it has been cleared" → "— cleared"; N10 drops "this item is"; N2 "it is"; N7 "it
  was … and"; N14 / N16 "now"; N11 "underneath"; N1 drops "so", "the" and "13 Jul" — none is one of the four kinds; test
  4 names "the … is" as staying; the protected list says "every number". D825's own reading (2) calls "it has been"
  filler and gives the real test — "the sentence says the same without it". So next month the agent leaves nearly
  every note whole, or reports approved notes as breaches. *Its replacement:* "**A word is cut only if the note says
  the same facts without it (D825).** Four kinds nearly always pass: [the four]. A fifth passes only by proof — a
  helper word round a fact ("it is", "it has been", "now", a "so" where a dash or colon keeps the link) — when the cold
  read (9) gives the same answers. The worked standard is the list he approved (D829). Anything else is content and
  stays." Kind two becomes "a thing said twice in the same note (the day named once, D806 — if the dropped mention
  carried more than the kept one, a date, 'show the cut' says so)". *Test 4 becomes:* "**The fact keeps its subject and
  its verb.** Never telegram style: "no times — no OIL from it" lost what had no times and who earns nothing. A helper
  word after the fact may go ("so it has been cleared" → "— cleared", D829); the thing the note is about, and what it
  did, may not." Test 7 ("your", "we") is already a fifth kind. Test 8 ends "each with which kind it is. A cut that fits
  no kind is not made."
- **Fable keeps the closed list and tightens it:** kind one gains "— a phrase that carries a clause's verb ('is
  covered', 'will not earn') is never padding", because the writer of 13 Oct could have classed "is covered" as padding
  and passed every test.
- **The host's note for the next draft:** both are right about different failures — the closed list is too tight for
  the cuts he approved, and an open test is what let the reversal through. Opus's test with Fable's verb rule inside
  it, and the fresh reader of finding 1 as the proof, covers both. **And D833 changes the ground under both:** with
  "more human, fewer dashes" the approved wordings are rechecked anyway — "— cleared" may become "and was cleared".

- **Opus — place:** test 6 (a word that is both a thing and an action) applies to NEW labels too; move it to §1 as a

- **`plain-language.md` check 4.** *Fable — two faults:* "never a name" collides with check 1, which DELETES
