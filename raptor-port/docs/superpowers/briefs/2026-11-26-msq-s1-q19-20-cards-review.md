## 1. The cold read — before any rule was opened

**Card 19, what I took it to ask.** A calendar is left half done on the Tracker and then hidden when the browser
window is made narrow. A squadron pick is refused. The note says "close the open window on the Tracker", but the man
is already on the Tracker and can see no window. Should the note say "on the Tracker’s Info tab" instead? Recommended:
yes.

Where I tripped:
- **The title says "you", the body says "he".** "A Tracker window you can no longer see" made me think it was about
  him at his own desk. The story is about an admin.
- **"Info panel" in the first sentence, "Info tab" three lines later.** I took them for two places until I worked out
  that the panel becomes a tab when the window is narrow.
- **"The Tracker folds into its tabs."** I did not know what "folds" meant until I saw the picture.
- **"Behind the Info tab."** I first pictured something lying under the tab's button. It means "on the Info tab, which
  is not the one showing".
- **"The calendar's grey cover blocks every other button until it is closed."** "It" could be the cover or the
  calendar, and the sentence does not say how it gets closed.
- **"149" is never introduced.** It is the squadron he picked. Nothing says so.
- **"The pick is refused."** Reads as what the app does today. I could not tell from the card whether I could try it.
- **In the first picture two dates stand out,** the 1st (filled) and the 26th (ringed). I did not know which was "the
  first day picked" until I read the line "Start 01/11/26" in the picture.

**Card 20, what I took it to ask.** On Admin, something is started on Users or on Squadrons and not saved, and the
admin clicks the other of the two. A squadron pick is refused. For one case (a person's changed row) the note already
names the section, by his ruling. For four more cases it does not. Should those four name the section too? Recommended:
yes, only while the section is out of sight.

Where I tripped:
- **"Admin's other section" in the title.** Other than which? It only makes sense after the first paragraph.
- **"Give access with its role changed."** Read as an instruction, or as if "Give access" were somebody. It is a form.
- **"A squadron's row with its place changed."** For a moment "place" read as its position in the list.
- **"The plain words."** Used three times and never said to mean "the words as built, with no section named".
- **"Only while that section is not the one showing; when he can see the unfinished thing the plain words stay."** A
  sentence with no verb, then a second thought hung on a semicolon. I read it twice.
- **"The letters showing just right of the shorter notes."** I looked for letters in every picture. There is one
  letter, an "n", in one picture.
- **The fourth recommended picture's Users button reads "1 waiting for access"** where the others read "Sign-in and
  roster". Nothing says why.
- **How do I tell which section is "showing" in a picture?** By the blue edge on its button, which no caption says.

## 2. The five wordings he has not seen

Each one's ruled part is left word for word. Only the added place words were weighed.

| # | The wording | Verdict | Why, in one sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Blocked: close the open window on the Tracker’s Info tab, then pick 149." | **Keep** | "Info" is the word printed on the tab he can see beside "Flow chart", so the note points at something on screen, and "the Tracker’s" still tells him where to go if he has since left the Tracker. |
| 2 | "Blocked: save or cancel the new squadron on Squadrons, then pick 149." | **Keep** (a second wording below, not recommended) | It doubles a word, but the place's only name on screen is "Squadrons", and it matches his ruled "…the open row on Users…" exactly, which matters more in a family of five notes. |
| 3 | "Blocked: save or cancel the open row on Squadrons, then pick 149." | **Keep** | It is his ruled Users wording with the other section's name in the same place, so the pair reads as a pair. |
| 4 | "Blocked: add or cancel the new person on Users, then pick 149." | **Keep** | Read cold it gives what was refused, what is unfinished, where it is and both ways out, and "Users" is the name on the button under the note. |
| 5 | "Blocked: finish or cancel giving access on Users, then pick 149." | **Keep** | Same shape as the other four, and the Users button beside it reads "1 waiting for access", which settles what "giving access" is. |

**Second readings found in 4 and 5, each settled by the screen (recorded as §1a test 9 asks):**
- 4: "add … the new person on Users" can be read as "add him onto Users" as well as "the new person, which is on
  Users". Both send him to Users to press Add or Cancel, so nothing is lost.
- 5: "giving access on Users" can be read for a moment as a kind of access ("access on Users"). The Users button's
  "1 waiting for access" and the form's own name settle it.

**The doubled word in 2 — a second wording, in hand only if he remarks on it.**
"Blocked: save or cancel the new squadron under Squadrons, then pick 149."
- What it gains: "under Squadrons" reads a little more clearly as "under the Squadrons heading", so the two
  "squadron" words are easier to tell apart.
- What it costs: one note of the five would say "under" where his ruled one says "on". The doubled word stays either
  way, because the section has no other name on screen.
- My recommendation: do not put it on the card. The card asks one thing (name the section or not), and a choice about
  one small word inside one of four notes would make it two. If he says the doubled word bothers him, this is the
  wording to offer.

No wording was adjusted.
