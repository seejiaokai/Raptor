## Where they differ — and how it was settled

**A note that opens with a word or two and a dash** ("Info only — …", "Warning hidden — …", "OIL mode — …").
*Fable:* the opening is the sentence of one done thing; the dash is a full stop, never a colon. *Opus (reading D834's
text):* "take the first that fits" puts the full stop before the colon, so the colon and the dash are never reached;
reorder — the link, then a colon where the first part only names or announces, then a comma for a renaming, then a full
stop, then a dash. **Settled by his own D834 ("Maybe : might be used") with a line an agent can apply without
judgement:** a colon where the first part is only a NAME (a mode, a state, a switch) or the second part is not a
sentence of its own and only spells the first out; a full stop where both parts are sentences. So "Info only: this item
is no longer checked…" and "Line is SPARE: standing by, …", but "Monday published. The Common Programme has no usable
times, so …".

**The date in N1.** *Fable:* it may go where the screen under the note already shows it, shown as a cut. *Opus:* it
moves to the kept mention, so no number is lost. **Taken: Opus's**, the stricter and simpler.

would a person say it. **What it changed:**

  change just made; the helper asked for "now" in both. D829 had cut it as a helper word; by the fifth kind's own proof

- **"cleared" is a flying word.** "…so it was cleared" can read as "approved", as in cleared for take-off → "removed".
- **"Info only:" can read as "for your information"** → the subject first: "This item is info only: …".

- **Found in wordings nobody had touched** (the same in the app today), each put to him as a small change of its own:
  "Publish the day before publishing its changes" reads as "publish Sunday" · "take a man off that event" reads as
  taking him off the schedule · "are bid against" is not something anyone says · "It returns if the day changes" reads
