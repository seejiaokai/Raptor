Both plans would likely catch the original SANS case through comparison with the crew list and warning. Neither yet reliably catches the next case.

**Plan 1 — method fix**

- **Must-fix — permits building before settling meaning.** C2 says: “Build the reading the app already uses for the same question”. The existing builder policy says: “When a material requirement is missing, contradictory, or conflicts with a documented invariant: stop the affected work”. SANS already had two competing meanings; the new instruction could justify choosing either and building first. **Fix:** preserve the stop for product choices; use neighbouring behaviour to frame the question, not to authorize the answer.

- **Must-fix — future drawings and wording remain uncovered.** C8 leaves their treatment “for the audit’s plan to say”, but that plan expressly “does not touch the working guides”. An interpretation introduced in a drawing or message can therefore become approved wording before any builder records it. **Fix:** put the common interpretation check in one place, with pointers from design, wording review and building; require the worked example before approval.

- **Should-fix — the automatic check cannot substantiate its promise.** C7 checks only “a `READING D<n>` tag whose row carries no mark”. The original “his to change” comment would pass untouched. An unnoticed interpretation with no neighbouring surface also escapes C4. **Fix:** describe this as a consistency check for recorded interpretations; require readers independently to identify meanings unsupported by the ruling, including untagged ones. Test it against the original comment, not just newly compliant tags.

- **Minor — avoid overlapping instructions.** C3 adds “Every brief … lists each reading not asked”, while the existing rule already says “A plan’s brief lists the builder’s own readings as targets”. Leaving both creates two overlapping lists to maintain, against D2460’s “each repeated rule stated once”. **Fix:** extend the existing instruction and use pointers elsewhere. The unchanged always-loaded files are otherwise a sensible restraint on bulk.

**Plan 2 — audit**

The breadth is justified, but the exclusions need tightening. Counts are plausible: I found **40** “his to change” lines across **27 files**, **11** outside tests; **1,012** “agent’s reading” occurrences and **314** “told to him” occurrences. These are occurrences, not independently classified interpretations.

- **Must-fix — disclosure becomes approval.** §3 says “A reading told is not a lapse” and “MATCH … (a told reading counts…)”. A question shown but unanswered would disappear as MATCH. Row-wide tagging also risks treating every interpretation as disclosed: D2338 says other readings were told, while “planned” was not. **Fix:** classify each interpretation separately; distinguish disclosed, awaiting answer, approved and contradicted. Require evidence of the answer before treating a product choice as settled.

- **Must-fix — engine differences lose finding status.** §5 says an engine difference is “a note for the host, never as a mismatch to fix in place”. That can remove a wrong scheduling result from the reviewed findings and owner’s rounds. The porting constraint limits edits, not detection. **Fix:** retain the mismatch and its harm in the findings; separately prohibit unauthorized engine changes.

- **Should-fix — rounds are undersized and exceed the existing cap.** “Rounds of 3-5 questions” conflicts with the design guide’s “rounds of at most four”. Four to eight rounds accommodate only 16–32 decisions, against 60–150 expected findings before consolidation. **Fix:** cap rounds at four, estimate rounds after grouping, and explicitly track every deferred or uncertain item rather than implying the stated rounds finish the audit.
