**Verdict: sound with changes.** One row for one genuinely shared input is clearer and fits a scheduler working mainly at a desk. It removes the misleading suggestion that each man’s row is independently editable. But the plan is not ready to build: it understates the rules it changes and leaves several consequential actions undefined.

This was an independent, read-only plan review. I read the plan, relevant rulings and backlog, and checked selected current behaviour in the code. I did not open the prohibited review files or rerun the survey in the app.

**What is missing or wrong**

1. **One man coming back early needs a complete, usable route.**  
   Ranger and Hex are on overseas duty Monday–Friday. Ranger returns Wednesday. Taking Ranger off Thursday’s combined row removes him from the input for **every day**, including Monday–Wednesday. Filing him again carelessly could lose his earlier absence, OIL answer or supporting documents.

   The plan acknowledges separate treatment, but “take him off and file his own” is not a sufficient description of the operation. Specify how the scheduler preserves his original days and changes only his return. My recommendation is an explicit action for separating one named person, keeping the others unchanged, completed in one save and one Undo. It must be deliberate, not an automatic split while typing. The existing manual route can remain if it is demonstrated end to end.

   Do not rely generally on “do it in the Leave War”: full editing there remains an open job under D1724.

2. **“Every leave kind” misses an existing distinction.**  
   An admin can file shared leave through Inputs today. However, leave approved through the Leave War is restricted to one person; adding a second person is expressly refused. Its approval, return-to-bid, move and deletion depend on that restriction.

   Otherwise, a scheduler sees two apparently identical leave rows, but only one accepts another man—or the build removes the restriction and damages the Leave War’s handling.

   State explicitly: shared leave filed through Inputs is covered; Leave War-approved leave remains individual unless redesigning that approval relationship becomes part of this job. Never combine independently filed leave merely because its kind and dates match.

3. **The proposed drop rule differs from Ground Programme today.**  
   Today, dropping onto a named puck in a shared Ground Programme input **adds** the newcomer; only dropping onto its sole person replaces him. The proposal makes any named puck a replacement target.

   There is also a source-side difference. Dragging from a flying seat onto Unavailable currently leaves the original seat occupied. Some replacements onto Ground Programme instead swap the displaced person into the source seat.

   Example: dragging Hex from a flight onto Ranger’s absence must not quietly put Ranger into Hex’s cockpit. The plan needs a table covering drops from the crew list, another assigned seat, another input, and another copy of the same input. State what happens at **both ends**. Include duplicate names, dropping back onto oneself, refusal, and removing the last person. Apply the chosen rule to Ground, Common, Personal Inputs and Unavailable together.

4. **Personal Inputs is changing its role during a drag.**  
   The survey establishes more than a missing add target: dropping there currently can unassign the dragged person from his existing row.

   A scheduler using that familiar removal gesture could instead add him to a rejected meeting. Conversely, an attempted addition that misses its target could remove him from his flight.

   Specify separate targets for a named input and for unassigning. The visible landing indication must describe the actual result. A refused input drop must leave the source untouched. Cover the folded panel, its heading, empty space and rejected rows. Adding someone to a rejected input must not silently accept it.

5. **ALL and ALL AVAIL cannot follow the named-person rule literally.**  
   They represent a changing crowd, not one replaceable man. They remain limited to eligible kinds and single-day inputs; an input cannot hold both, and neither belongs under Unavailable.

   There is a further existing behaviour to resolve: adding a permitted placeholder through an input’s window can move the entire input out of Unavailable and onto the Common Programme. The plan does not say whether the new drop does that or refuses.

   Preserve the placeholder restrictions and its continued presence when a named person leaves. Distinguish removing a named entry from excluding someone represented by ALL AVAIL: removing his named puck does not necessarily stop the crowd representing him. Keep the crowd’s separate removal rules separate.

6. **A member withdrawing, or the filer leaving, needs explicit consequences.**  
   Ranger files a group commitment; Hex takes himself out on his phone. Only Hex should leave, across the input’s complete date range. The others’ dates, remarks, OIL answer and acceptance must remain unchanged.

   The plan’s permission summary also omits an existing qualification: once a scheduler has added others, the original member filer does not retain unrestricted control of the whole input. State that qualification, what happens when the filer withdraws, and who receives any unanswered OIL question. Do not let a simpler-looking row weaken those rules.

7. **Posting out, moving squadron and borrowing are not interchangeable.**  
   Suppose Ranger moves squadron halfway through shared leave. Removing him from the whole input would erase his earlier part; letting the old squadron’s scheduler edit everyone could exceed that scheduler’s authority.

   D2261 already requires leave spanning a squadron move to remain one input, editable or removable only by the man himself or an admin of both squadrons. With shared leave, his own action must still affect only his part. The plan needs an explicit account of this combination, including what remains visible on each day.

   Borrowing is different again. A scheduler being allowed to plan another squadron’s man does not automatically grant authority over that man’s home leave or medical input. A hand-filed Attach and an automatic row showing work for another squadron are also different things. Preserve that distinction and the ruled notifications when a home absence affects a borrowed assignment. Some of this is future borrowing work; name the dependency rather than claiming it already works.

8. **Published-day counts are necessary, but not sufficient.**  
   A scheduler removes Hex from a Monday–Friday input while viewing Wednesday. Monday and Tuesday may already be published, and Friday may not be loaded.

   Specify that every affected day is considered. Published copies keep their issued people and details; affected working days become pending; sign-offs clear where required; OIL changes only through publication. Showing one combined row must not make an old issued version read today’s membership.

   Also define the result when the Leave War separates one man after publication: unchanged people must not appear to have been deleted and re-filed merely because one input became two. Counts, change descriptions and history must agree. Undo must restore the whole operation and refuse to overwrite someone else’s later change to the same input.

9. **Reject, “→ Ground” and deletion need distinct rules.**  
   Rejecting a shared course or overseas duty takes the whole input out of Unavailable and counts its people back into Leave War manning. Moving it onto Ground keeps them counted in. Removing one person changes membership; deleting removes the input itself.

   The proposal’s blanket “one Reject button” must not imply that leave and medical acquire Reject or Ground actions. They do not share the commitment rules. Add a short table by kind and state, including reaccepting, cancelled inputs and information-only inputs. D760’s cancellation rule must continue to affect the input itself.

10. **“OIL unchanged” leaves the new actions underspecified.**  
    A scheduler adds Hex to a weekend course whose OIL question has never been answered. The change must wait for the answer; cancelling the question must leave both ends of the drag unchanged. Answering and making the change must be one Undo step.

    Preserve the distinction between adding to an answered group and replacing the only person, whose answer may need asking again. Keep individual scheduler exclusions in OIL Earn, even though the input has one common answer. Leave and medical never earn; qualifying work under Unavailable can. OIL taken must still be charged to each person separately.

11. **Individual marks and warnings must survive the combined row.**  
    Ranger filed on time; Hex joined late through the input window. Reading only the first man would hide Hex’s LATE status—or make Ranger appear responsible for it.

    Define the combined LATE indication and how the scheduler can identify the late person. Schedule-side edits must not create lateness; existing lateness remains. Hiding the mark is currently input-wide, not a separate decision for each man.

    Similarly, adding a man already away must use the existing checks. Where an overlapping commitment is allowed, he remains named and visibly flagged; where the existing absence rules refuse it, nothing changes. A warning about Hex must find and highlight Hex within the group, not merely the first puck or the whole row without distinction.

12. **The phone and the claimed removal of old faults need stronger acceptance cases.**  
    A ten-person row may have little obvious empty space on which to add someone. A finger landing on the nearest puck would replace him under the proposal.

    Draw distinct add and replace targets, provide a usable tap route for a small correction, and retain access to removal, the input window and Undo. Check typing followed immediately by Reject, keyboard Tab, scrolling and both Board layouts.

    Combining rows removes some split-during-typing cases. It does **not** prove that all the listed held-row faults disappear: the Leave War, withdrawal, deletion or another edit can still change an input while its row or window is open. Retain those checks until individually shown obsolete.

**The exception**

Medical is right, including **upchits**, which the proposal should name explicitly. Each is an individual medical record.

It is **not the only exception today**:

- Leave War-approved leave remains one person’s input.
- SANS availability keeps its own individual rules and dedicated controls.
- Automatic borrowing rows remain derived from the other squadron’s programme, not editable shared inputs.

I would not make every leave, course, OD or hand-filed Attach a compulsory row per man. They can remain genuinely shared where their people have the same dates and details, provided separating one person is safe and straightforward.

**Recommendation and questions before building**

Approve the direction after revising the plan, then approve desktop and phone drawings. The eventual build needs the **FULL** check because it reaches published records, permissions and earned leave.

The owner’s remaining choices are:

1. **Combine eligible shared leave while keeping Leave War-approved leave individual?**  
   **Recommend yes.** Do not enlarge this into a redesign of leave approval.

2. **Replace a named person whenever another is dropped directly on his puck, on every input surface?**  
   **Recommend yes**, with a clearly separate add target and the change to existing Ground/Common behaviour stated openly.

3. **Provide an explicit way to separate one man when his dates or times differ?**  
   **Recommend yes**, preserving his existing details in one operation and one Undo, without automatic splitting while typing.

4. **When dragging from an occupied flying or duty seat onto an input, should the displaced man inherit that seat?**  
   **Recommend no automatic exchange.** Change the input’s membership, retain the unrelated assignment and show any resulting conflict. This requires an explicit change to the existing replacement behaviour.

5. **What should an allowed ALL/ALL AVAIL drop onto an Unavailable commitment do?**  
   **Recommend matching the input window:** move the whole input to Common Programme, with that destination clear before release. Unsupported kinds and several-day inputs remain refused.

The other points above preserve existing decisions or complete the plan; they do not need the owner to decide technical details again.
