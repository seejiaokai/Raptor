### A5 — one swap; built only if he answers Q1 "yes"
**Final:** "You're planned to fly on Sat 18 Jul, 12:00–15:30, but you offered Fly 08:00–12:00 only. Tap to change your availability."
- **Swapped:** "from 12:00 to 15:30" → ", 12:00–15:30".
- **Reason:** the draft printed one span in words ("from 12:00 to 15:30") and the other as a range ("08:00–12:00") in
  the same sentence. The app prints a span of hours one way everywhere — the crew list's "available 08:00–12:00 only",

  2). Now both spans can be compared at a glance. The ruled opening ("You're planned to fly on Sat 18 Jul") and the
  ruled close are whole.
- **Nothing cut, nothing added.** Word count 22 → 20.
- **A fact I doubt, back to the builder:** which hours "12:00–15:30" are. The check in the app today holds his offer
  against the flight's whole span — from the earlier of report and step to the end of de-kit — not take-off to landing.
  Ranger does not see that span named anywhere; the warning list names the same flight by its take-off ("VL BFM (T/O
  13:00)"). See Q2.

### How A1–A5 split into title and body
The bell draws a bold title and a quieter body (today: "**You're SANS from Fri 17 Jul.**" over "You're planned on Sat
18, Mon 20 Jul with nothing filed. Tap to file for availability.").

**Recommended: split at the comma, no word changed or moved.**

| | Bold title | Quieter body |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | You're planned on the OFT sim on Sun 19 Jul, | but you offered Fly only. Tap to change your availability. |
| A2 | You're planned to fly on Sat 18 Jul, | but you offered OFT only. Tap to change your availability. |
| A3 | You're planned on the AMT sim on Sun 19 Jul, | but you offered OFT only. Tap to change your availability. |
| A4 | You're planned on the AMT sim on Sun 19 Jul, | but you offered Fly and OFT only. Tap to change your availability. |
| A5 | You're planned to fly on Sat 18 Jul, 12:00–15:30, | but you offered Fly 08:00–12:00 only. Tap to change your availability. |

- **Why:** his ruled sentence stays whole, in its order, read straight through; only the weight changes. The bold half
  is the fact a man out of the office most needs (what he is on, and when); the quieter half is why it is on his bell
  and what to do.
- **Its cost:** the title ends on a comma and the body opens on "but". Read as one line that is fine; if the bell
  stacks the two as separate lines it may look cut. That is a thing the drawing must show him — Q3.
- No one is named after the line (as on the reminder): the app worked it out.

### A6 — two days missed at once: one line a day
**No combined line proposed.** One line for each day is clearer, because:
1. each day can miss differently (Sat: planned to fly, offered OFT only; Sun: planned on the AMT sim, offered Fly only)
   — one sentence cannot carry two events and two offers and stay a sentence a person would say;
2. a tap opens his SANS calendar **on that day** — a line covering two days has no one day to open;
3. each line goes when its own day is put right; a combined line would have to be reworded as days drop off;
4. his ruled words name one day.
- The reminder for nothing filed lists several days in one line, and may go on doing so: there every day says the same
  thing. Here they do not.
- Even where two days miss in the very same way (OFT planned both days, Fly only offered both days), keep two lines —
  one rule, no special case.
- This is how the line behaves, not only how it reads, so it is the builder's to confirm and to show in the drawing.

## D. The Legend — the amber "S" (D2580)

**Draft:** "S" + "SANS availability"
**Recommended:** "S" + "SANS availability — nothing filed, or planned outside it"
- **Added:** "— nothing filed, or planned outside it".
- **How it is known to be true:** D2580 names exactly these two flags as wearing "S" — the nothing-filed line (D2579)
  and the mismatch (D2570–D2572), which in the app today covers both the wrong event and the wrong hours.
- **Why:** every other line of the Legend says what is wrong ("double turn", "misses flight brief", "crew pairing —

  + ground"): a family's mark, kept, and the one dash in the line.
- **Longer:** 2 words → 8. **The Legend row was not measured.** Because "SANS availability" stands in the bold of
  D2580's rule (though its row says the words are chat 173's, not ruled), this goes to him as a choice — Q5.

## E. The crew list on SC MAIN, SC SPARE, BB and AVALON (D2576, D2577)

### E1 — kept as drafted, with a caveat
"SANS — told on his bell"
- It takes the family's shape ("SANS — …"), is short enough for the column, and says what his lean asked ("state that
  notification is sent") in the words the rulings use (D2566: "told in his bell").
- **The caveat, a fact for the builder:** "told" is only true once the line is on his bell — after he has been put on
  the seat. On the crew list of a seat Saber has just armed, Ranger is not on it yet and nothing has been sent. So the
  note can only show beside a man already planned on that seat (or that line), never beside a candidate. The brief's
  reading says the same ("shown only once that bell line has gone"); the drawing must show it on a man already
  seated, or it will read as a promise.
- "told" does not claim he has read it. If the drawing makes it look like "seen", the plainer swap is "SANS — bell
  sent"; I do not recommend it — it is a clipped phrase with no person in it.

### What shows before the bell line has gone
**Recommended: nothing.** His name plain and unstruck, no note.
- Before he is planned there is nothing to report: he has not been told, and he need file nothing (D2576). A note such
  as "SANS — will be told on his bell" explains how the app works on a list opened many times a day — the guide's
  delete test 2 and 3 — and D2420 already ruled against a reason line for nothing filed.
- **And until the bell line itself is built, no note at all** — the app must never say a man was told when nothing was
  sent. E1 is built with, or after, the SANS info line ([SANS-INFO-LINE]), not before it.
- Whether he wants a note before the man is planned is his call — Q6.

### Q2. If yes to Q1 — which times does his line give for the flight?

- **(b) the take-off, as the warning list names a flight:** "You're planned to fly on Sat 18 Jul (T/O 13:00), but you
  offered Fly 08:00–12:00 only." Shorter and familiar; but when the take-off is inside his hours and only the debrief
  runs past them (T/O 10:00, offered to 12:00, done 12:30) the line would look wrong to him.
**Recommendation: (a)**, because it is the only one that is right in every case. The builder confirms the span first.

### Q3. How his bell line is drawn: bold first half, or a short bold heading over the whole sentence?

**Recommendation: (a)**, shown in the drawing at his iPhone's width so he can see whether the bold line ending on a
