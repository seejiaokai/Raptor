# Cold read of the Logic line (a fresh Fable, 28 Nov 26)

Read from one file only: the items sheet (the page description, the word list, the check's name and the line). Nothing
else was opened.

The line, under the check named "An input closes its hours":

> On the working copy the man an input is for counts as taken for the hours it covers, as soon as it is filed. SANS
> availability and an upchit take nobody's hours. Once a scheduler rejects an input, cancels it (CX) or marks it Info
> only, it stops taking the man's hours.

## 1. What is this?

An explanation of one scheduling check. It says that when somebody files an input (leave, a duty, another commitment),
the person that input is about is treated as busy for the times the input covers, on the editable day.

## 2. Why?

The line gives no reason. From the check's name and the page I take the reason to be: so the programme does not put a
person on a flight, sim or ground item while he is on leave or on another commitment. That is my inference; the line
does not say what the check does when it finds a clash (warn, block, mark).

## 3. What does it mean for me?

- As a scheduler: the moment an input is filed, that person is no longer free for those hours on the day I am editing.
  I do not have to accept anything first.
- Two kinds of input never make anyone busy: SANS availability and an upchit.
- If I reject the input, cancel it (CX) or mark it Info only, the person is free again for those hours.
- As a member filing an input: my hours are counted as busy straight away, until a scheduler does one of those three
  things.

## 4. What do I do, if anything?

Nothing is asked of me. It is an explanation. If I want the person free again, the line tells me my three ways: reject
the input, CX it, or mark it Info only.

## 5. Can any part be read two ways? Unclear or unknown words

**Two-way readings**

- **"On the working copy"** — (a) the rule holds only on the editable day, so on the day viewers have been issued the
  man is not counted as taken until the change is issued; or (b) it is only saying where I will see it, and he is taken
  everywhere at once. I lean to (a), but the line does not settle it and says nothing about the issued day.
  Suggestion, if (a) is meant: "On the working copy — not yet on the issued day — …".
- **"counts as taken"** — (a) the man is busy, not available for anything else in those hours (my reading); or (b) the
  man has been picked or used for something, as in "that seat is taken". A weaker third: with leave, "taken" is the
  ordinary word for leave used up ("leave taken"). The sentence's subject is the man, so (a) wins, but I had to stop
  on it. Suggestion: "counts as busy" or "counts as not available".
- **"as soon as it is filed"** — (a) with no acceptance needed at all (my reading, and the word list's "puts … back
  onto the programme" fits it); or (b) as soon as it is filed and accepted. A reader who expects inputs to need
  approval may doubt (a).
- **"cancels it (CX)"** — (a) the scheduler cancels the input itself (my reading; "it" is the input); or (b) the
  scheduler CXes an item on the programme. The word list defines CX as cancelling "an item", which is the word used for
  flights, sims and ground items, so a reader may wonder whether an input can be CX'd at all.

**Not two-way, but I stumbled**

- **"the man an input is for"** — I had to read it twice ("the man [that] an input is for"). Once parsed it is clear:
  the person the input is about, who need not be the person who filed it.
- **Three verbs for one thing** — the name says an input "closes" its hours; the first sentence says the man "counts as
  taken"; the next two say an input "takes" hours. I read all three as the same effect, but nothing says so, and a
  literal reader could ask whether "closes" is something more than "takes".
- **"its hours"** in the name and **"the hours it covers"** — I read both as the input's own time span. For a
  whole-day or several-day input, "hours" reads slightly oddly but is understood.
- **"it stops taking the man's hours"** — "it" is the input, not the scheduler; clear on a second look.
- **"the man"** — I read it as any person, man or woman.

**What the line leaves unsaid (not unclear words, gaps a reader may ask about)**

- What happens if a rejected input is accepted again. The word list says a rejected input is "inactive until accepted
  again", so I assume it takes the hours again; the line does not say.
- What happens if the input is deleted or withdrawn by the person who filed it. Only a scheduler's three actions are
  named.
- What "counts as taken" does on screen — whether I am warned, stopped, or see a mark.
- Whether SANS availability and an upchit are themselves inputs. The line implies they are the exceptions among
  inputs; it does not say so outright.

**Unknown words:** none, given the word list. "SANS", "upchit", "CX", "Info only", "working copy" are all in it. "Taken"
is the one everyday word doing work the list does not define.

## 6. Every claim, restated

- On the working copy (the editable day), the person an input is about is treated as busy for the times that input
  covers.
- This starts the moment the input is filed — nothing else has to happen first.
- SANS availability does not make anyone busy for any hours.
- An upchit does not make anyone busy for any hours.
- Once a scheduler rejects an input, that input no longer makes its person busy.
- Once a scheduler cancels an input (CX), that input no longer makes its person busy.
- Once a scheduler marks an input Info only, that input no longer makes its person busy.
- (From the check's name) an input "closes" the hours it covers — read as the same thing as the above.
