## Drafted lessons — one per NEW kind of gap

Numbered on from 24. These faults are older than lessons 1–24; where they stand in the list is the host's call.

25. **A thing walked only where the demo already had it (his find on his phone, OIL mode's ALL AVAIL count, 22 Sep 26).** Every ALL AVAIL row the OIL walk examined was one the demo seed already carried — a Common Programme row, one of the two places the crowd is honoured. Minutes after the sheet called the walk done he put ALL AVAIL on a duty desk he had added himself, and it counted and earned nobody, silently. The same gap let a counter row's Rearrange fault through on 15 Oct 26: the default war has no counter, so no walk dragged one (`docs/handpass/2026-10-15-lw-landing-line.md`). **Lesson:** the roll-call lists every place a person can PUT the thing — each kind of row, seat or list it can be dropped on, typed into or dragged within — not only the places the demo data already shows it; the walk makes each one through the app's own controls (§7.7) and reads the result there (anti-pattern 2; `docs/handpass/2026-09-22-oil-walk.md` §11).

26. **A gesture checked by the number it moved, not by where the thing went (his report, the Tracker's pinch, 23 Sep 26).** The walk's Edit-layout step asked that "the pinch zooms" — the zoom figure changed — and passed. The chart was zooming about a point below and right of the fingers, 807px off on a phone. **Lesson:** a gesture step measures what the person sees in screen terms — what was under the fingers stays under them, the dragged thing follows the finger — before and after, in every mode the surface has; a figure that moved proves nothing (§7.8; `scripts/handpass/trk-pinch.mjs`; `docs/handpass/2026-09-23-tracker-pinch.md` §6).

27. **A mode walked on its own, never straight after leaving another (his report from his phone, the Tracker's chart, 23 Sep 26).** *Doubtful — the sheet records his find, not why the walk missed it; this is the agent's reading.* The pinch was walked in the ordinary chart and in Edit chart layout, each on its own. Leaving Edit chart layout kept its own pan and zoom in force, so the next pinch on the ordinary chart drew it at both zooms at once, shifted and cut off at its left edge ("68 %" shown, about half drawn). **Lesson:** for every mode a surface can switch into, the walk leaves it and at once does the ordinary gestures of the mode it returns to — pinch, drag, scroll, tap — and checks the view, the zoom and the place against what the plain mode shows from fresh (`docs/handpass/2026-09-23-tracker-pinch-ball.md` §3 fault 2).

28. **A control's place never measured across the change beside it (his iPhone pictures, the Inputs tab, 9 Oct 26; again 21 and 23 Oct 26).** His standing rule (2 Sep 26) is that a control never moves under the finger that pressed it. Three walks passed screens where it did: the Calendar / List switch jumped left on the List (D687); pressing the filter button sent the gear to a line of its own (D693); the › beside a day or month name moved as the words changed, up to 38.7px (D1122); "Open for bidding", docked at the foot, grew upward and moved its month buttons each time a line was added (D1264). Lesson 17 measures that a page still fits after such a press, not that the controls around it stay put. **Lesson:** for every press that changes what stands beside a control — a view switched, a fold opened, a value with words of another length, a line added to a window — the walk measures each neighbouring control's box before and after, at a phone's width and a desktop's, and any move is a fault unless he ruled it (`docs/handpass/2026-10-08-inputs-sans-calendar-check.md` §17, §20; `scripts/handpass/bsw-arrows.mjs`).

29. **A press made by a script that scrolls first (his iPhone picture, the Inputs List's dates calendar, 10 Oct 26).** On a phone the calendar opened a whole screen below its button, out of sight. Four browser tests and every walker of the night's FULL check pressed its dates by a scripted press that first scrolls its target into view, so each step passed; a finger does not scroll the page to something it cannot see. The removal that moved the button (the List's own add form) was listed as taken away, never as moving what stood under it. **Lesson:** on a phone, what a press opens is proved on screen before anything in it is pressed — the page not scrolled by the script, the pop-up's box inside the screen, a finger at a point on it — and a change that removes or adds a part names in its roll-call what that moves up or down the page (§7.2; `scripts/handpass/rng-repro.mjs`; `docs/handpass/2026-10-10-list-dates-picker.md` §1).

30. **A page left open while the day turns (his iPhone, the Leave War's today ring, 23 Oct 26).** He opened the page before midnight and looked after it: the today ring stayed on 22 Oct while the chip already read from the 23rd, and a member's dimmed and outlined days stayed a day behind. No walk kept a page open across midnight. **Lesson:** anything that reads today — a ring, a lock, a "from today" count, a landing day — is walked with the clock moved past midnight while the page stays open, and again on reopening, and every reading is checked to follow the new day without a reload (`ui/dayclock.ts`, `dayclock.test.ts`; `docs/handpass/2026-10-22-lw-m1-open-dates.md` §16, D1266).

31. **A tint left by a press, unseen because the test browser drops it (his report, the Leave War's month strip, 23 Oct 26).** "Why do I see unclean blue background" when months were pressed: the grid's row-hover tint reached the month strip and turned its pinned cell see-through for as long as the strip counted as under the pointer — on an iPhone until the next tap, in the test browser for a quarter of a second. This lesson points to the line it already made, §7.10's first two bullets (D1281). **Lesson:** on a phone every tap is pictured within about 100ms of the lift with the pointer left where it pressed, beside the settled picture, and each dense screen keeps a standing test that its pinned cells stay opaque while pressed (§7.10; `docs/handpass/2026-10-23-lw-m2-looks.md`, his look of 23 Oct midday).
