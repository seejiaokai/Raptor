# Cold read of eight lines (a fresh Fable, 28 Nov 26)

Read from the items file alone: each line, what is above it on screen, and the word list. Nothing else was opened.
Where I say "not said", the line and what is above it do not tell me, and I have not guessed.

## Line 1 — Leave War card, "Where it came from" (OIL earned on Hunter's Sat 18 Jul)

> Earned off the published schedule. Change the schedule and publish the day again, and the OIL follows.

1. **What this is.** The source of the OIL shown on Hunter's Sat 18 Jul: it was earned because of what the published
   schedule has for that day.
2. **Why.** Because the published schedule says so. Which item on the schedule earned it is not said.
3. **What it means for me.** The OIL is tied to the published schedule. If the schedule for the day is changed and the
   day is published again, the OIL will change to match. In which direction (more, less, gone) is not said.
4. **Where I put it right.** Not on this card. I go to the schedule, change the day there, and publish the day again.
   The line does not name the page (Edit Schedule) or say that I cannot change the OIL here; I infer both.
5. **Two-way readings and unclear words.**
   - **"the OIL follows"** — (a) the OIL changes to match the changed schedule (it may shrink or disappear as well as
     grow); (b) the OIL comes afterwards, that is, it will be granted once I have done those two things. Reading (b)
     makes the line sound like the steps for getting OIL. I lean to (a) but (b) is a fair reading. Plain suggestion:
     "…and the OIL changes to match."
   - **"Change the schedule and publish the day again"** — (a) "if you change…" (a description of what would happen);
     (b) an instruction, as if something needs doing now. Nothing above says anything is wrong, so I take (a), but the
     wording is a command.
   - **"Earned off"** — I read "earned from". Beside the word OIL ("off in lieu") a reader could stumble on "off" for
     a moment (earned time off / earned off). Small.
   - **"publish the day again"** — the word list gives "Publish day" as the day's *first* published version. "Again"
     must mean a later issue of the same day; the word list gives me no name for that.
   - Not said: whether a change that is not published again leaves the OIL as it is. The line implies it does.
6. **Every claim, restated.**
   - This OIL (Hunter's, Sat 18 Jul) was earned from the published schedule.
   - If someone changes the schedule and then publishes that day again, the OIL will go with the change.
   - (Implied) Both steps are needed: the change and the fresh publishing.

4. **Where I put it right.** On the Inputs page (inferred; not named), by changing that input, then publishing the day

5. **Two-way readings and unclear words.**

   - **"that was accepted"** — (a) the ordinary sense: somebody approved it; (b) the word list's sense: Accept "puts

   - **"a duty input"** — which one is not named: no person who filed it, no duty, no hours. If the day has more than

   - **"publish the day again"** — I read that changing the input is not enough by itself; the day must also be

## Line 3 — Edit Schedule warning list, the hint on the small ✕ beside "Conflict: Ranger — VL SAT 12:45–14:15 & LECTURE 13:00–14:00"

> Hide this warning — it flags no puck and is not counted; it stays hidden while the same man is on the same two items, even if their times change

1. **What this is.** What the ✕ does: it hides this one conflict warning (Ranger on two things at once).
2. **Why I would.** Not said. Presumably I know about the clash and accept it.
3. **What it means for me.** After hiding, the warning marks no name tile and is left out of a count. It stays hidden
   for as long as Ranger is on both of these items, even when the items' times are moved.
4. **What I do.** Press the ✕ to hide it. How to bring it back is not in this line (the word list has "Flag this
   again", but the hint does not mention it).
5. **Two-way readings and unclear words.**
   - **"it flags no puck and is not counted"** — genuinely two-way. (a) What will be true *after* I hide it: it will
     stop flagging any puck and stop being counted. (b) A statement about the warning *now*: this warning flags no
     puck and is not counted, so hiding it is harmless. The present tense straight after the dash invites (b). Plain
     suggestion: "Hide this warning — once hidden it flags no puck and is not counted…".
   - **"Hide" / "stays hidden"** — (a) the line disappears from the list; (b) the line stays in the list and only its
     flags and its place in the count go. The word list says the line is retained in the list; the word "hidden" on
     its own says the opposite to me. A reader without the word list would expect the line to vanish.
   - **"not counted"** — counted in what? I assume a total of warnings shown somewhere; the line does not say where.
   - **"even if their times change"** — (a) "their" = the two items' times (my reading); (b) the man's times. Also:
     if the times change so the two no longer overlap there is no conflict at all, so the phrase only matters when
     they still overlap, or overlap again later. That means a *different* clash in time between the same two items
     will also be silent — I take that to be the point, but I had to work it out.
   - **"while the same man is on the same two items"** — if Ranger is taken off one and later put back, does it come
     back flagged or stay hidden? "While" suggests it comes back; not said.
   - **"the same man"** — Ranger, from the line above. Clear.
   - **The ✕ itself** — commonly means close or delete; the hint says hide, which settles it only if I rest the
     pointer (no pointer on a phone).
   - Unknown to me: **"VL SAT"** in the warning line above — I do not know what VL is, and SAT could be Saturday or a
     name of an item. Not part of the line under test.
6. **Every claim, restated.**
   - Pressing this hides this warning.
   - The warning (hidden) flags no person's name tile.
   - The warning (hidden) is not counted.
   - It stays hidden as long as the same man is on the same two items.
   - A change to those items' times does not bring it back.

## Line 5 — Logic page, under the check "An input closes its hours"

> On the working copy an input closes the man for the hours it covers, from the moment it is filed. SANS availability and an upchit close nothing, nor does an input a scheduler takes off the programme, cancels (CX) or marks information only.

1. **What this is.** An explanation of a scheduling check: an input makes a man "closed" for the hours the input
   covers.
2. **Why.** Not said beyond the rule itself. I take it that a man with leave, a duty or another commitment in those
   hours should not be put on something else.
3. **What it means for me.** On the editable day, once an input is filed the man counts as closed for its hours
   straight away. Five things do not close anyone: SANS availability, an upchit, an input a scheduler has taken off
   the programme, a cancelled (CX) input, and an input marked information only. What "closed" does on screen — a
   warning, a refusal to place him, a greyed puck — is not said.
4. **What I do.** Nothing is asked. If I want an input not to close a man, I can take it off the programme, CX it or
   mark it information only (inferred from the list of exceptions).
5. **Two-way readings and unclear words.**
   - **"closes the man"** — unclear to me as a phrase. I read "makes him unavailable". The check's own name says the
     input closes *its hours*; the line says it closes *the man*. Same thing, I think, but two objects for one verb.
   - **"the man"** — genuinely two-way: (a) the person the input is about; (b) the person who filed it. Line 6 shows
     these can be different people (Ranger filed for Ace). I assume (a). Plain suggestion: "closes the man it is
     for".
   - **"from the moment it is filed"** — genuinely two-way. (a) It takes effect as soon as it is filed, with no
     accepting or publishing needed; (b) the closed time starts at the minute of filing. Next to "for the hours it
     covers" I take (a), but (b) is there. Plain suggestion: "as soon as it is filed".
   - **"On the working copy"** — (a) the rule applies only on the editable day, and the published day behaves
     differently; (b) merely where I will see it. What happens on the published version is not said.
   - **"takes off the programme"** — is this the word list's Reject ("leaves it inactive until accepted again")? The
     line uses different words from the buttons, so I cannot be sure it is the same act.
   - **"marks information only"** — the word list's on-screen word is "Info only". I assume the same thing.
   - **"an input"** — every kind (leave, medical, duty, other)? Read literally, yes, except the kinds named.
   - **"SANS availability and an upchit close nothing"** — clear with the word list; it implies these two are also
     inputs, which the line does not say outright.
   - **"cancels (CX)"** — clear with the word list.
6. **Every claim, restated.**
   - On the working copy, an input closes the man for the hours the input covers.
   - That holds from the moment the input is filed.
   - SANS availability closes nothing.
   - An upchit closes nothing.
   - An input that a scheduler takes off the programme closes nothing.
   - An input that a scheduler cancels (CX) closes nothing.
   - An input that a scheduler marks information only closes nothing.
