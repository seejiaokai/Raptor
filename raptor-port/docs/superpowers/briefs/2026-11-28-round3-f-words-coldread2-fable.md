# Cold read of three lines (a fresh Fable, 28 Nov 26)

Read from the items file alone: the line, what is said to sit above it, and the word list. Nothing else was opened.

## Line 1 — the Leave War, Hunter's Sat 18 Jul, under "Where it came from"

> Earned off the published schedule. Change the schedule and publish the day again, and the OIL changes to match.

1. **What happened / what this is.** The OIL shown on Hunter's Sat 18 Jul was earned because of the schedule as it was published. The line is the answer to the label "Where it came from". It does not say what Hunter was scheduled for, or how much OIL it is.
2. **Why.** Because the published schedule had something on it that earns OIL. The line gives no more reason than that.
3. **What it means for me.** The OIL is not fixed. It follows the published schedule: if the schedule is changed and the day is published again, the OIL becomes whatever the new schedule earns. The line does not say whether that could be more, less or none.
4. **Where I put it right.** Not on this card. I change the schedule (the line does not name the page; I would guess Edit Schedule) and then publish the day again. Both steps seem to be needed: a change that is not published leaves the OIL as it is.
5. **Two-way or unclear words.**
   - **"publish the day again"** — (a) press the same publish control once more; (b) issue an amendment. The word list has "Publish day" only for the *first* version and "Publish AL1, AL2…" for later ones, so there is no button called "publish again". I would work it out, but I would look for a button that is not there.
   - **"Change the schedule"** — (a) any change at all to the schedule; (b) a change to what Hunter does on that day. Only (b) can sensibly alter his OIL, but the line says the wider thing.
   - **"Change … and publish …"** — (a) an instruction: do this now; (b) an explanation: if you ever do this, the OIL follows. I read (b), because nothing says anything is wrong, but the sentence is shaped as an order.
   - **"Earned off"** — a small snag in an app about time off: "earned off" can be heard for a moment as "earned [time] off". The second reading dies by the end of the sentence.
   - **"the day"** — I take it to be Sat 18 Jul, the day tapped. The line does not say so.
   - **"the schedule"** — the word list says "programme" and "working copy"; "schedule" is not in it. I take it to mean the week's programme on Edit Schedule.
   - **"to match"** — match what? I read: match the newly published schedule.
6. **Every claim, restated.**
   - This OIL was earned from the schedule in its published form.
   - If someone changes the schedule,
   - and then publishes that day again,
   - the OIL will change,
   - and it will end up agreeing with the newly published schedule.
   - (Implied, not stated: changing without publishing does not change the OIL.)

## Line 2 — the same card, Dusk's Sat 4 Jul, under "Where it came from"

> Earned off a duty input that was accepted. Change that input and publish the day again, and the OIL changes to match.

1. **What happened / what this is.** Dusk's OIL on Sat 4 Jul came from a duty that was filed as an input (on the Inputs page, by the word list), and that input was accepted.
2. **Why.** Because a duty input was accepted. Not said: who filed it, who accepted it, what the duty was.
3. **What it means for me.** The OIL follows that input. If the input is changed and the day published again, the OIL changes to agree. Until both are done, I take it the OIL stays.
4. **Where I put it right.** Change the input — the line does not say where, and the line itself does not show or link the input; I would go to the Inputs page and look for Dusk's duty on 4 Jul. Then publish the day again, which is a different page from Inputs. Two steps in two places, neither named.
5. **Two-way or unclear words.**
   - **"that was accepted"** — (a) somebody pressed Accept on it; (b) it simply stands, in force. The word list says Accept "puts an eligible input *back* onto the programme", which suggests Accept is only pressed after a Reject. So I cannot tell whether this input was once rejected and brought back, or was never rejected at all.
   - **"Change that input"** — (a) edit its details (hours, date, kind); (b) also reject, cancel or delete it. If I wanted the OIL gone, I would not know from this line whether rejecting the input counts as "changing" it.
   - **"publish the day again"** — as in line 1: no button has that name; and "again" assumes the day was already published, which the line does not say outright (line 1 said "published schedule"; this one does not).
   - **"that input"** — "that" points at something the line has only described, not shown. Fine if the card shows the input nearby; I cannot tell.
   - **"Change … and publish …"** — order or explanation, as in line 1.
   - **"Earned off"** — the same brief snag as line 1.
   - **"duty input"** — I read: an input whose kind is a duty. Clear enough from the word list ("duties and other commitments").
6. **Every claim, restated.**
   - This OIL was earned from an input.
   - The input was of the duty kind.
   - That input was accepted.
   - If someone changes that input,
   - and then publishes the day again,
   - the OIL will change,
   - and it will end up agreeing with the changed input.
   - (Implied, not stated: the day has been published before; changing the input alone, without publishing, does not change the OIL.)

## Line 3 — the Logic page, under the check "An input closes its hours"

> On the working copy an input closes the hours it covers for the man it is for, as soon as it is filed. SANS availability and an upchit close nothing, nor does an input a scheduler rejects, cancels (CX) or marks Info only.

1. **What this is.** A rule of the app. When an input is filed for a person, the hours that input covers become "closed" for that person on the editable day, at once. Three kinds of thing do not close hours: SANS availability, an upchit, and any input a scheduler has rejected, cancelled (CX) or marked Info only.
2. **Why.** No reason is given. I can guess one — a man on leave or on duty cannot also fly — but that is my guess; the line does not say what "closed" hours stop.
3. **What it means for me.** As a scheduler: once an input is filed, I take it I cannot (or should not) put that person on something in those hours on the working copy — whether the app refuses, or only warns, is not said. As the person filing: my hours are closed the moment I file, with nobody needing to accept it first.
4. **What I do.** Nothing needs doing; it explains a check. If I want the hours open again, the line implies a scheduler rejects the input, cancels it (CX) or marks it Info only.
5. **Two-way or unclear words.**
   - **"closes the hours"** — the key word, and it is not in the word list. (a) The person is treated as taken, not available, in those hours; (b) the hours are finished or locked, as when a record is "closed" and can no longer be edited. I lean to (a) only by guessing. The check's own name, "An input closes its hours", leans further towards (b): the input's hours, shut.
   - **"nor does an input a scheduler rejects…"** — (a) such an input never closes anything; (b) it closed its hours when filed, and stops closing them from the moment it is rejected, cancelled or marked. The first sentence says closing starts "as soon as it is filed", so (b) must be what happens, but the line never says the hours open again.
   - **"On the working copy"** — (a) this happens only on the editable day, and the issued version is untouched until the next publish; (b) merely where the scheduler will see it. Also, if the day has no published version yet, I take the working copy to be the only copy.
   - **"cancels (CX)"** — the word list says CX "cancels an *item*", which I had taken to be a flight, sim or ground item on the programme. Here it is done to an input. Either an input can be CX'd too, or I am unsure what is being cancelled.
   - **"a scheduler"** — only a scheduler's act is named. If the person withdraws or deletes their own input, the line does not say whether the hours open.
   - **"the man it is for"** — clear as "the person the input is about, not the one who filed it". "Man" would read oddly for a woman in the squadron.
   - **"SANS availability and an upchit"** — the sentence treats these as kinds of input that are exceptions. The word list calls one "offers" and the other "a record", so I take them to be filed on Inputs too. An upchit closing nothing makes sense to me (it clears a man to fly); SANS availability closing nothing I accept without knowing why.
   - **"the hours it covers"** — clear for an input with times. For an all-day input (leave), I take it to be the whole day; not said.
6. **Every claim, restated.**
   - This is about the working copy (the editable day).
   - An input closes hours.
   - The hours closed are the ones the input covers — no more.
   - They are closed for one person only: the person the input is for.
   - This happens the moment the input is filed — no acceptance or publishing is needed first.
   - SANS availability closes no hours.
   - An upchit closes no hours.
   - An input a scheduler rejects closes no hours.
   - An input a scheduler cancels (CX) closes no hours.
   - An input a scheduler marks Info only closes no hours.

## Readings I would call genuinely two-way

1. **Line 3, "closes the hours"** — "the person is taken in those hours" against "the hours are locked or finished". The word carries the whole line and is not in the word list. One plain suggestion: say what a person can no longer do in those hours, in the line or the word list.
2. **Line 3, "nor does an input a scheduler rejects, cancels (CX) or marks Info only"** — "never closed anything" against "stops closing, and the hours open again". Suggestion: say the hours open again when a scheduler does one of the three.
3. **Line 2, "Change that input"** — "edit it" against "edit, reject, cancel or remove it". Someone wanting the OIL gone cannot tell which act counts.
4. **Line 2, "that was accepted"** — "someone pressed Accept" against "it stands, in force"; made harder by the word list's "puts … *back*".
5. **Lines 1 and 2, "publish the day again"** — "press the same publish control" against "issue the next amendment (Publish AL1…)". The second is surely meant; the on-screen button will not say "again" or "day".

Smaller, not truly two-way: line 1 "Change the schedule" (any change, or Hunter's part of that day); the order-or-explanation shape of "Change … and publish …" in lines 1 and 2; "cancels (CX)" used of an input when the word list defines CX for an item; "Earned off" heard for a moment as "earned time off".
