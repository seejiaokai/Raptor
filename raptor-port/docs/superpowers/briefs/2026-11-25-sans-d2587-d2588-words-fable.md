## Wording 1 — the crew list's reason (D2587)

**Recommendation: keep the draft's words, without the closing full stop.**

> SANS from Fri 17 Jul: no availability filed

- **Every fact the scheduler needs is kept.** Two facts: why a man who is active today is struck on this day (his SANS posting starts Fri 17 Jul), and that nothing is filed. The sentence cut carried no fact about the man, only an instruction to the wrong person (D2587, D2420, lesson 100).
- **Reads as a person would say it.** "SANS from Fri 17 Jul" is a name-like opening and what follows is a phrase, so the colon is the right join (§1b test 2: the second part is not a sentence of its own).
- **Sits well beside the usual words.** "SANS — no availability filed for today" and "SANS — not offering Fly" both open on SANS and carry no full stop. The dash in those two is as built and is not this job's to change.
- **The one change: drop the full stop.** It is now a note of one sentence, and such a note takes none (§1b test 4), as its two siblings take none. The old line had full stops only because it was two sentences. Where the drop's note joins this reason to other words, the join adds the stop.
- **Exact words cut from today's:** "File it on the Inputs page's SANS tab." (an instruction, to the wrong reader) and the full stop after "filed". Nothing added, nothing swapped.

**One second reading, settled by the screen — recorded, not a fault.** "no availability filed" does not say for which day, where the usual line says "for today". A reader given the line alone could take it as "he has filed nothing at all". On screen it sits under his name in one day's crew list, or over one seat, so the day is plain. I would not add "for this day": the ruled words (D2338, D2340) never had it, and lesson 100 asks for fewer words here. For the builder to confirm, not the owner: the line is still raised when he has filed for other days but not this one — if so, the words remain true.

No instruction to anybody is added.

## Wording 2 — the schedulers' bell line (D2588)

### What I changed and why

1. **"withdrew" becomes "deleted".** One name for one thing (§1: "If the button says Unpublish, the note does not say withdrawn"). What Ranger presses is Delete; the bell's other lines say "deleted" (D2563 "Training on Wed 15 Jul was deleted."); and "withdraw" is already the app's word for two other things — Unpublish (the word list) and a leave bid taken back on the Leave War. Not checked on the running app: I read the delete question's words in the design rulings, not the SANS tab's own button.
2. **One bold title for the two cut-back cases, and what he now offers moved to the quieter line.** The drafts gave (b) and (c) different verbs and shapes ("cut his SANS availability … to", "now offers … only"), and (c) dropped the words "SANS availability". A family speaks alike (§1b test 9). In the plain style he approved (D2391, D2392) the bold title says what changed and the quieter line says what it means. So: title = who, what he did, which day; quieter = what he now offers, then what he is planned on.
3. **"cut" becomes "cut back".** His own words in D2588, and what a person says of hours or an offer; "cut his availability to Fly 08:00–10:00" reads as clipped.
4. **(d): both events named the same way and in time order.** The draft had "VL BFM (T/O 13:00) and the OFT sim (10:00)": one bare name, one with "the … sim", the later one first.
5. **Kept:** "He's planned on" (the reminder's own word, "You're planned on…"), "(T/O 13:00)" (a bracket that says what the word before it covers — allowed), "He's" (a contraction as people say it), no dash, a full stop on each sentence.

### The adjusted words

Bold title, then the quieter line.

**(a) deleted for the day**
- **Ranger deleted his SANS availability for Sat 18 Jul.**
- He's planned on VL BFM (T/O 13:00).

**(b) cut back to fewer hours**
- **Ranger cut back his SANS availability for Sat 18 Jul.**
- He now offers Fly 08:00–10:00. He's planned on VL BFM (T/O 13:00).

**(c) cut back to other events**
- **Ranger cut back his SANS availability for Sat 18 Jul.**
- He now offers OFT only. He's planned on VL BFM (T/O 13:00).

**(d) planned on two things that day** (the last sentence of any case)
- He's planned on OFT (10:00) and VL BFM (T/O 13:00).

Word counts, (a)–(c): draft 15 / 19 / 16; adjusted 15 / 20 / 19. **(b) and (c) are longer** by one and three words — his to take (§1b: a longer wording is marked and put to him). The cost buys one title for both cut-back cases and a quieter line that can hold any offer.

### Notes for the builder (facts, not his questions)

- **What he now offers is printed as the SANS card prints it** — "All day", the morning or afternoon words, or his own times — and "only" is used only where an event he offered before is gone. Where he still offers two things, the slot holds both ("He now offers Fly 08:00–10:00 and OFT.").
- **No "· Ranger" after the title.** The bell's other lines end with who did it ("· Saber"); here the title already names him.
- **A sim's name** in (d) should be whatever the schedule prints for that box, said the same way as the flight's; I wrote "OFT (10:00)" on that footing and did not check a sim line in the running app.
- **"T/O"** in a bell line: the squadron's word and in his own example (D2588), so it stays; I found no other bell line to compare it with.
- **Undo:** if he puts the filing back (an Undo, or files again) before a scheduler reads the line, D1693's rule for a line whose cause is undone applies — not reworded here.
- I joined the two quieter sentences with a full stop, not "but". "But" would claim the planned event no longer fits what he offers, and whether that is always true depends on question 1 below (§1b: no link added on a guess).

### Readings of D2588 to put to him, each as its own question with a worked example (D2611) — none decided here

**Q1. Does a cut-back that still covers what he is planned on send a line?**
When: Ranger is planned on VL BFM, take-off 13:00, on Sat 18 Jul, and has filed Fly 08:00–17:00. He shortens it to Fly 10:00–17:00. The flight still falls inside his hours.
- A: no line — the schedulers are told only when something he is planned on is no longer covered.
- B: a line for any cut-back on a planned day.
His words say "deletes or cuts back … for a day he is already planned on", which reads as B; the reason he was given (a line only where the plan is hit) leans to A. Under A the second sentence could say so plainly ("That no longer covers VL BFM (T/O 13:00).") — a wording to draw only once he answers.

**Q2. Hours moved, not cut.**
When: same day, Ranger changes Fly 08:00–12:00 to Fly 14:00–18:00. He offers as many hours as before, but the 13:00 flight is now outside them. "Cuts back" does not strictly cover this. Is a line sent, and does its title still say "cut back", or "changed"?

**Q3. When a scheduler does it for him.**
Admins may file SANS availability for a man (D2340). When: Saber deletes Ranger's filing for Sat 18 Jul himself. D2588 says "when a SANS man deletes or cuts back". Does any line go to the other schedulers then? If one does, "Ranger deleted…" would be untrue and the title needs the real name.

**Q4. One filing over several planned days.**
When: Ranger's filing covers Sat 18 and Sun 19 Jul, he is planned on both, and he deletes it in one press. One line naming both days, or one line a day? D2588 says "one line" and its tap opens "the day", which fits one line a day; the reminder's own style ("Sat 18, Mon 20 Jul") fits one line for both.

**Q5 (small). Two planned events, only one hit.**
When: Ranger is planned on OFT at 10:00 and VL BFM at 13:00, and cuts back to "OFT only". Does the line name both things he is planned on, as draft (d) does, or only the one no longer covered (VL BFM)? This follows from Q1's answer and could be asked with it.

One assumption I did not raise as a question: "planned" means a flying seat or a sim only, as he ruled for the man's own reminder (D2564). If the builder means anything wider here, that is a sixth question.
