## 3. The rule

**R1: one body, `mayGivePlanOf`.** The pure core goes in `state/units-model.ts`, over an account, a squadron, a day, the rows and the fallback. It is true only for:
1. a master admin whose role in force is an admin's (false in the member view); or
2. an account whose home that day is `unit` and whose level there, read through `levelFrom`'s home branch, is admin. `adminRoleHolds` stays unchanged and its `first` path is included. Never the grant branch, and never a stored `role === 'admin'` read beside a home.

The core calls `levelFrom` and keeps no second copy of `adminRoleHolds`.

There are two thin readers, and both fail closed:
- **The gate's**, in `state/perms.ts`: `mayGivePlanOf(unit, actor, rows, today)`. It reads the actor's account as it stood before the command, as `unitRowViolation` builds it at l.2721–2727, with the role in force and the rows before (`unitRowsBefore(env.changes)`). It never reads `worldsOf`, which is null for a command that only writes rights.
- **The doors' and the screen's**, in `state/accounts.ts`: `mayGivePlanNow(unit)`. It reads the signed-in account, `roleOf()`, the live rows, `PEOPLE` and today. It lives in `accounts.ts` because `units.ts` already imports from it. **Do not use an installed hook in `units.ts`:** one not yet installed would answer "no" in every test that does not load `perms.ts`.

Both readers answer false for:
- no account, including at the doors;
- an unreadable home;
- rows not handed in;
- an account switched off;
- a man archived, deleted, or a placeholder (D2730 Q10).

The receiver of Plan is judged on the rows and accounts after the command, by the role in force.
