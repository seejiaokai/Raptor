# Clean sheet, round 1 — Astra (GPT-6 Astra, 27 Nov 26)

Written blind to Fable, read-only, from the brief `2026-11-27-absence-clean-sheet-brief.md`; the run's final message, kept whole.

**1. The concept**

I would build **one Availability home, connected directly to the schedule**. A member records what is happening; an administrator decides leave bids against manning; a scheduler sees what that means for the week. Those are different views of the same input, rather than separate places with different editing rules. A bid remains recognisable when it becomes approved leave. A group remains one group until someone deliberately separates a person. After a week, the scheduler should say: “I can see who I have, understand why someone is missing, and change the right thing without wondering where it was entered.”

**2. The places**

**Availability replaces the separate Inputs and Leave War destinations.** It has two main views:

- **My inputs:** the member’s everyday starting point, especially on a phone. A compact dated list shows upcoming leave, pending bids, medical and commitments. A calendar remains available. “Add” offers clear actions, including **Bid for leave**, **Record approved leave**, **Medical** and **Commitment**.
- **Squadron:** the longer-range planning view. It retains the dense grid, the whole selected period, pilot and WSO manning, and open bidding dates. Administrators can bring the requests awaiting decision alongside that grid.

Medical records, the SANS calendar, leave balances and OIL remain directly reachable within Availability. They keep their specialised workspaces. They do not become a collection of summary cards, and SANS offers do not enter the absence list.

**The schedule remains a separate working destination.** Its purpose is still building and publishing the programme. It reads the same inputs and opens the same item window.

I would remove the separate **Personal Inputs decision list**. An input already on the programme or under Unavailable carries its own permitted actions. Commitments awaiting a scheduling decision have a compact **To review** list; rejected ones remain findable. The scheduler should not search a second list to act on something already in front of him.

These are deliberate departures from earlier layouts:

- **D620 and D626:** replace the three-tab Inputs arrangement and the member’s calendar-first default. Keep the calendars and their useful behaviour.
- **D1254 and D1483:** replace the separate Leave War navigation. Keep the balance tables’ useful phone behaviour, but put approval work beside manning on a desk.
- **D2773:** replace Personal Inputs as the permanent accept/Reject list. Preserve the decisions themselves, including what Reject means.

**3. The objects**

An **input** is one recorded event: its kind, people, dates, duration and shared details. It can be leave, medical, a duty, a course or another commitment. These kinds retain their different rules; sharing a window does not make them interchangeable.

Three questions must remain distinct:

- **What is happening?** For example, leave, a course or medical.
- **What decision has been made?** A leave bid may be pending, approved or refused.
- **What does it mean for planning?** The person may be unavailable, restricted from flying, or attending something on the programme.

A pending leave bid is visible in the member’s own list and the squadron’s bidding view. It reserves balance and affects bidding manning under the existing rules. It is visibly **pending**, rather than presented as an approved absence on the schedule.

**Approval changes that same item into approved leave.** Its original request and decision remain attached to it. There is no second leave for the member to find or maintain. Approval must not deduct manning or balance twice.

Leave already approved verbally uses **Record approved leave**. It does not enter another approval queue, and closed bidding dates do not block it. This preserves the substance of D1166 while removing the requirement to use a particular page.

**A group is one input with several people**, preserving D756. One row shows the event and its people; a person’s grid cell carries a small shared mark. Dates, time and shared remarks belong to the group.

The common window uses your short line: **Change [6 pax] [Cutter]**. The group button edits the shared input. Cutter’s button immediately separates him into his own input, with the same details, then opens it. Closing without a change leaves him separate; one Undo rejoins him. That preserves D2783 and D2784.

I recommend making that choice available everywhere the person has permission to use it. This sets aside the remaining prohibition on the shortcut in **D2770 and D2778**. It does not introduce hidden personal dates inside a shared group.

Private papers belong to named people, with access limited to their owner and administrators, as D2781 requires. Adding a paper never separates someone. I recommend that his paper follows him when he is deliberately separated.

A bid begins as one person’s request. Adding someone later must never give that person the bidder’s approval history. Each person’s authority for approved leave must remain clear.

OIL earned and OIL taken remain distinct. Changing a commitment or separating a person must not silently duplicate, move or erase an earned-leave award.

For filed inputs, I recommend a consistent duration choice wherever the kind permits it: **Whole day / AM / PM / Times**. Bidding retains its permitted choices. This replaces **D1350’s** page-specific whole-day restriction and **D1365’s** requirement to go elsewhere for exact times. For Other commitments, the intended planning effect is explicit and follows the input, rather than being decided by the page used to create it.

**4. Five moments**

**(a) A member bids for March leave during the January war**

1. He opens Availability on his phone. His upcoming inputs are visible, with **Bid for leave** prominent while bidding is open.
2. He chooses March dates from the calendar. Open dates and the existing manning information remain visible while he selects.
3. The window shows the leave kind, dates, permitted duration and balance effect. He submits.
4. The item appears in My inputs as **Pending** and in the squadron grid. He can reopen it without remembering where he filed it.
5. Closed dates block a new bid. The existing right to withdraw his own pending bid remains.

**(b) An administrator approves bids against manning**

1. She opens Squadron and selects March.
2. **Approvals** opens a narrow list beside the grid. Selecting a request highlights its people and dates without losing the surrounding period.
3. She sees the relevant pilot and WSO manning, including the pending bids already counted. Competing requests for the same dates can be viewed together.
4. She approves or refuses. The row stays in place for that visit and shows the decision, avoiding a moving target.
5. Approval changes Pending to Approved. The manning is not reduced again; the balance moves from pending to approved under the existing calculation.
6. Bulk approval applies only to the displayed, filtered requests. Anything that cannot be approved remains with its reason.

**(c) A member files medical on his phone this morning**

1. He opens Availability, presses Add, then Medical. His name and today are already filled.
2. He chooses the appropriate medical category and dates or times, and attaches the required paper.
3. The window states the resulting restriction plainly. It does not treat every medical category as a whole-day absence; an ATT B restriction remains different from being wholly away.
4. He saves. The item appears in his list and the scheduler’s current view. Other members cannot open his paper.
5. If a published day is affected, the scheduler sees that it needs attention. The published record itself waits for a signed amendment.

**(d) Six men have a stand-down; Cutter is recalled a day early**

1. The authorised filer creates one stand-down, selects the six people, sets the shared dates and saves.
2. It appears as one group row. Each person’s calendar entry opens that same input.
3. When Cutter is recalled, the scheduler opens it through Cutter and presses **Change [Cutter]**.
4. Cutter immediately receives his own input with the existing details. The remaining five stay together.
5. The scheduler shortens Cutter’s dates and saves. Cutter becomes available on the recalled day, subject to any other restriction.
6. If that day is published, the official programme changes only through the signed amendment. Undo can rejoin the group under the existing permitted conditions.

**(e) An hour before publication, Ghost becomes unavailable**

1. Ghost’s new input reaches the scheduler’s current view. His planned flying shows the conflict.
2. The scheduler opens the input from that conflict and sees the reason, duration and affected work.
3. The scheduler removes Ghost from the affected flight and uses the existing crew selection and drag controls to choose a replacement. TALON checks that replacement’s restrictions and clashes.
4. Any unresolved affected work remains visible. Entering the absence does not silently rearrange the programme.
5. Once the programme is sound, the scheduler publishes normally. Any affected day already published follows the amendment route instead.

**5. The main screens, in words**

**Screen 1 — My inputs, phone first.**  
A short header says Availability, with My inputs and Squadron beneath it. Add sits beside the current view’s title. Below is a compact dated list, starting with today and upcoming entries. Each row gives the event, dates or times, and any meaningful status such as Pending. Groups show their size. One tap opens the item. The calendar switch is small and persistent; specialist views are reached through a clearly labelled view menu. No greeting panel or dashboard cards consume the first screen.

**Screen 2 — Squadron, primarily desktop.**  
The existing dense grid occupies the screen. Names stay at the left; the selected period runs across it. Manning, period and open bidding dates sit in a compact bar above. Opening Approvals places a request list on the right, with a movable divider so the grid remains useful. Selecting a request highlights its dates. On a phone, requests use the full width, with the selected dates’ manning immediately above the decision buttons and a direct route to the full grid.

**Screen 3 — The common item window.**  
The heading names the event and its status. People and the short Change buttons come first for a group, followed by dates, duration and shared details. The planning effect is shown where relevant. Personal papers have a separate, clearly named section. Save and Cancel stay reachable at the bottom. A phone uses the available screen width; a desktop keeps the window compact. The same input offers the same permitted edits wherever it was opened. Viewing it never splits it.

**Screen 4 — The scheduler’s week.**  
Keep the dense programme and existing crew controls. Unavailable entries remain easy to scan, with one row per group. To review contains only commitments still needing a scheduling decision. Accepted items carry their own Reject action; rejected items have a reachable filter and can be accepted again. A group row accepts the existing permitted person drops. A conflict opens the relevant input directly. Routine drops remain fast, without a confirmation at every move.

Across all four, retain the dark Raptor appearance, talon, restrained spacing and meaningful colours. Use words and marks as well as colour for status. Phone filing should not wait for the entire squadron’s year to load.

**6. What it costs and risks**

This is a substantial redesign, although much of the useful screen work survives.

**Reuse:** the calendars, dense squadron grid, manning calculations, balance and OIL rules, medical rules, permissions, schedule controls, clash checks and published-day protection.

**Rebuild:** navigation; the member’s personal view; approval work beside manning; the shared editing window; the bid-to-approved-leave relationship; and the schedule’s duplicated decision controls.

The greatest risks are incorrect balance deductions, pending bids becoming real absences prematurely, group edits affecting the wrong people, private papers becoming visible to others, and current inputs leaking into a published record. Two people editing the same group also needs explicit protection.

I would deliver it in usable steps:

1. Agree drawings of the destination at phone and desktop sizes.
2. Introduce the common window through the existing pages, including full permitted editing.
3. Deliver group rows, scope buttons, explicit separation, personal papers and removal of silent separation together.
4. Make a bid’s progression into approved leave dependable, keeping its history and charging balances once.
5. Introduce Availability and approvals beside manning, reusing the established calendars and tables.
6. Remove the schedule’s duplicate decision list once every action has a clear home.

Each step must leave one dependable way to change an input. Temporary navigation may coexist; competing copies of the same leave must not.

The later Power Apps and Dataverse move remains a requirement. This proposal does not depend on a new outside service, but private document access and complete group saves need proving on that platform. I have not verified them here.

This was a document and source review, not a live phone or browser test. The drawings must still prove that the approval pane leaves enough useful grid space and that ordinary phone filing stays quick.

**7. Against the agreed consistency design**

It is **a different destination built on the same essential foundation**.

The earlier design makes three existing places behave consistently. This proposal removes the need for members to understand those three places, puts leave decisions beside their manning consequences, and follows a request through approval as one item. It also removes the schedule’s second location for acting on an accepted input.

I would still build the **common window first**, after agreeing pictures of the destination. It is useful immediately and supports every later step. Full editing from the planning grid is already directed by D1724; it should not be presented as a newly discovered requirement.

However, the window alone would not fulfil this clean-sheet proposal. Navigation, approval work and the request’s progression are central changes, rather than optional polish.

I would preserve your recent decisions on immediate separation, the short Change buttons and private papers. The new design should carry those forward, not reopen them by stealth.

**8. Questions only you can answer**

1. **When a member opens TALON during an active leave war, what should come first: his own inputs or the squadron bidding grid?**  
   My recommendation is his own inputs, with Bid for leave prominent and a direct route to Squadron. That serves everyday medical and commitments without hiding bidding.

2. **When deciding competing leave requests, do you usually work through people one by one, or compare everyone asking for the same stretch?**  
   My recommendation is to support both in the same approval list, with “same dates” as a quick filter. Your usual practice should decide its initial order.

3. **When you say “Leave War” to the squadron, is that name important enough to remain visible in navigation?**  
   My recommendation is Availability as the overall home, with **Leave War** naming the bidding view within it if that is the squadron’s established language.
