## The causes of the confusion (both readers)
1. What you may do with an item follows where it was born (a bid approved on the war, or filed on the Inputs page),
   not what it is — and a member's edit on the Inputs page can silently change which kind it is.
2. Each page opens its own window for the same item: the Inputs page the full window; the schedule the same window
   without its dates; the war one of three other windows, never the item's own.
3. A group is one thing on one page and several on another, and some gestures split a man off without a word (a time
   or remark typed on his Unavailable row, a remark or paper saved on his war box, the day list's "Add to the leave").
4. Three ways to say "when": the war's whole day / AM / PM, leave's and medical's four, everything else's all-day tick
   and times.

## The model (agreed)
The three pages keep their jobs — the war for bidding and judging manning ahead, Inputs for everyday filing, the
schedule for the scheduler's planning. An existing item behaves the same wherever it appears:
1. **One window,** the item's own, opened over the page in use and closing back to it, with its people, dates,
   duration, remarks, papers and the actions this person may use. A day with several entries lists them.
2. **One set of rights,** by the person and the item, never the page. A member corrects his own; whole-group editing
   stays with those entitled to change everyone; a scheduler's placing, Reject or CX is not cleared by a personal copy;
   View-only Sched stays read only; a published day changes only by a signed amendment.
3. **Two kinds of thing:** a bid waits for a decision (leave, one man's, the war only); everything else stands. Once
   approved, where a leave was born no longer decides what can be done to it; its approval history is kept, and
   "Back to a bid" acts on the bidder alone.
4. **A group is one item everywhere** — one card, one row of pucks, a box a man on the war that lights with its group
   when selected (and under the mouse on a desktop). Every shared change is for everyone; a puck added or taken off is
   so on every day. Nothing splits a man off by itself; a man's own paper needs no split (D2781).
5. **"Change for Cutter only"** — one deliberate action on every page: on the war a button named for the tapped man
   beside "Change for all 3 people" (Astra's; the host took it: it is his own ask and keeps the war's day window, which
   carries the manning line and Grant OIL — Fable would open the group's window straight away, "Cutter only…" its side
   door); on the Inputs card and the schedule's row, the tapped puck's action. It opens his own copy; he leaves the
   group only on a changed, successful Save; Cancel, an unchanged or a blocked Save leave the group whole; one Undo;
   a change to some of his days shows what happens to the rest.
6. **One Duration control:** Whole day · AM · PM · Times for filed items where duration applies; a bid keeps the
   first three; the war's grid keeps halves and the window shows how exact hours count there; medical keeps its own
   counting and overlap rules.
7. **Moves and adds alike:** a drag on the war or the Inputs calendar moves the whole item's days, a puck on the
   schedule changes who; every drag has a tap way too. A member's own approved leave dragged on the war just moves,
   keeping its approval, as on the Inputs page. Several men filed together for an OD or a course make one item, on the
   war as on the Inputs page; bids stay one man's. The schedule's add takes several people and a range.

Medical stays one man's; ALL / ALL AVAIL keeps its crowd controls and never shows on the war; Delete and Reject stay
different; an OIL award is never carried by an absence move.

## The order (Astra's, which Fable's round 2 nearly matches)
1. **The common window** from all three pages, dates included; each "change it on the Inputs page" line goes as its
   action arrives. Answers three of his five examples. Medium risk.
2. **Groups:** the combined row under Unavailable and its drops (the job in flight), the group lighting on the war,
   "Cutter only", a paper a man, one Undo — the silent splits removed in the same delivery. High risk.
3. **Duration:** the one control and the war's count shown beside exact hours.
4. **Movement:** every standing item draggable on the war, a group together. High risk.
5. **Creation:** one group OD or course from the war; several people and a range from the schedule.
6. **Approval history:** an admin adds a man to a bid-approved leave. High risk; last.
