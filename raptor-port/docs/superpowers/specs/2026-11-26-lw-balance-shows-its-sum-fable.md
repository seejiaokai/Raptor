# Question 16 — how a leave balance shows what counts (Fable's design, 26 Nov 26)

## 1. The recommendation: the balance shows its own sum

No sentence explains the rule. The screen shows the working, so a person can check it against the official leave app in a glance. The rule itself does not change (D1479 stands).

**One rule a person has to know, and it is the only cure for every slip:**
App bal should match the official leave app. If it doesn't, type it again.

### What a person sees

**A. One grey line under the App bal box, in the person window** (desktop and phone, same words; on a phone it is a line of its own, like "till 31 Dec"). Members see it too, with no box, so each man can check his own.

| When | The line reads |
|---|---|
| Never typed (q11 A: "Not typed" in amber) | `Type what the official leave app shows` |
| Typed, nothing has come off since | `typed 26 Nov` |
| Typed, leave approved since | `typed 24 on 26 Nov · −1 since` |

The instruction shows only while the box is empty — the one moment it is needed — and goes once a figure is typed.

**B. In the same window's Leave list, each leave that came off the typed figure carries its figure at the right: `−1`** (`−0.5` for a half). A leave with no figure took nothing off: it was already on TALON when the balance was typed, or (q12 A) it falls before his post-in date. The figures on the rows add up to the "since" figure on the line. Pending leave keeps its amber look as today.

His example, both orders:
- Leave put on first, then 24 typed: box **24**, line `typed 26 Nov`, the Wed 2 Dec row has no figure. Right, and it looks right.
- 24 typed first, then the leave put on: box **23**, line `typed 24 on 26 Nov · −1 since`, the Wed 2 Dec row shows `−1`. The slip is named on the screen: which leave, how much. He types 24 again; the line goes back to `typed` with the new date and the row loses its figure.

**C. On the Balances page, nothing is added to the rows** (they stay dense, no added height). One closed "How it works" fold at the page's foot, three lines:
- Type what the official leave app shows today.
- TALON takes off each leave approved after that.
- Doesn't match the official app? Type it again.

A name on that page already opens the man's window, where A and B are.

**No passing note, no question box, no new mark or colour.** The figures use the tile's own grey and white.

## 2. The double-count cases

- **(a) Leave already off the official figure, put on TALON after typing.** Not prevented — TALON cannot know what the official app has taken off. But it is no longer silent: the window shows `−1 since` and marks the very leave. Cure: type the official figure again.
- **(b) He applies in the official app and the figure is typed while his bid is still pending.** While pending, Pending and War bal already show the day. Once approved, the row shows `−1` and the line `−1 since`. Same cure. Rare: the normal order is approve here first, then apply.
- **(c) Leave before his post-in date, put on after typing.** With q12 A it never comes off, whatever the order. Its row never shows a figure, so the screen agrees.
- **q11:** "Not typed" and the instruction line belong together; the instruction replaces the baseline's standing sentence.
- **q10:** the recommended answer (keep the order, no tick on each leave) still holds. This design makes the wrong order show itself rather than asking people to remember it.

Honest limit: a slip in (a) or (b) is seen only when someone opens the man's window or compares with the official app. Nothing catches it for them.

## 3. Why it beats the baseline, and its cost

The baseline is a sentence the person must read, understand and remember every time, and it sits there for ever after. This puts three words on screen in the usual case (`typed 26 Nov`), and when something has come off it shows which leave and how much — the thing he actually wants to check — on the leave itself. The one instruction appears only while the box is empty.

- **Words on screen:** 3 usually; 6 with the sum; 7 while never typed; 21 inside a closed fold.
- **Build:** small to medium. One line in a tile, a figure on leave rows, one fold. The app already keeps the day typed and which leave was inside the figure, so nothing new is stored and no rule changes.
- **To settle at the build (my readings, not checked):** after the 1 Jan roll-over the line should start from 1 Jan (`42 on 1 Jan · −3 since`), since the typed figure no longer adds up; a leave deleted after it came off gives its day back and its row goes, so the sum still holds; the other kinds (CCL to CL) are typed the same way and could take the same line later — LVE first.

## 4. One alternative worth showing him

**Tap the `−1` to put it back.** An admin taps the figure on a leave row and picks "Already in the official figure"; the day returns and the row stays unmarked from then on. It fixes one leave exactly without retyping (q10's tick, placed where the slip shows) — at the cost of one more control and a second way to correct a balance.
