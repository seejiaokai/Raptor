Why a new chat starts 30–40% full

An empty chat is only 14% full. The jump to 30% comes the moment it opens its first app file: that pulls in your rulings, and they have more than doubled in two weeks. They also cost almost twice what they should, because their rules are written in capital letters.

Will it get worse? Yes, at this pace

Share of the chat taken by the rulings, worked out from the files' size on each day and the measured cost of loading them.

About 30 rulings a day are being added (D484 on 1 Oct, D968 today), and under the standing rules every one stays a line in the loaded list for good. That adds about 5 points to a build chat's start every two weeks. Left alone, a build chat would start near 45% in a month.

The app getting more complex is not the cause. The cause is how the rulings are kept: every line stays, including the ones a later ruling changed (220 of 754 lines say so), one-off permissions that were used up, and all of it in capitals.

It matters beyond room: every step of a chat re-reads everything loaded, so a fuller start also uses up your weekly allowance faster.

Your "mods" idea

A mod is an automatic check that runs at a set moment, such as before a push. It can take over a procedure ("never push while the checks are running", "take the PC lock before the full checks"). It cannot take over a ruling about what the app must do: I need to know those while I build, so they have to be read.

The procedure text that loads in every chat is only about 1 to 2 points in all, so mods would save under half a point. They are worth having because a check that runs by itself is never forgotten, not because they free room. The room is in the rulings.
