## 3. [LW-FROZEN-BAR-GAP] The frozen dates bar arrives one painted frame late

**(a) The question.** When the grid's dates row scrolls up under the top bar, the pinned copy of it arrives one frame
late, so for that frame the dates are cut or missing. Should this be fixed, or left?

**(b) Still open? Yes; the code is unchanged since 23 Sep 26.** Measured tonight. Before each frame was drawn, a timer
noted where the real dates row stood and whether the pinned copy was there. The browser's own painted frames were also
recorded.
- **One frame is 1/60 of a second (16.7 ms)**, measured.
- **His PC, the mouse wheel.** 3 runs: in every run 1 frame has the dates row half under the top bar (20 of its 45 px
  still showing, the weekday names gone) and no pinned copy. An earlier run of the same 3 had 2 such frames in two of
  them, so the blink is 1 to 2 frames.
- **His PC, a slow scroll.** 2 runs: 1 frame each, but only 5 px of the row hidden, so hard to see.
- **The phone too.** A finger pushing the grid up at the iPhone size, six runs at three speeds: 1 bad frame each by the
  page's own count. The row was 2 px hidden at a slow push and 4 px at a normal one, so neither is visible; at a quick
  push 29 of its 44 px were hidden.
- **What the phone painted.** At a quick push, two painted frames are wrong: one with the row cut, the next with no dates
  row at all. Then the pinned copy appears at the same scroll position. On a touch the browser moves the page by itself,
  ahead of the page's own code, which is why it painted one more bad frame than the page counted.
- **Not his iPhone.** This is Chrome made to the iPhone's size, not Safari on his iPhone. Whether his phone draws 60 or
  120 frames a second is not known. At 120 a frame is half as long, but the bar may then be late by more frames.
- **The Quals page** pins its header the same way (the same code shape). Not pictured.

**(c) Pictures:**
- `q-blink-desk-old-2026-09-23.png` — real, the old frames of 23 Sep 26: a copy of
  `docs/img/handpass/2026-09-23-lw-monthjump/fixed-desktop-frozen-bar-first-frames.png`, from before the rename and the
  newer look. Its frame 1 has no dates row at all.
- `q-blink-desk-today.png` — real, today, his PC: three painted frames in order. Frame 1 has the dates row in its place.
  Frame 2, ringed in amber, has the row half under the top bar, its weekday names gone, and no pinned copy. Frame 3 has
  the pinned dates bar drawn, whole.
- `q-blink-phone-today.png` — real, today, iPhone size, a quick push: frame 1 the row just under the top bar; frames 2
  and 3, both ringed, the row cut and then none at all; frame 4 the pinned bar drawn.

**(d) Options:**
- **A — fix it (recommended).** The pinned bar takes the row's place in the same frame the row reaches the top bar, on
  a desktop and on a phone, and Quals' header the same. No new words; nothing new to see except the blink gone.
- **B — leave it.** A flash of 17 to 33 thousandths of a second; the item closes.

Neither changes a ruling. A follows D991, which judges a scroll by how smoothly it runs, and D1518's spirit, that a
pinned bar shows the right thing at every scroll position.

**(e) Recommendation: A, as a small Leave War job with its own speed check.** The flash is short, but it shows on the
phone too, where it is two frames and once has no dates at all. It sits on the one gesture he makes most on the grid,
scrolling. The risk is speed: the obvious fix redraws more of the grid in the scroll's frame. So the build measures the
painted frames before and after, at both sizes, with this script, and stops if scrolling gets slower. If he says he never
sees it, B is a fair answer.

**(f) Worked example.** On his PC, Saber opens the Leave War on November and turns the mouse wheel twice. For one frame
the weekday names (SAT, SUN, MON …) vanish under the top bar and only the date numbers show, then the pinned dates bar
appears. On his iPhone, a quick push up shows a frame with the dates row cut, then a frame with no dates row at all,
then the bar. With A, the dates stay in place throughout.

**(g) For the build.**
- **Where the bar is set.** `src/leavewar/ui/Matrix.tsx`, `pin()` (around lines 1975–2030): `setStuck` runs in a passive
  window scroll listener, and React draws the change in a later task, after the frame has been painted. The bar is
  `[data-testid="sticky-head"]` (`.mxfixed`, around line 4006).
- **The fix directions from the backlog item.** Either commit the stuck-or-not change at once (`flushSync`) only when it
  flips, never on every scroll event, or keep the bar mounted and show or hide it outside React.
- **The phone needs more.** Tonight's painted frames show the phone's browser scrolls ahead of the page's own code on a
  touch. A fix driven by the scroll event can still leave one phone frame. Only a bar the browser keeps in place itself
  closes that last frame: sticky positioning, if the grid's sideways scroller can allow it, which is why the pinned copy
  exists at all.
- **Measuring.** Before and after, run
  `PR_ONLY=blink node scripts/handpass/pr-lw.mjs`. It logs the frames before paint, records the painted frames (desktop
  wheel, slow scroll, phone finger at three speeds) and saves the frames to `PR_SCRATCH`. The browser's own synthesized
  scroll gesture does not move this page headless, so the script uses real touch events; this was measured.
- **Quals.** `src/ui/QualsPage.tsx` around line 360: the same `stuck` state and scroll listener.
