# Cold read of the new words

Read from `words-for-cold-read.md` alone. Each section: (1) what it tells me, (2) what I would do or expect, (3) what is unclear.
A verdict closes each: **Clear**, **Clear, small catch**, or **Unclear**.

## 1. "No OIL this day" (label beside the switch)

1. With the switch on, nobody earns OIL on this day.
2. I would flip it on for a day that shouldn't earn, and expect every man's OIL for that day to stop.
3. "No OIL" can be read two ways. OIL is earned and also taken as a day off, so "No OIL this day" could just as well mean "nobody may take OIL off on this day". The bar's name ("OIL Earn") tips it towards the earning meaning, but the words alone don't say so. Across a desk I'd say "This day earns no OIL" or "No OIL earned this day". Also, a switch label that only describes the on state doesn't tell me which way is on.
**Verdict: Clear, small catch.** The earn-or-take double meaning is the catch.

## 2. "covers anything added later too" (words on hover over that switch)

1. The switch isn't only for what is on the day now: anything put on the day afterwards won't earn either.
2. Nothing to press. It tells me I don't need to flip the switch again after adding a line.
3. It's a fragment with no subject, starting lower case. Out of context, "anything" could mean a flight, a duty, a man or a later day. "Added later" means later than the moment the switch was flipped, but it doesn't say so. Something like "Also stops anything added to this day later" would stand on its own.
**Verdict: Clear, small catch.**

## 3. "Stop OIL for this day" (the button, before it is pressed)

1. The day earns OIL now, and this button can stop that.
2. Pressing it should make the whole day earn nothing, and the button should change to something that undoes it.
3. "Stop OIL" doesn't say "earning", so it has the same take-or-earn double meaning as 1, but weaker here because it sits on the Earn bar. I'd want to know whether pressing it takes back OIL already granted for that day, or only stops new earning. It also doesn't say whether it asks first.
**Verdict: Clear.**

## 4. "Let this day earn again" (the same button once pressed)

1. The day is stopped now. Pressing undoes the stop.
2. Pressing it should bring back the day's earning just as it was before.
3. "Earn" has no object. "Earn what?" is answered only by the bar it sits on. The pair is also uneven: 3 names OIL ("Stop OIL…") and 4 drops it ("…earn again"). "Let this day earn OIL again" would match. A button that shows the reverse action reads fine, but it relies on note 5 to show plainly that the day is stopped.
**Verdict: Clear.**

## 5. "Nothing on this day earns OIL. The marks underneath are kept and come back when you let the day earn again." (note under the bar while stopped)

1. The first sentence is clear: the day is stopped. The second tells me something is kept and will come back.
2. I'd carry on. When I want the day back, I'd press "Let this day earn again" and expect what was there before to reappear.
3. "The marks underneath" is the problem. Underneath what: this note, the stop, the pucks? And which marks: each man's OIL choices, warning marks, amendment marks? "Come back" suggests they're hidden now, but I can't tell whether they've vanished from view or are still there but greyed out. I'd say something like "Each man's OIL choices are kept and come back when…". Also unclear: does "Nothing earns" cover OIL an admin granted by hand?
**Verdict: Unclear (the second sentence).**

## 6. "Nothing on this day earns. Let the day earn again first." (passing note after tapping a puck while stopped)

1. My tap did nothing because the day is stopped.
2. I'd look for the control that lets the day earn again, press it, then tap the puck again.
3. It doesn't say where that control is. It quotes the button's wording from 4, so it only works if the button design is chosen. With the switch design (1), the note should say to turn off "No OIL this day" instead. "Earns" has no object again, but that's fine inside OIL Earn. It doesn't say outright that nothing changed, though "first" implies it.
**Verdict: Clear, small catch.** It must match whichever control is chosen.

## 7. "Fable: no OIL worked out — this row has no end time" (passing note, row with a start but no end)

1. I take "Fable" to be the man's callsign. The app couldn't work out his OIL because the row has no end time.
2. I'd add an end time to that row and tap again.
3. Three things trip me up:
   - "Fable:" with a colon reads like a speaker's name, as if someone or something called Fable is talking, not "this is about Fable". I only guessed it's his callsign because the note follows tapping a man's puck.
   - "No OIL worked out" has two meanings in an app where OIL is earned by working: "no OIL could be calculated" or "he worked no OIL". It also echoes "it didn't work out".
   - It doesn't say whether the tap did anything (was he marked, or not?) or whether the OIL will appear by itself once the end time is added.

   "Fable: can't count his OIL — this row has no end time" would avoid the double meaning.
**Verdict: Unclear.**

## 8. "Fable: nothing measurable to earn from here" (passing note, row with nothing to measure)

1. Something about this row means Fable can't earn from it.
2. I don't know what to do. I can't tell what's missing or whether anything would fix it.
3. "Measurable" is an engineer's word: measured how, in hours or times? "From here" is vague: this row, this kind of line, this spot on the board? Unlike 7, it gives no reason I can act on. It also has the same speaker-label problem with "Fable:". I'd want "this kind of row has no times to count OIL from" or the actual reason.
**Verdict: Unclear.**

## 9. "Blocked: no OIL worked out — this row has no end time" (grey refusal, in place of 7)

1. My tap was refused and nothing changed, because the row has no end time.
2. I'd add the end time and try again.
3. "Blocked:" makes it clear the tap did nothing, which 7 doesn't. But it drops the man's name, so after several taps I can't tell which puck it's about. "No OIL worked out" keeps its double meaning from 7. And "Blocked" is a heavy word for a missing end time; it sounds like permissions or a lock. Notes 13 and 14 end with "Nothing was saved."; this one doesn't, which is inconsistent if they share a look.
**Verdict: Clear, small catch.**

## 10. "↓ 8 more" (foot of a long list in a day's window)

1. There are 8 more lines below what I can see.
2. I'd scroll down. I might also tap it, expecting it to scroll or expand.
3. It doesn't say whether it's tappable or just a hint. "More" what: lines, people, inputs? The window makes that obvious. Does the number count down as I scroll? I'd expect it to.
**Verdict: Clear.**

## 11. "This week's saved copy could not be read, so it can't be shown here. It is kept as it was saved. Report it on the Help page." (notice on each day of an unreadable week)

1. The week's stored data is damaged or unreadable, it isn't shown, it hasn't been deleted, and I should report it.
2. I'd go to the Help page to report it, and expect a way there to do so.
3. "Saved copy" hints that some other copy exists, such as a working one. Is there? "Can't be shown here" suggests it might be shown somewhere else; where? "Kept as it was saved" reassures me nothing was deleted, but it doesn't tell me whether the week is lost to me in practice or whether someone can recover it. It doesn't say whether I can rebuild the week meanwhile (13 says I can't edit it). Shown on every day, the same three sentences repeat seven times. To a member who only reads the schedule, "saved copy could not be read" sounds alarming, and it isn't clear whether the report is his job or a scheduler's.
**Verdict: Clear, small catch.**

## 12. Same as 11 without "Report it on the Help page."

3. Shorter, but a notice that something is broken with no action leaves the reader stuck. I'd keep the report line, perhaps only once rather than on every day.
**Verdict: Clear, small catch.** Clear, but no next step.

## 13. "Blocked: this week's saved copy could not be read, so it can't be edited. Nothing was saved." (refusal when saving into that week)

## 14. "Blocked: this week was published by an older version of the app, so it can't be edited. Nothing was saved." (refusal for a week published by an older app)

1. The week is locked because it came from an older version of the app. My change wasn't kept.
2. I'd be stuck. I'd ask whether I can never amend this week again, and who can fix that.
3. Users don't think in app versions, so this reads like a fault rather than a rule. It gives no next step: whom to ask, whether it's permanent, whether it can still be viewed or amended another way. People publish weeks, not the app, so "published with an older version of the app" is more natural than "by".
**Verdict: Clear, small catch.** Clear, but a dead end.

## 15. Logic page, under "Default rally before take-off"

Words: "An empty R box suggests take-off less this time, and "+ In-time" fills in the same time. With no in-time or rally entered, a flyer's OIL counts from here. His long day and Insights hours count from step. It also sets when the tight crew-rest advisory shows."

1. The setting is a default gap before take-off, used to suggest a rally time. With nothing entered, OIL is counted from it, while long day and Insights hours are counted from step. It also affects a crew-rest warning.
2. I'd change the minutes if our usual rally lead differs. I'm not sure what else would change.
3. This is the hardest of the set.
   - "R box": I guessed R means rally, but it's never spelled out.
   - "Take-off less this time": I first read "less" as "less often", not "minus". "Take-off time minus this" would be plain.
   - "Fills in the same time": the same as what, the suggestion?
   - "Counts from here": "here" for a setting is odd. It means "from this rally time".
   - "His long day and Insights hours count from step": I can't tell whether "with no in-time or rally entered" still applies. Is that always so, or only when nothing is entered? And why do OIL and long day start from different points? Read cold, it looks like a contradiction.
   - "Long day", "step", "tight crew-rest advisory": none is explained. "Tight" especially leaves me guessing.
   - "It also sets…": "it" = the setting, I think.

   Five ideas packed into four sentences. I'd split it into short lines: what it suggests; what OIL counts from; what long day and Insights count from, with the condition repeated.
**Verdict: Unclear.**

## 16. Logic page, added under "Step before take-off": "With no in-time or rally entered, a flyer's long day and Insights hours count from it too."

1. When nothing is entered, long day and Insights hours start from step time.
2. Nothing to press. I'd understand the step setting matters for those hours.
3. "It" = step, and "too" refers to whatever the earlier text says counts from step, which I can't see. It states the condition clearly, and that shows up 15's missing condition, so the two should be worded the same way. "Long day" is still unexplained, but a scheduler would likely know it.
**Verdict: Clear.**
