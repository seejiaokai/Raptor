## The lessons as they stood on 10 Nov 26

**Lesson 93 (8 Nov 26, D1842 — "the left have a line but the right doesn't and also the word no document is very far to the right edge so I did try to scroll horizontally myself … can we fix it such that it doesn't mislead the user").** The desktop Inputs list's right end must not mislead a person into thinking more of it runs on past it. He named two causes, the first "partly maybe": its rows carry a line on the left and none on the right, and "No document" stands "very far to the right edge"; he tried to scroll sideways and took it for a design issue. *(His case: the list at 821 wide, his phone's picture of it.)* *The agent's reading, not his words: "No document" ended 3px from the list's end, past its own column's padding, where the names start 12px in from the left; the fix is that the right side is not left open where the rows carry a line on the left, and nothing stands right against the end — the other half of §3's "a list that scrolls shows a cut row, not a clean edge": a list that does not scroll shows a closed end.* *The agent's suggestion, under D852 — other places, not looked at for this and not changed: the Leave War's Approvals list on a desktop (its last column at the right end), Admin → Users' list, the OIL tracker's figures; Quals already closes its table with a line round every cell.*

**Lesson 89 (5 Nov 26, D1699 — "If the post out was taken back shouldn't it show that it's striked out? Instead of faded which means it's read and the post out happened?").** A bell line for something taken back keeps its own words, struck through, with "taken back · <who>" after them — never only a new line in its place, and never shown only by fading, which reads as "read, and it happened" (the struck line still fades once read, like any line). *(His case: the drawn "Posting out taken back · Saber" replacing a posting's line, beside a faded line of a posting that stood.)* *The agent's reading, not his words: a strike is how the app says "did not happen" (a cancelled input's title, a refused bid, a hidden warning), so the same sign carries it in the bell.* *Other places, named under D852: the Leave War's own taken-back lines (built with this one, D1699), the input lines and the holiday's OIL line when their undo is built ([NOTICE-UNDO-RETRACT] step 2).*

**Lesson 92 (5 Nov 26, D1700 — "Isint the oil earned symbol the same as history? That u drew").** The board's OIL Earn must not wear a clock while History's clock stands beside it on the same bar. *(His case: round 4's drawing gave the Scheduler Board's OIL Earn the Leave War's OIL clock, beside the board's History button, itself a clock with an arrow turning back.)* *The agent's reading, not his words: it is lesson 70's other half (one icon, one thing) — before a new mark is proposed, it is set beside every mark already on its bar and on its page, and any two a person could mistake are redrawn or one takes its words alone.* *Other places, named under D852: the Leave War's OIL tab (the same plain clock, on another page); the reset arrow of Reset order and Reset layout (History's arrow without the clock); the Tracker's chart-layout tools drawn in the same round (Select and Select all, Connect and Arrow).* *(Answered 5 Nov 26 — D1704: OIL's own mark is a ticket, on OIL Earn and the Leave War's OIL tab.)*

**Lesson 82 (5 Nov 26, D1654 — "is it weird that the fade is abruptly stopped at the top half"; then "I like the fade that starts from top left").** A fade is not cut off abruptly partway across the thing it tints. *(His case: the pass on the screen after asking for access, its blue tint from the top left stopped hard at the tear line; he asked whether that was weird, and of the fixes shown he chose to keep the fade from the top left.)* Shown the fade laid over the whole card as one surface, thinning out across the tear, he approved it: *"My fade looks good"* (D1656). *The agent's reading, not his words: the same holds for any tint or wash on a card, a band or a page.*

**Lesson 77 (3 Nov 26, D1626 — "if u want to close, you position your hand to reach it. Now u put it at the bottom the user has to shift drastically making it not ergonomic").** A question raised by pressing a control appears right at that control, where the hand already is — a small card hanging from it and pointing at it — never at the foot of the window, which makes the hand shift. *(His case: the input window's "Close it and lose them?" after its ✕; of three drawn designs — the window's foot (recommended by the agent, as an iPhone does it), a line under the title, a card from the ✕ — he picked the card, "for all similar cases".)* *The agent's reading, not his words: "similar" is any confirmation or choice put up because a control was pressed — close, delete, discard, open another over unsaved work; one raised by a key with no control (Escape) hangs from the control that does the same thing (the ✕); the card covers what is under it only while it is up.* *Other places (the agent's list), named under D852 and filed as [ASK-AT-THE-CONTROL]: the same window's "Open the other input and lose them?", the opened day's "Delete this input?", the upchit and medical-clash sheets raised by Save, and any other confirmation the roll-call finds.* **Narrowed 4 Nov 26 by D1627 ("Just change these"):** of the 71 questions the roll-call drew, only five change — the input window's "Open the other input and lose them?", "Delete this input?" and "Take yourself out?", and the Leave War posting's delete at its Delete chip and PO from date — and, by D1628, the same posting delete from the Post out button and the Delete key's "Delete this input?"; the rest stay as today.

**Lesson 81 (3 Nov 26, D1608 — "seems like the talon page is abit off").** The sign-in's claw, left where it had stood beside RAPTOR, read apart from the word TALON; he picked the claw moved 7px nearer. *(His case: the sign-in page after the rename, where the agent had measured the claw in the same place and found nothing wrong.)* *The agent's reading, not his words: a first letter whose upright stands in from its edge (a T) leaves more room by the mark than one whose upright is at its edge (an R); so after a word beside a mark changes, the spacing is looked at by eye against the letters as drawn, not only measured by the boxes.* *Other places, named under D852: the top bar's claw beside "142 TALON", the request-access screens (fixed with the sign-in — the same mark), the tab's claw beside its title.*

**Lesson 76 (3 Nov 26, D1578 — "shouldnt it save in the leave war OIL count first? as well as the oil earned? If the period is created after then it shows the chip. Doesnt make sense for an input to create a leave war period right?").** An input never creates a Leave War period, and never offers to: its OIL answer is saved as on any other day, and the OIL shows on the Leave War, in his OIL count and as OIL earned, once a period holding the date exists. *(His case: the input's OIL question on a weekend no Leave War period covers, where the agent had recommended the question offer "Create the period".)*

**Lesson 75 (2 Nov 26, D1568 — "when I click on my own row, specific day. the highlighted grid selected can’t be seen since it’s the same colour. I’m able to see the if I click on other days.").** A mark that shows what was picked can be seen on every row it lands on — his own highlighted row included. *(His case: the Leave War, Saber’s own row lit in cyan, Mon 30 Nov tapped, its cyan selection mark lost in the row. The agent’s readings: a mark is checked on each background it can sit on, his own row and a lit row among them; where a mark and its background share a colour the mark is made heavier, not given a new colour — his pick, A, D1569.)* *Where else it applies (D852, named, not changed):* the Scheduler Board’s own puck ("this is you", purple) under a highlight chip or a selection; the Inputs calendar’s just-added ring on a day already ringed as today; the OIL tab’s own row and its picked cell; the Leave War’s day heading ringed cyan (D1449) while its date is picked.

**Lesson 73 (30 Oct 26, D1527 — "U didnt show me a mock up of how transparent it is. make it similar to the old one").** How see-through a thing is, is shown to him in a picture before it is built. *(His case: the week's ‹ › arrows sliding out; his "still slightly transparent" was read as 0.9 and lit blue, built and walked, and he first saw it on the evidence page. **Lesson 72 (29 Oct 26, D1518 — "U see this gap. I don’t like to see it. Make sure at all zooms or scroll I don’t see it. Make this a design note. I don’t like to see such leaky designs. Like in this case a bar with things visible behind").** A bar, or anything else that stays put while what is under it scrolls, shows nothing of what is behind it — no gap at its edges through which that content shows — at every zoom and every scroll position. *(His case: the Leave War's Balances page on his iPhone, zoomed out, the boxes of a row showing through the foot of the bar, in a thin band just above the column headings — D1519. The agent's reading: "at all zooms or scroll" means the check is made zoomed in and out and scrolled, with the spill a test browser does not draw forced (the bug-check order §7.11 lesson 15), not only at rest.)* *Where else it applies (D852, named, not changed):* the OIL tracker's headings, which share the Balances page's pinch; the Leave War grid's frozen date row and its corner; Edit Schedule's pinned headings and names; the Tracker's frozen rows; the Inputs list's headings.

**Lesson 71 (29 Oct 26, D1512 — "theres no edit icon or cross icon").** A line of the Leave War's Events list that can be edited and deleted shows an edit icon and a cross icon on the line itself. *(His case: the Leave War's Events list, each line opened only by a tap on it, beside his colleague's two lists with an Edit and a Remove on every line. The agent's reading, put to him as a drawing: the app's one pencil (D1134) and a ✕ that asks once before it deletes; that the same holds for other such lists is the agent's reading, named in the closing report, not changed.)*

**Lesson 70 (29 Oct 26, D1507, D1508 — "We have like different settings icon. Can we standardise it?"; "Also the calendar icon is different. Can we follow 1 design throughout the app? Use the one in edit schedule?").** One thing has one icon throughout the app: every settings gear one design, every calendar icon Edit Schedule's. *(That each is drawn by the app, so his iPhone does not swap in a coloured picture of its own for the ⚙ or 📅 symbol, is the agent's reading, from D1134's pencil; which gear is his pick, put to him with pictures.)* *Where else it applies (D852, named, not changed):* every other icon the app draws in more than one place — the look of [UI-CONSISTENCY-LOOK] (D1124) goes over them all. *(His pick, 29 Oct 26 — D1509: the gear is the Leave War's line gear.)* *(5 Nov 26 — D1707: each gear in its button's own colour, as every drawn icon is.)*

**Lesson 69 (29 Oct 26, D1503, D1506 — "when u exit it goes into leave war page and not back to the same balances page"; "Likewise for this page. It should go back from OIL to the inital page"; "And from approvals, my leave, oil, and it should go back to the inital page").** Closing a window takes him back to the page he opened it from, as he left it — never to another page. *Where else it applies (D852, named, not changed):* every window the app opens from more than one page — the person window, the document viewer, the Calendar window opened from the Leave War's ⚙ — is checked to close back where it was opened.

**Lesson 68 (29 Oct 26, D1496 — "I would like all mockups to standardise the design to the rest of the app").** Every mock-up's design is standardised to the rest of the app. *(Said with his "Yes" to the drawing check, on its page showing M5a's drawings and build differing part by part; he named no part. The agent's reading: each part of a mock-up is drawn the way the app already draws that kind of part — what it is, where it sits, its icon, colours, letters, sizes, corners and spacing — found on the running app; a part that has to differ is named to him as a new design, with its picture and its reason.)* Other places it may apply, suggested and not changed: M5b's drawings before they are built; a mock-up's new buttons beside the app's own; the drawing check, which could later also measure a drawn part against the same kind of part elsewhere in the app.

**Lesson 67 (28 Oct 26, D1468, D1471 — "Can we just make the leave war to be puck style like the chip is now merged with the name"; then, shown a restyled bar, "Wouldn't it be better to be like this design pick … Puck").** On the Leave War grid a person's name is drawn puck style, the chip merged with the name, as it is on the schedule — the schedule's own puck, as the Approvals list draws it, not a look of its own. *(His case: the Leave War grid's name column, where the chip stood apart from the name; and the agent's first drawing, a restyled bar he sent back for the Approvals list's puck. **Lesson 66 (28 Oct 26, D1463 — "Approve what I enter, is there way shorter way to write it but still have the same meaning").** A control's words are kept as short as they can be without changing their meaning. *(His case: the admin's switch in the Leave War's bid windows, "Approve what I enter", now "Auto-approve". **Lesson 65 (28 Oct 26, D1462 — "do u think the Tue 13 Apr title should be abit more brighter and obvious?").** A window's title makes the day it is about bright and obvious, not faint. *(His case: the Leave War's bid window, its date small and faint grey beside the name. **Lesson 64 (27 Oct 26, D1438 — "Why is static name aligned to the right? should it be left … (Why u align right????) Can lowest avail align the numbers instead of the word text?").** In a list, names and figures read from the left; where a figure carries a name of its own, the figures line up with each other and the heading stands over the figures. *(His case: the Leave War's Approvals list — a WSO's name at the right of its puck, DAYS and LVE BAL set to the right, LOWEST AVAIL's heading over "W" and "P" rather than the numbers. **Lesson 63 (27 Oct 26, D1437 — "Seems like the alignment of the title and the text is off. fix it. Check the desktop too").** A column's heading and the figures under it line up, on a phone and on a desktop alike. *(His case: the Leave War's Approvals list on his phone, its headings DAYS · LVE BAL · LOWEST AVAIL · PLACED over each request's second line. The agent's reading: "the title" is the column headings and "the text" the figures; lined up means a figure set to the left starts where its heading starts and one set to the right ends where its heading ends, checked by measuring, not by eye.)* Other places it may apply, suggested and not changed: the Inputs list's desktop columns, Admin → Users' list, Quals' headings over its table, the OIL tracker's figures.

**Lesson 62 (27 Oct 26, D1429 — "Maybe move lower avail and placed abit more space to the right. dont wanna confused the lve bal numbers on the left").** Figures in two columns side by side stand far enough apart that one column's numbers are not confused with the next one's. *(His case: the Leave War's Approvals list on a desktop, LOWEST AVAIL and PLACED moved a little further right of LVE BAL. The agent's reading: the gap between those two columns is clearly wider than the gaps between the other figure columns.)* Other places it may apply, suggested and not changed: the Approvals list's second line on a phone, the OIL tracker's figures, the Leave War person window's balance figures.

**Lesson 61 (27 Oct 26, D1412 — "all the text areas I click screen jerks or bounce a little when the keyboard opens. Even for text that are above the keyboard … Why can’t the text box I click on, auto scroll proportionately with the keyboard that appears and the window doesn’t shrink awhile. My idea is a smooth feel for this app and this made it worse").** When the keyboard opens, the box tapped rises in step with the keys, proportionately — one motion, the keys' own — and nothing else on screen moves: a box already above the keys stays exactly where it is, and no window shortens and grows back or lets the page behind it show while the keys move. What it says about the app he intends (D852): a correct end place is not the measure; the feel of the motion is, and a mechanism that steers a window frame by frame against the phone's own slide is judged by what he sees, not by its numbers. *Other places, named under D852 — the same check applies to each: the Leave War's sheets and bid windows (they place themselves, `leavewar/ui/Sheet.tsx`), the Tracker's event editor, Admin's Duty templates box, the Logic page's boxes, and the Scheduler Board's list ([KEYBOARD-SCROLL]).*

**Lesson 60 (21 Oct 26, D1098 — "Its normal that every sqn will change the rules to fit their rules").** A squadron setting its own rules is the normal way the app is used, so View-only Sched and Edit Schedule do not flag it: the schedule shows the schedule, and how a setting differs from the standard is said where the settings are kept (the Logic page's strip). *(The agent's reading, not his words: the same holds for the app's other everyday pages, and a mark earns a place on an everyday page only where it changes what a person does there.)* *Other places it may apply, named not changed:* the Logic page's wording "changed from the squadron standard" and its per-setting standard values — both on the settings page itself, so they stay; nothing else in the app flags a setting as changed (searched 21 Oct 26).

**Lesson 59 (26 Oct 26, D1387 — "the delete sure? Doesn't look like Cus it exceeds the button").** A button's words stand inside its edge in every state it shows — its resting words and any it changes to when pressed ("Delete — sure?"), at a phone's width with an iPhone's wider letters; a longer state is planned for when the button is drawn. Other places it may apply, suggested and not changed: the day's list's Delete, the drag window's Delete, Rearrange's "⇅ Done".

**Lesson 58 (26 Oct 26, D1385 — "Could u widen it such that the list view edges space is similar to the calendar view edges space?").** On a phone, the same things shown in two places of one page stand the same distance from the screen's edges: a window holding cards does not inset them further than the page's own list does. Other places it may apply, suggested and not changed: the SANS calendar's opened day, the Medical tab's cards, the Leave War's day panel.

**Lesson 57 (26 Oct 26, D1384 — "Should the left side of the inputs also show the colours of the input? And match?" … "List view too").** Where a kind has a colour, every mark of that kind uses it — a bar, a box, a card's small square — so a person reads the kind the same way everywhere; no mark keeps an older two-colour scheme beside it. Other places it may apply, suggested and not changed: the schedule's Unavailable and Personal Inputs rows, the bell's lines, the Leave War's Approvals list.

**Lesson 56 (26 Oct 26, D1382 — "there's a lot of space on the middle and right … max 4 rows … more will go to the middle then to the right column … aligned horizontal and vertically neat").** A short list of names and figures in a window with room beside it fills columns left to right, each only a few rows tall (at most four on the Leave War's day panel, his case), rather than one tall column beside empty space; a name too long for its column wraps and its row grows; every name and figure lines up across the columns and down them. **Lesson 55 (25 Oct 26, D1367 — "The others input ground programme and unavailable should show like amber and red boxes yeah? Looks nicer … As in buttons").** A button that chooses which of the app's coloured sections something goes to wears that section's colour, because it looks nicer. *(His case: the Inputs page's choice for an Other, Ground Programme in the schedule's amber and Unavailable in its red. **Lesson 54 (25 Oct 26, D1348 — "seems like the contrast of the approved or refuse is not that obvious").** A state a control shows — a decision taken, a choice made — stands out plainly from the same controls at rest beside it, at a glance: a lit Approve only a little denser than its neighbours (about 2 to 1, measured) is not plain enough. *(That it must change in kind — a solid fill in its own colour against plain neighbours, never only a stronger shade — that the words may say the state, and that it holds on a phone in daylight, are the agent's readings from the design skill's critique of 25 Oct 26, not his words; his pick from its drawings decides the look.)* *Other places it would apply: the Duration row's picked Whole day / AM / PM (a pale tint today); the leave buttons' cyan ring on the day's own leave; the day's list's "approved" / "refused" beside each entry; the "Approve what I enter" switch — suggested, not changed.*

**Lesson 53 (24 Oct 26, D1328, D1329 — "that's a lot of words like the description of ATT b. **Lesson 51 (24 Oct 26, D1317 — "seems like the grey background and the white words dont have much contrast").** Words on a coloured bar or box read clearly against it, whatever the colour: a mid grey with white words is too faint; where a kind's colour cannot carry light words, the words go dark, or the bar goes darker with its colour on its edge. *(That "clearly" is measured — at least 4.5 to 1 for the words, as the accessibility guides set — is the agent's reading, not his words.)* *Other places it would apply: the Leave War's boxes in their kind colours (set C's slate and purple), the Inputs list's kind pills, the SANS calendar's bars — checked when each is drawn.*

**Lesson 47 (24 Oct 26, D1300 — "perhaps the medical input button can be put on the right so that when i click on that the window that pops up doesnt block the main bulk of the data behind and the user can also immediately see the data they input show. and when a new input they fill in is select, it should also flash for a few seconds with cyan border on that input. just like how when the admin adds a new line in scheduler board").** A button that opens a window to add something stands on the side where that window opens, and the window opens clear of the list it adds to, so what is added is seen arriving, wearing the just-added mark, rather than hidden behind the window that made it. *(On a phone a window stands in the middle, D997; this is a desktop's lesson.
