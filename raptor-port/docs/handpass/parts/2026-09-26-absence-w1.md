## c. Findings

**W1-F1 — on a phone the calendar's month SWIPE does nothing (only the ‹ › arrows page).**
What happens: a finger swiped 150 px sideways across empty day space leaves the month where it is, quick or slow. The
browser takes the sideways finger as its own pan and cancels the pointer (`pointerdown` → one `pointermove` →
`pointercancel`, logged by `w1-06-probe-swipe-tap.mjs`), so the calendar never sees the release its swipe is decided
on. The same gesture with a mouse pages at once. Confirmed on a full phone emulation (`isMobile` + `hasTouch`,
`w1-11-probe-mobile-ctx.mjs`). The grid carries no touch rule of its own (computed `touch-action: auto`).
Steps (fresh world, 390 × 844, a finger): Inputs → Calendar view → ‹ to July 2026 → put a finger on empty space in
8 Jul, sweep 150 px left, lift → still "July 2026".
Rule: the owner's 22 Aug 26 ask ("allow me to swipe left and right… to see different months", `InputsCal.tsx`
SWIPE_MIN); Fable S15 / M11 ("a 50 px sideways drag pages the month"). Pictures: `w1-05-06-swipe-left.png`.
Who it hurts: anyone on a phone — the gesture he asked for is dead there; the arrows still work.

**W1-F2 — on a phone a tap on a chip opens its edit and the same tap closes it again.**
What happens: the calendar opens the edit on the finger's lift; the phone then sends that tap's click, which lands on
the dialog that has just appeared. Where the chip lies outside the dialog's box (the Monday column, the top row, the
lower rows) it hits the dim backdrop and the dialog closes at once — the person sees a flicker and nothing; where the
chip lies under the box it hits the dialog's head (harmless). Survey of every chip in the demo July
(`w1-07-probe-ghostclick.mjs`): 10 of 33 closed at once, 23 stayed open; no input changed. A mouse click opens it
normally. Confirmed on the full phone emulation. **Predicted, not seen:** a chip under one of the dialog's buttons
would press that button — the edit's Delete has no confirm.
Steps (fresh world, 390 × 844, a finger): Calendar view → July 2026 → tap Vapor's chip on Mon 20 Jul (or Cinder's on
Sat 4) → no dialog.
Rule: R3's promise (a tap on a chip opens that input's edit — `caldrag.ts` onTap); Fable S15. Pictures:
`w1-05-09-tap-chip-edit.png`, `w1-11-mobile-ctx-after-tap.png`. Who it hurts: a phone user opening an input from the
month; the day popover's rows are a working way round.

**W1-F3 — a MEMBER's calendar treats another man's chip as his: it lifts, and a tap opens it with Delete and Save.**
What happens: Ranger, with the Inputs filter widened to everyone, can pick up Tally's LL — the ghost follows his
pointer and a day lights — and only on the drop is he told "Only a scheduler can move someone else's input". A tap
on the same chip opens Tally's input with Type, When, Remarks, Delete and Save; Delete says "You can only delete your
own inputs", Save "You can only edit your own inputs" (nothing is changed — the write path holds). The Inputs TABLE,
same filter, shows no control on other men's rows (`w1-09-probe-member-cal.mjs`), so the two doors disagree.
Steps: sign in as us → Inputs → person filter "all" → Calendar view → July → drag Tally's chip on 24 Jul; tap it.
Rule: "a member cannot act on another man's row" (deliberate, register §8 / D166) and the brief's expectation that
another man's chip does not lift. Pictures: `w1-08-other-in-flight.png`, `w1-08-other-tap.png`,
`w1-08-other-delete-refused.png`. Who it hurts: a member, offered controls he may not use; low.

**W1-F4 — the war's tap list prints a noon leave as starting 12:01.**
What happens: LL typed 12:00–14:00 is stored as 12:00 and the Inputs page prints 12:00–14:00, but the Leave War's day
list prints "LL — local leave, 12:01–14:00". The HALF is right (afternoon, N1); the time printed is not what was
filed. Steps: file LL 12:00–14:00 on 24 Aug (Comet), bid a morning beside it, tap the day on the war.
Rule: N1 (noon is the afternoon — it decides the half, it does not move the start); the Inputs page and the war should
say the same thing. Pictures: `w1-10-noon-taplist.png`, `w1-03-n1-inputs-row.png`. Who it hurts: a reader comparing
the two pages; low.
