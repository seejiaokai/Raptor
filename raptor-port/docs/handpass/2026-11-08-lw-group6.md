| Case | Blink frames, before → after | Flip frame at 4×, ms | Median / p95 frame at 4×, ms | Stays pinned, median / p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leave War desktop wheel | 1–2 → 0 | 683 → 600 | 33 / 283 → 17 / 200 | 17 / 150 → 17 / 250 (spread 150–433 → 200–400) |
| Leave War phone finger | 1 → 0 | 217 → 350 | 17 / 33 → 33 / 67, re-run order swapped: 17 / ≤33 both | 17 / 50 → 33 / 50, re-run: no difference |

**Scrolling slower: no.** The blink is gone in every case. The frame of the flip itself is a little longer in three cases
(the pinned copy now mounts in that frame rather than one or two later — the same work, earlier), inside the run-to-run
spread but one phone outlier; everything else within one or two frame steps. The blink count was read from each frame's
log (the frame's animation callback, after its scroll events, before its paint); the host did not open the screencast
pictures one by one — the host reproduced the finding instead with the four browser pins, all red on `c11a5506e` and
green after. A real iPhone's finger scroll is the phone's own (lessons 6, 7, 21): his look on his iPhone is the proof
there.
