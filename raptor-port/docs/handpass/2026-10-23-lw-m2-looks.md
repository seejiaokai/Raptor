## 3. The door check

Every write M2 adds goes through one named command, admin-gated at the store and at the control: `lw.availrow` (the
Available rows' points), `lw.reqname` (the Required rows' names) — `perms.ts` "Setting U", word for word in
`data-model.md` §11 (the code read checked the parity rows); the HO's half rides the existing award commands
(`setDayAward`, the tracker's grant and editor). No new door into the store; a member's controls for these are absent, and
the store refuses the write anyway (the unit tests of each command).

## 4. Sizing the walk (§7.0, D607)

Type of change: a wide look change over every surface that draws a leave cell, plus three new controls and one saved field
(the HO's half). Size: **wide** — member and admin, phone (390) and desktop (1536), the desktop at a real 125% too, the
turned phone, 431–700 widths for the top; 66 scenarios from the independent scenario designer (a fresh Opus, D790), walked
by three Sonnet 5.5 walkers (D787), each in its own world, every step a real tap or click through the app's controls (states
not reachable by a control built through the app's own input door, said in each table). Cost: about 2 h 40 of walking,
270 pictures.

## 5. The speed (performance.md §E — before and after)

Measured on the built bundle, CPU 4×, five runs (`../img/handpass/2026-10-23-lw-m2-looks/perf-m2.txt`): nodes desk admin
17,306 (M1's sheet: 18,292), phone admin 7,918 (8,441) — fewer (the band row and the drawer gone); the page's open and a
cell's tap within M1's spread. One box a cell, no node added.
**His fling (his video, 23 Oct 26 — §14):** `../../scripts/handpass/lw-m2-scroll.mjs`, phone 390, CPU 4×, paired runs
before / after the fix: an eleven-month fling back from December — striped about 0.95 s both (a fast fling outruns any
draw; the longest frame unchanged, 289 ms both); the iPhone's edge bounce replayed — January drawn while it bounces in
both here (the bounce's wait is Safari's; on the iPhone the fix draws January the moment the fling reaches it — his look);
a steady drag back (12 px a frame, at full speed): a stripe stood in view 38 frames running before, ≤ 15 after (the
browser test, §7). An earlier version that drew left months at any speed made the fast fling worse (striped 1.24 s, longest
task 444 ms) and was replaced by the speed gate.
