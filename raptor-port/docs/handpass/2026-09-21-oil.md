## 9. A correction: the reload finding was mis-diagnosed, and the fix would have hidden a real bug

Recorded in full because getting this wrong would have been worse than missing it.

**What was reported (host, §6 fix 3):** a plain browser reload manufactures a pending amendment on
a published day that earns OIL. Press it and an empty amendment goes out. Suppress it.

**What Fable said, reading the code:** it is not a phantom. Across the reload everything is
byte-identical except the family day's ALL AVAIL membership — the demo's posted-out man walks back
in, because his posting lives only in the demo overlay and is never written to the persisted
posting record. So "1 pending" is the day honestly reporting a roster change, the amendment was not
empty, and only the arithmetic kept his cell from moving.

**Settled by one observation** (`scripts/handpass/settle-d4.mjs`, host-run):

| | members of the family day | the day says |
|---|---|---|
| published, before the reload | **27** | ORIG · no pending change |
| after a plain reload | **28 — Torch walked back in** | ORIG · **1 pending · Publish AL1** |

Nobody dropped out. One man rejoined. **Fable is right and the host's reading was wrong.**

**Two consequences, and the second is the serious one:**

1. **Do not suppress the pending change.** It is the day telling the truth. Suppressing it would
   silence every genuine post-out made after a day is published — a man leaves the squadron, the
   schedule never says the day changed, and nobody re-signs it.
2. **The real defect is not about OIL at all.** A posted-out man's posting is not persisted, so a
   reload quietly puts him back in the squadron's availability. That reaches the crew picker,
   ALL AVAIL, the manning counts and every availability read in the app — not just this feature.
   It should be raised on its own, not carried inside the OIL fixes.

**What this cost the method, per §11 of the standing order** — an escaped mis-diagnosis is a method
failure before it is a bug. The walk saw a symptom on one surface and named a cause without
checking what else had changed underneath. The lesson, written where it belongs: **before calling
a pending change phantom, diff what the day is actually made of, not just what it says.** A day
reporting a change it will not explain is a prompt to look harder, not evidence of a bug in the
reporting.
