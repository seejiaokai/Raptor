## What the walk found, and each disposition

1. **FIXED — a finger's touch mid-glide still opened the event under it** (D986 broken). Seen red first (G4: the
   pop-up opened). Cause: the guard that takes the press's click was dropped straight after the lift, but a finger's click
   comes a moment after its lift. Now it is kept up to 600ms past the lift and dropped at once by the next press. Re-walked:
   G4, G4m, G5, I3, I4 green.
2. **FIXED — a date box the stopping finger landed on was left wearing its focus outline** (nothing opened, but the box
   looked picked). The stopping press now takes no focus. I3 checks it (`tookFocus: false`).
3. **Not faults — the test browser and the script, each shown before it was called one:**
   - after a scripted flick Chromium sends NO click for the next finger tap anywhere on the page — counted at the window,
     before the app sees anything (a probe, 17 Oct 26; `docs/walk-scripts.md` §4 already names the trap). The walk spends
     one throwaway tap first, and presses a tab or the zoom through its own handler straight after a flick;
   - several presses aimed at an event landed beside it or off the screen's edge while the chart moved; the walk now reads
     what each press landed on as it lands, retries up to four times and counts only presses that hit an event while the
     chart was still moving (a frame-by-frame log showed every such press stopping a running glide);
   - a press point under the foot of the screen landed on the zoom bar's "+"; the walk now proves the button is under the
     point.

## For his look on his iPhone — what only a real device can prove

- The feel: how far a flick throws and how it slows (the iPhone's own rate), and the size of the bounce at an end.
- On the Info tab: Safari scrolls it the Tracker's way too (the Info half is set to take no browser scrolling, so both
  halves feel the same); the Failures card's own list still scrolls by itself.
- At the bottom of the Info half and the right end of the chart: the stretch is drawn by moving the content; Chromium does
  not count that toward the scroll range — if Safari does, the bounce at those ends would not show (the chart's top and bottom
  are safe: its own spacer holds the range).
- A finger that moves a few pixels on a chip and lifts opens nothing (this test browser sends no click for it, so only a
  phone can show it).
- A tap on an event while the chart glides opens nothing, and the next tap opens it (Safari's click comes after the lift;
  the guard waits 600ms for it).
